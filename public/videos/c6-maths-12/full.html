Understanding Ratio: Definition, Notation, and Mathematical Representation

• Ratio: A comparison of two quantities of the same kind by division.
• Notation: Written as 'a : b' and read as 'a is to b'.
• Order Matters: The first number is the antecedent, the second is the consequent.
• Fraction Form: The ratio 'a : b' can be expressed as the fraction 'a / b'.
• Unit Consistency: Quantities must be in the same units before comparing.

Expressing Ratios in Simplest Form and Unit Consistency Rules

• Simplify ratios by dividing by the Highest Common Factor (HCF)
• A ratio is in simplest form when the only common factor is 1
• Rule: Quantities must be in the same unit before comparing
• Always convert larger units to smaller units to avoid decimals

Deep Dive into Proportions: Definition, Equality of Ratios, and Properties

• Proportion: A mathematical statement where two ratios are equal.
• Terms: Extremes are the first and last terms; Means are the middle terms.
• Property 1: Product of Extremes = Product of Means.
• Property 2: Ratio of corresponding terms in proportional sets is equal.
• Continued Proportion: A special case where a/b = b/c.

The Rule of Cross-Multiplication: Derivation and Application

• Proportion: Two equal ratios, a/b = c/d
• Rule: Product of extremes = Product of means (ad = bc)
• Derivation: Using the property of equality to isolate variables
• Application: Set up ratios, cross-multiply, and solve for the unknown
• Crucial Tip: Maintain consistent order of terms in both ratios

Practical Activities: Using Ratio in Recipes and Mixing Colors

• Ratios ensure consistency in taste and color when scaling quantities.
• Scaling a recipe means multiplying all ingredients by the same number.
• A lemonade ratio of 2:3 stays the same even if quantities increase.
• Color mixing relies on fixed ratios to create specific shades.
• To maintain a proportion, always multiply or divide all parts by the same factor.

Solving Word Problems: Part-to-Part vs. Part-to-Whole Relationships

• Part-to-Part: Compares one specific group to another specific group.
• Part-to-Whole: Compares one specific group to the entire total.
• Always calculate the 'Total' first when dealing with part-to-whole problems.
• Keywords like 'Total' or 'All' indicate a part-to-whole relationship.
• Always simplify your final ratio to its lowest terms.

Advanced Word Problems: Scaling, Maps, and Model Building

• Scale is a ratio comparing a model/map to the actual object.

Common Pitfalls: Unit Mismatches, Order Errors, and Calculation Mistakes

• Always convert quantities to the same unit before finding a ratio.
• The order of numbers must match the order of terms in the question.
• Antecedent is the first term; Consequent is the second term.
• Simplify ratios by dividing both terms by their Greatest Common Divisor.
• Double-check your division and zero-handling to avoid calculation errors.

Mathematical Reasoning: Identifying Proportional vs. Non-Proportional Relationships

• Proportional relationship: The ratio between two quantities remains constant.
• Constant of Proportionality: The fixed value that the ratio always equals.
• Non-proportional: When the ratio changes even if both quantities increase.
• The 'Zero Test': Proportional relationships must result in zero for both values.
• Graphical Tip: Proportional lines must be straight and pass through the origin.
