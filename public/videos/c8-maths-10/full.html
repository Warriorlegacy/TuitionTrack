Defining Exponents: Base, Exponent, and Positive Integer Powers

• Base: The number that is being multiplied by itself.
• Exponent: The number of times the base is used as a factor.
• Notation: a raised to the power of m is written as a^m.
• Common Error: a^m is NOT the same as a multiplied by m.
• Example: 5^4 = 5 x 5 x 5 x 5 = 625.

Laws of Exponents Part 1: Product and Quotient Rules

• Product Rule: a^m * a^n = a^(m + n)
• Quotient Rule: a^m / a^n = a^(m - n)
• Crucial Condition: Bases must be identical to apply these laws
• When multiplying: Keep base, ADD exponents
• When dividing: Keep base, SUBTRACT exponents

Part 4 of 12 · ~5 min

Laws of Exponents Part 2: Power of a Power and Power of a Product

• Power of a Power Rule: (a^m)^n = a^(m * n)
• Power of a Product Rule: (a * b)^m = a^m * b^m
• Example: (2^3)^2 = 2^(3*2) = 2^6 = 64
• Example: (2 * 5)^3 = 2^3 * 5^3 = 8 * 125 = 1000
• Note: These laws apply to products, not sums or differences

Negative Exponents: Understanding Reciprocals and Rules

• Negative exponents do not make the base negative.
• A negative exponent indicates the reciprocal of the base.
• Rule: a raised to the power of negative m equals 1 divided by a raised to the m.
• To solve, flip the base and change the exponent to positive.
• Example: 5 raised to the power of negative 3 is 1 over 125.

Part 6 of 12 · ~4 min

Zero Exponent Rule and Mastery of Combined Laws

• Zero Exponent Rule: Any non-zero base raised to power zero equals one.
• Product Law: Add exponents when multiplying same bases.
• Quotient Law: Subtract exponents when dividing same bases.
• Power of a Power: Multiply exponents when raising a power to a power.
• Mastery Strategy: Simplify numerator and denominator separately first.

Standard Form: Expressing Very Large Numbers

• Standard Form: A number between 1 and 10 multiplied by a power of 10.
• Avoids errors caused by writing long strings of zeros in usual form.
• Step 1: Place the decimal point after the first non-zero digit.
• Step 2: Count the decimal shifts to determine the positive exponent.
• Example: 590,000,000 becomes 5.9 x 10^8

Standard Form: Expressing Very Small Numbers (Decimal Notation)

• Standard form for small numbers: a number between 1 and 10 multiplied by 10 to a negative power.
• Moving the decimal point to the right results in a negative exponent.
• The exponent's value equals the number of decimal places shifted.
• Negative exponents represent division by powers of ten.
• Example: 0.0008 = 8 x 10 to the power of negative 4.

Part 9 of 12 · ~5 min

Converting Between Usual Form and Standard Form

• Standard to Usual: Positive exponent moves decimal right; Negative moves it left.
• Usual to Standard (Large): Move decimal left; use a positive exponent.
• Usual to Standard (Small): Move decimal right; use a negative exponent.
• The goal of Standard Form is to keep the coefficient between 1 and 10.
• Always count the number of jumps to determine the exponent value.

Common Pitfalls: Avoiding Mistakes in Signs and Operations

• Distinguish between (-x)^n and -x^n
• Never add exponents when adding bases
• Negative exponents indicate reciprocals, not negative values
• Apply exponents to every factor inside a bracket
• Any non-zero base raised to the power of zero is one
