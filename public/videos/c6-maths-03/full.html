Factors and Multiples: Definitions, Properties, and Identification

• Factor: A number that divides another number exactly with zero remainder.
• Multiples: The products obtained by multiplying a number by whole numbers.
• Properties of Factors: One is a factor of all; factors are finite and <= the number.
• Properties of Multiples: Every number is a multiple of itself; multiples are infinite.
• Key Difference: Factors break numbers down; multiples build numbers up.

Prime and Composite Numbers: The Building Blocks of Mathematics

• Prime Numbers: Exactly two factors (1 and itself)
• Composite Numbers: More than two factors
• The number 2 is the only even prime number
• The number 1 is neither prime nor composite
• Prime numbers are the building blocks of all numbers

Divisibility Rules: Part 1 (2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10) and Shortcuts

• Divisibility by 2, 5, 10: Check the last digit (even, 0/5, or 0).
• Divisibility by 3 and 9: Sum of the digits must be divisible by 3 or 9.
• Divisibility by 4: The last two digits must be divisible by 4.
• Divisibility by 8: The last three digits must be divisible by 8.
• Divisibility by 6: Must be divisible by both 2 and 3.

Part 5 of 12 · ~5 min

Divisibility Rules: Part 2 (7, 11) and Advanced Testing Techniques

• Rule for 7: Double the last digit and subtract from the rest.
• Example: 231 -> 23 - (1 x 2) = 21. Divisible by 7!
• Rule for 11: Difference between sum of odd and even position digits.
• Example: 1331 -> (1+3) - (3+1) = 0. Divisible by 11!
• Advanced Tip: Use alternating sums for very large numbers.

Common Factors and Common Multiples: Finding the Shared Connections

• Common Factor: A number that is a factor of two or more numbers.
• Example: Common factors of 12 and 18 are 1, 2, 3, and 6.
• Common Multiple: A number that is a multiple of two or more numbers.
• Example: Common multiples of 4 and 6 are 12, 24, 36, etc.
• Factors are finite, while multiples are infinite.

Prime Factorization: Factor Trees and Division Methods

• Prime Factorization: Breaking composite numbers into prime building blocks.
• Factor Tree Method: A visual branching technique using multiplication pairs.
• Division Method: A systematic vertical approach using successive prime division.
• The goal is to continue the process until only prime numbers remain.
• Prime Factorization is written as a product of prime numbers.

Part 8 of 12 · ~5 min

Highest Common Factor (HCF): Concepts, Methods, and Worked Examples

• HCF is the largest number that divides two or more numbers exactly.
• Listing Method: List all factors and pick the largest common one.
• Prime Factorization Method: Multiply the common prime factors.
• HCF is also known as the Greatest Common Divisor (GCD).
• The HCF is always less than or equal to the smallest given number.

Part 9 of 12 · ~5 min

Lowest Common Multiple (LCM): Concepts, Methods, and Worked Examples

• LCM is the smallest non-zero number that is a multiple of two or more numbers.
• Method 1: Prime Factorization - Use the highest power of every prime factor.
• Method 2: Common Division - Divide numbers simultaneously by prime divisors.
• Example: LCM of 4 and 6 is 12.
• Example: LCM of 12, 15, and 20 is 60.

Relationship between HCF and LCM: Formula Derivation and Application

• HCF and LCM are mathematically connected.
• Formula: Product of two numbers = HCF × LCM
• Example: 12 × 18 = 216 and 6 × 36 = 216
• Use the formula to find a missing number quickly.
• Note: This specific rule applies to two numbers only.

Part 11 of 12 · ~5 min

Word Problems: Mastering HCF and LCM in Real-Life Scenarios

• HCF is used for splitting, dividing, or finding the largest possible size.
• LCM is used for repeating events, cycles, or finding the smallest common time.
• HCF Keywords: Greatest, Largest, Maximum, Equal groups.
• LCM Keywords: Smallest, Minimum, Least, When will they meet again?
• Tip: Always identify if you are 'breaking down' (HCF) or 'building up' (LCM).

• Common Error: Including '1' as a prime factor in factorization.
