Defining Political Parties: Meaning, Components, and Functions

• Political Party: A group contesting elections to hold power
• Three Components: Leaders, Active Members, and Followers
• Function 1: Contesting elections and putting forward policies
• Function 2: Making laws and forming/running the government
• Function 3: Role of Opposition and shaping public opinion

Party Systems I: One-Party vs. Two-Party Systems

• Party System: The way parties are organized and compete for power.
• One-Party System: Only one party controls the government (e.g., China).
• One-Party System is non-democratic as it lacks real competition.
• Two-Party System: Two major parties dominate the electoral process.
• Two-Party System (e.g., USA, UK) offers stability but limited choice.

Party Systems II: Multi-Party Systems and Alliances

• Multi-Party System: More than two parties compete for power.
• Advantage: Represents a wider range of social and ideological interests.
• Disadvantage: Can lead to political instability and fragmented power.
• Coalition Government: Multiple parties joining hands to form a government.
• Alliance Dynamics: Balancing diverse interests vs. risk of government collapse.

Part 6 of 12 · ~3 min

National vs. Regional Parties: Criteria and Classification

• National Party: 6% votes in Lok Sabha/Assemblies + 4 Lok Sabha seats
• National Party: 2% of total Lok Sabha seats from different states
• State Party: 6% votes in State Assembly + 2 Assembly seats
• National parties influence the whole country; State parties focus on regions
• The coexistence of both leads to coalition politics and alliances

Challenges to Political Parties I: Lack of Internal Democracy

• Political parties face several structural challenges in a democracy.
• Power often concentrates in the hands of a few top leaders.
• Ordinary party members frequently lack a voice in decision-making.
• Many parties fail to hold regular and transparent internal elections.
• Lack of internal democracy prevents new, merit-based leaders from emerging.

Part 9 of 12 · ~3 min

Challenges to Political Parties II: Dynastic Succession and Money Power

• Dynastic Succession: Leadership passing through family lines instead of merit.
• Impact of Dynasty: Talented grassroots workers are denied leadership roles.
• Money Power: High election costs favor wealthy candidates over ordinary citizens.
• The Cycle of Corruption: Wealthy candidates may prioritize donors over public interest.
• Criminalization: The dangerous link between money, muscle power, and politics.

A split-screen 3D animation: one side shows a family tree turning into a political ladder, the other shows a scale tipping heavily toward a bag of money over a ballot box.

Part 10 of 12 · ~4 min

Challenges to Political Parties III: Lack of Meaningful Choice

• The core issue: Voters often face a lack of distinct ideological alternatives.
• Convergence of policies: Parties often adopt similar economic and social stances.
• Personality vs. Ideology: Competition shifts from ideas to symbols and leaders.
• Consequences: Voter apathy and disillusionment with the democratic process.
• The need for substantive differences to ensure a healthy democracy.

How Parties can be Reformed: Legal and Institutional Measures

• Mandatory organizational elections and filing of income tax returns
• Election Commission monitors candidate expenditure during polls
• No legal limit currently exists on total party election spending
• Mandatory candidate affidavits disclosing assets and criminal cases
• Supreme Court interventions to curb the criminalization of politics
