Poetic Devices II: Similes, Metaphors, and Onomatopoeia

• Simile: Comparing two things using 'like' or 'as' (e.g., mouth like a fireplace).
• Metaphor: A direct comparison stating one thing is another to show deeper meaning.
• Onomatopoeia: Words that imitate natural sounds to create a sensory experience.
• Similes help visualize Custard's physical intensity and fearsome appearance.
• Onomatopoeia adds a cinematic, auditory layer to the poem's dramatic climax.

Common Mistakes: Misinterpreting Tone and Character Motivation

• Avoid treating the poem as a serious epic; it is a 'mock-heroic' parody.
• Distinguish between 'cowardice' and 'performative bravery' in the pets.
• Custard's bravery is an instinctual reaction, not a personality change.
• The ending shows a lack of moral growth, highlighting human hypocrisy.
• The pirate is a caricature used to drive the plot, not a complex villain.
