Concept of Factors and Common Factors: Definitions and Identification

• A factor divides an expression completely with zero remainder.
• Multiplying factors together results in the original expression.
• Algebraic factors include both numbers and variables.
• Common factors are terms shared by two or more expressions.
• The Greatest Common Factor (GCF) is the largest shared term.

Factorisation by Regrouping Terms: Techniques and Common Pitfalls

• Use regrouping when no single factor is common to all terms.
• Group terms into pairs to identify local common factors.
• Ensure the resulting binomial in the brackets is identical.
• Watch out for the Sign Trap: factor out negative one if needed.
• Always verify your answer by multiplying the factors back.

Division of Algebraic Expressions: Monomial by Monomial and Polynomial by Monomial

• Monomial division: Divide coefficients and subtract exponents of variables.
• Law of Exponents: x to the power of m divided by x to the power of n is x to the power of m minus n.
• Polynomial division: Use the distributive property to divide each term separately.
• Step-by-step: Split the polynomial into individual fractions before dividing.
• Verification: Multiply the quotient by the divisor to check your answer.

Division of Algebraic Expressions: Polynomial by Polynomial and Long Division Method

• Arrange terms in descending order of their degrees first.
• Divide the first term of the dividend by the first term of the divisor.
• Multiply the entire divisor by the new quotient term.
• Subtract carefully by changing the signs of the terms.
• Final Answer = Quotient + (Remainder / Divisor)

Introduction to Algebraic Identities: Deriving (a+b)² and (a-b)²

• Identity 1: (a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b²
• Identity 2: (a - b)² = a² - 2ab + b²
• Derivation uses the distributive law (FOIL method)
• The middle term sign depends on the operation in the bracket
• Identities are equations that are true for all values of variables

Part 8 of 12 · ~4 min

Mastering Identities: Deriving (a² - b²) and (x+a)(x+b)

• Deriving the Difference of Two Squares: a squared minus b squared equals (a plus b)(a minus b)
• Observe how middle terms cancel out in (a plus b)(a minus b)
• Deriving the Product of Binomials: (x plus a)(x plus b) equals x squared plus (a plus b)x plus ab
• The middle term is the sum of constants; the last term is the product of constants
• Mastering these patterns makes factorising quadratic expressions much faster
