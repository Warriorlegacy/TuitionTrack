Barter System: Limitations, Double Coincidence of Wants, and the Need for Money

• Barter System: Direct exchange of goods without the use of money.
• Double Coincidence of Wants: Both parties must want exactly what the other offers.
• Divisibility Problem: Difficulty in dividing certain goods for smaller trades.
• Store of Value: Perishable goods cannot be easily saved for future use.
• Need for Money: Acts as a medium of exchange to solve barter limitations.

Money as a Medium of Exchange: Modern Forms and Functions

• Money acts as an intermediary, eliminating the need for double coincidence of wants.
• Medium of Exchange: An item used to facilitate the buying and selling of goods.
• Modern money includes paper notes and coins authorized by the government.
• In India, the Reserve Bank of India issues currency on behalf of the central government.
• Modern forms include digital payments, debit/credit cards, and cheques.

Understanding Credit: The Role of Interest, Collateral, and Terms of Credit

• Credit: An agreement for future payment of money, goods, or services.
• Positive Role: Helps increase earnings and business expansion.
• Debt Trap: A situation where credit pushes a borrower into a cycle of debt.
• Terms of Credit: Includes interest rate, collateral, documentation, and repayment.
• Collateral: An asset owned by the borrower used as a guarantee to the lender.

Formal Sector Credit: Functions of Banks and the Role of the RBI

• Formal sector includes banks and cooperatives following strict regulations.
• Banks use deposits to provide loans while keeping a cash reserve.
• Bank income comes from the difference between deposit and loan interest.
• The Reserve Bank of India (R.B.I.) supervises all formal lending.
• R.B.I. ensures credit reaches small farmers and small-scale industries.

Informal Sector Credit: Moneylenders, Traders, and the Debt Trap Cycle

• Informal sector includes moneylenders, traders, employers, and relatives.
• No organization supervises the functioning of informal lenders.
• Lack of regulation can lead to unfair lending practices.
• High interest rates in the informal sector increase the cost of borrowing.
• The Debt Trap: When credit pushes a borrower into a cycle of worsening debt.

Part 7 of 12 · ~5 min

Comparative Analysis: Formal vs. Informal Credit and Socio-economic Impact

• Formal Sector: Regulated by RBI; Informal Sector: Unregulated and autonomous.
• Interest Rates: Formal is low and fixed; Informal is high and exploitative.
• Collateral: Essential for banks; Often bypassed by informal lenders.
• Economic Impact: Formal sector drives growth; Informal sector can cause debt traps.
• The Debt Trap: A cycle where high interest prevents principal repayment.

Self-Help Groups (SHGs): Structure, Functioning, and Empowerment of the Poor

• SHGs consist of 15-20 members from similar socio-economic backgrounds.
• Members make regular small savings to create a common fund.
• Small loans are provided to members without the need for collateral.
• Groups can access bank loans once they demonstrate regular repayment.
• SHGs empower women socially and provide a platform for collective discussion.

Common Mistakes: Misinterpreting Collateral, Interest Rates, and Credit Terms

• Collateral: An asset owned by the borrower used as a guarantee for the lender.
• Collateral vs. Creditworthiness: Assets owned vs. ability to repay.
• Terms of Credit: Includes interest rate, collateral, documentation, and repayment mode.
• Interest Rates: Always specify if the rate is 'per annum' or 'per month'.
• Debt Trap: A cycle where new loans are taken to pay off old ones due to low income.
