Vocabulary & Literary Devices: Alliteration, Simile, and Imagery

• Alliteration: Repetition of consonant sounds (e.g., 'September sunlight').
• Alliteration's effect: Creates rhythm and emphasizes specific moods.
• Simile: Comparison using 'like' or 'as' (e.g., 'sleek as a lizard').
• Simile's effect: Illustrates the bird's sudden, sharp, and quick movements.
• Imagery: Using sensory language to create mental pictures (Visual and Auditory).

Common Mistakes: Misinterpreting Tone and Contextual Errors

• Avoid describing the tree as 'dead'; use 'silent' or 'still' instead.
• Use 'kinetic' or 'frenetic' rather than just 'happy' for the bird's arrival.
• The 'engine' metaphor represents biological life-force, not literal machinery.
• The ending is 'cyclical' and 'transient,' not merely 'sad' or 'lonely.'
• Distinguish between imagery (visuals) and metaphor (comparisons) in answers.
