Introduction: The Significance of Genetics and Mendel's Legacy

• Genetics: The study of heredity and variation.
• Heredity: Passing of traits from parents to offspring.
• Variation: Differences between parents and progeny.
• Gregor Mendel: The Father of Genetics and pea plant researcher.
• Pisum sativum: The model organism used for Mendelian experiments.

Part 2 of 12 · ~4 min

Mendel's Laws: Monohybrid Cross, Dominance, and Segregation

• Monohybrid Cross: A cross involving a single pair of contrasting characters.
• Law of Dominance: The dominant allele masks the expression of the recessive allele.
• F-one Generation: All offspring show the dominant phenotype (Tt).
• F-two Generation: Phenotypic ratio of three to one (3:1) and Genotypic ratio of one to two to one (1:2:1).
• Law of Segregation: Alleles separate during gamete formation so each gamete carries only one allele.

A 3D split-screen showing a tall pea plant and a dwarf plant, transitioning into a Punnett Square grid where colorful T and t alleles move into cells to form the F-two generation.

Part 3 of 12 · ~3 min

Dihybrid Cross: Law of Independent Assortment and Punnett Squares

• Dihybrid cross tracks the inheritance of two different traits simultaneously
• F-one generation results in all Round and Yellow phenotypes (RrYy)
• Punnett Square helps predict the sixteen combinations in the F-two generation
• The characteristic F-two phenotypic ratio is nine-three-three-one
• Law of Independent Assortment: Traits segregate independently during gamete formation

A 3D animation showing two pea plants producing colorful gametes that fly into a glowing 4x4 Punnett Square grid, populating it with different colored pea seeds.

Part 4 of 12 · ~4 min

Post-Mendelian Genetics: Incomplete Dominance and Co-dominance

• Mendel's Law of Dominance is not always applicable in all species.
• Incomplete Dominance: Heterozygote shows an intermediate phenotype (e.g., Pink Snapdragons).
• Incomplete Dominance F-two ratio: 1 Red : 2 Pink : 1 White (1:2:1).
• Co-dominance: Both alleles express themselves equally (e.g., ABO Blood Groups).
• ABO Blood Grouping: I-A and I-B alleles are co-dominant.

A split-screen 3D animation showing red and white snapdragons producing pink offspring on one side, and a human red blood cell displaying both A and B antigens on the other.

Part 5 of 12 · ~5 min

Multiple Alleles, Pleiotropy, and Polygenic Inheritance

• Multiple alleles: more than two allele forms exist for one gene in a population
• ABO blood group has three alleles, six genotypes, and four phenotypes
• Pleiotropy: one gene influences many unrelated phenotypic traits
• Polygenic inheritance: many genes jointly determine a single character
• Continuous variation in polygenic traits forms a bell curve distribution

3D animation zooming from a chromosome showing three ABO alleles, branching into a blood group chart, then splitting into a sickle cell, a melanin pathway, and a bell curve representing polygenic height.

Part 6 of 12 · ~3 min

Chromosomal Theory of Inheritance and Linkage-Recombination

• Sutton and Boveri proposed that genes are located on chromosomes.
• Thomas Hunt Morgan proved the theory using Drosophila melanogaster.
• Linkage: Physical association of genes on the same chromosome.
• Recombination: Exchange of genetic material during crossing over.
• Recombination frequency indicates the distance between genes.

A 3D animation showing homologous chromosomes pairing up and exchanging segments during Prophase I, transitioning into a map showing gene distance based on recombination frequency.

Part 7 of 12 · ~4 min

Sex Determination in Humans, Insects, Birds, and Plants

• Humans: XX females and XY males (XY type mechanism)
• Drosophila: XX females and XO males (XO type mechanism)
• Birds: ZZ males and ZW females (ZW type mechanism)
• In birds, the female's genotype determines the offspring's sex
• Plants: Sex determined by genes or environmental factors

A split-screen 3D animation showing a human XY cross, a Drosophila XO cross, and a bird ZW cross side-by-side.

Part 8 of 12 · ~3 min

Mutation: Types, Chromosomal Aberrations, and Genetic Disorders

• Mutation: Sudden, stable, and heritable changes in DNA sequence
• Point Mutation: Single nucleotide change (e.g., Sickle-cell anemia)
• Frameshift Mutation: Insertion or deletion shifting the reading frame
• Chromosomal Aberrations: Deletion, Duplication, Inversion, and Translocation
• Aneuploidy: Change in chromosome number due to non-disjunction

A 3D DNA double helix undergoing a base substitution, followed by a zoom-out to show a whole chromosome breaking and reattaching incorrectly.

Part 9 of 12 · ~4 min

Mendelian Disorders I: Haemophilia, Sickle-cell Anemia, and Phenylketonuria

• Mendelian disorders are caused by single-gene mutations.
• Haemophilia: Sex-linked recessive disorder affecting blood clotting.
• Sickle-cell Anemia: Autosomal recessive; Glutamic acid replaced by Valine.
• Sickle-cell mechanism: HbS polymerization causes distorted RBC shape.
• Phenylketonuria (PKU): Autosomal recessive; deficiency of phenylalanine hydroxylase.

A 3D split-screen showing a normal biconcave red blood cell transitioning into a rigid sickle shape, alongside a molecular model of the hemoglobin protein chain.

Part 10 of 12 · ~3 min

Mendelian Disorders II: Thalassemia, Colour Blindness, and Cystic Fibrosis

• Thalassemia: Quantitative defect in globin chain synthesis (Alpha and Beta types)
• Thalassemia is an autosomal recessive disorder affecting hemoglobin levels
• Colour Blindness: X-linked recessive disorder affecting red-green perception
• Males are more prone to Colour Blindness due to having a single X chromosome
• Cystic Fibrosis: Autosomal recessive disorder causing thick, sticky mucus

A split-screen 3D medical animation showing defective hemoglobin molecules, an X-chromosome inheritance pattern, and thick mucus obstructing a lung airway.

Part 11 of 12 · ~3 min

Chromosomal Disorders: Down, Klinefelter, and Turner Syndromes

• Aneuploidy: Gain or loss of chromosomes due to non-disjunction
• Down Syndrome: Trisomy of chromosome 21 (Total 47 chromosomes)
• Klinefelter Syndrome: Presence of extra X chromosome in males (XXY)
• Turner Syndrome: Absence of one X chromosome in females (45, X)
• Clinical features include physical traits and infertility

A 3D comparison of three human karyotypes: one with three copies of chromosome 21, one with XXY, and one with a single X.
