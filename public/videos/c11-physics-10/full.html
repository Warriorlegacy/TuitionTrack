Introduction: Why Thermal Physics Matters, Temperature vs Heat, and Thermometry Fundamentals

• Thermal Physics: Study of heat, temperature, and energy transfer
• Temperature: Measure of average kinetic energy of molecules
• Heat: Total energy transferred due to temperature difference
• Thermometric Property: A physical property that changes with temperature
• Kelvin Scale: The absolute SI scale where zero is absolute zero

Thermal Expansion: Linear, Areal, and Volumetric Expansion with Derivations and Real-world

• Thermal expansion is caused by increased average particle separation.
• Linear Expansion: delta L = L zero * alpha * delta T
• Areal Expansion: delta A = A zero * beta * delta T (where beta = 2 * alpha)
• Volumetric Expansion: delta V = V zero * gamma * delta T (where gamma = 3 * alpha)
• Real-world: Expansion joints in railway tracks and bridges prevent structural damage.

Anomalous Expansion of Water and Thermal Stress in Solids

• Water reaches maximum density at 4 degrees Celsius
• Anomalous expansion allows aquatic life to survive in winter
• Thermal stress occurs when expansion is restricted
• Formula: Thermal Stress = Young's Modulus * alpha * delta T
• Thermal stress can cause structural failure in solids

Calorimetry: Specific Heat Capacity, Latent Heat, and the Principle of Calorimetry

• Specific Heat Capacity (c): Q = m * c * delta T
• Latent Heat (L): Q = m * L (Temperature remains constant)
• Latent Heat of Fusion vs. Latent Heat of Vaporization
• Principle of Calorimetry: Heat Lost = Heat Gained
• Thermal Equilibrium: Reaching a common final temperature

• Phase change occurs at constant temperature
• Latent Heat of Fusion: Solid to Liquid transition
• Latent Heat of Vaporization: Liquid to Gas transition

Heat Transfer I: Conduction, Thermal Conductivity, and Fourier’s Law of Heat Flow

• Conduction: Heat transfer through direct particle-to-particle contact in solids.
• Mechanism: Atomic vibrations and free electron movement in metals.
• Fourier's Law: Heat flow rate is proportional to area and temperature gradient.
• Formula: Q/t = k * A * (T1 - T2) / L
• Thermal Conductivity (k): Measure of a material's ability to conduct heat.

Heat Transfer II: Convection, Natural vs Forced Convection, and Fluid Dynamics

• Convection: Heat transfer via bulk movement of fluid particles
• Natural Convection: Driven by buoyancy and density gradients
• Forced Convection: Driven by external means like fans or pumps
• Heat Transfer Rate: Depends on the convective heat transfer coefficient 'h'
• Turbulent flow enhances heat transfer compared to laminar flow

Heat Transfer III: Radiation, Stefan-Boltzmann Law, and Wien’s Displacement Law

• Radiation requires no medium and travels at the speed of light.
• Stefan-Boltzmann Law: E = epsilon * sigma * T^4.
• Emissivity (epsilon) measures how effectively a surface emits radiation.
• Wien’s Displacement Law: lambda_max * T = constant.
• Increasing temperature shifts peak radiation to shorter wavelengths.

Blackbody Radiation: Kirchhoff’s Law, Emissivity, and Absorptivity Concepts

• Absorptivity (alpha): Fraction of incident radiation absorbed by a surface.
• Emissivity (epsilon): Ratio of radiation emitted by a surface to a blackbody.
• Blackbody: An ideal surface where absorptivity and emissivity both equal one.
• Kirchhoff’s Law: For any body in equilibrium, emissivity equals absorptivity.
• Relationship: Good absorbers are naturally good emitters of radiation.

A split-screen 3D animation showing a dark matte sphere absorbing 100% of light rays while a shiny silver sphere reflecting them, transitioning to a graph showing the equality of alpha and epsilon.

Newton’s Law of Cooling: Mathematical Derivation, Graphical Analysis, and Word Problems

• Newton's Law: Rate of cooling is proportional to temperature difference
• Mathematical Form: d-T/dt = -k(T - T-s)
• Exponential Decay: Temperature decreases exponentially over time
• Graphical View: T vs t is an exponential curve; Rate vs Delta-T is a straight line
• Limitation: Most accurate when temperature difference is small

Common Pitfalls: Conceptual Errors in Thermodynamics and Mathematical Calculation Mistakes

• Heat is energy transfer (Joules); Temperature is kinetic state (Kelvin).
• During phase changes, temperature is constant; use Latent Heat, not Specific Heat.
• Maintain sign consistency in the Principle of Calorimetry (Q lost = Q gained).
• Always convert Celsius to Kelvin when using Stefan-Boltzmann Law.
• Distinguish between Thermal Conductivity (flow) and Diffusivity (speed of change).
