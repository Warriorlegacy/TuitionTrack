Reflection of Light: Laws, Incident/Reflected Rays, and Plane Mirror Properties

• Reflection: The bouncing back of light from a polished surface
• Incident Ray, Reflected Ray, and the Normal line
• First Law: Angle of Incidence equals Angle of Reflection
• Second Law: Incident ray, reflected ray, and normal lie in one plane
• Plane Mirror: Virtual, erect, same size, and lateral inversion

Spherical Mirrors: Understanding Concave and Convex Geometry and Terminology

• Spherical mirrors are parts of a hollow sphere: Concave (inward) and Convex (outward)
• Center of Curvature (C): The center of the original sphere
• Pole (P): The geometric center of the mirror's surface
• Principal Axis: The straight line passing through C and P
• Principal Focus (F): The point where parallel rays converge or appear to diverge

Part 4 of 12 · ~4 min

Image Formation by Concave Mirrors: Ray Diagrams and Case Studies

• Two key rays: Parallel to axis passes through F; through F becomes parallel.
• Object at Infinity: Image at F, real, inverted, and highly diminished.
• Object at C: Image at C, real, inverted, and same size as object.
• Object between F and P: Image is virtual, erect, and magnified behind mirror.
• Real images are inverted; Virtual images are always erect.

Image Formation by Convex Mirrors: Characteristics and Applications

• Convex mirrors are diverging mirrors that spread light rays outward.
• Images formed are always virtual, erect, and diminished in size.
• The principal focus and center of curvature lie behind the mirror.
• Wide field of view allows seeing a much larger area than plane mirrors.
• Common applications: Vehicle side-view mirrors and security surveillance.

A 3D animation showing parallel light rays hitting a bulging mirror, diverging outward, and dashed lines tracing them back to a single focal point behind the mirror, followed by a split-screen comparison of a car's view using a plane mirror versus a convex mirror.

Mirror Formula and Magnification: Mathematical Derivations and Sign Conventions

• New Cartesian Sign Convention: Pole is the origin
• Distances in direction of light are positive; against light are negative
• Mirror Formula: one over v plus u equals one over f
• Magnification m equals h-prime divided by h or negative v divided by u
• Positive m means virtual/erect; Negative m means real/inverted

Part 7 of 12 · ~4 min

Refraction of Light: The Bending Phenomenon and Refractive Index

• Refraction is the bending of light when passing between different media
• Bending occurs because the speed of light changes in different materials
• Light bends towards the normal when moving from rare to dense media
• Light bends away from the normal when moving from dense to rare media
• Refractive Index = Speed of light in vacuum / Speed of light in medium

A 3D animation showing a laser beam traveling through air, hitting a glass block, and bending towards the normal line, with speed vectors changing size.

Part 8 of 12 · ~4 min

Lenses: Anatomy of Converging (Convex) and Diverging (Concave) Lenses

• Convex Lens: Thicker at center, converges light rays to a point.
• Concave Lens: Thinner at center, diverges light rays outwards.
• Optical Centre (O): The central point through which light passes straight.
• Principal Axis: The imaginary line passing through the center of the lens.
• Focal Length (f): Distance between the Optical Centre and the Principal Focus.

A 3D split-screen animation showing a convex lens converging parallel rays to a point F, and a concave lens spreading parallel rays outward, with labels for Optical Centre and Principal Axis appearing dynamically.

Part 9 of 12 · ~4 min

Image Formation by Convex Lenses: Detailed Ray Diagrams and Scenarios

• Convex lenses are converging lenses that bring light rays together.
• Three key ray rules: Parallel rays pass through F, center rays go straight, F-rays emerge parallel.
• Object at infinity: Image at F, real, inverted, and highly diminished.
• Object between 2F and Focus: Image is virtual, erect, and magnified.
• As object distance decreases, image distance and magnification increase.

Lens Formula and Magnification: Mathematical Mastery and Worked Examples

• Lens Formula: 1/v - 1/u = 1/f
• Sign Convention: u is always negative; f is positive for convex, negative for concave
• Magnification (m) = h' / h = v / u
• m > 1: Enlarged | m < 1: Diminished | m is negative: Real & Inverted
• m is positive: Virtual & Erect
