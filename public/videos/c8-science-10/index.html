Topic 1 · Reflection of Light

Reflection of Light

Reflection is the bouncing back of light when it hits a surface. It is like a tennis ball bouncing off a floor, where the direction changes based on the surface angle.

• Regular reflection occurs on smooth surfaces.
• Diffused reflection occurs on rough surfaces.
• The incident ray, reflected ray, and normal all lie in the same plane.

Smooth mirror = clear image; Rough wood = no clear image.

Topic 2 · Plane Mirrors

Plane Mirrors

A plane mirror is a flat reflective surface. It creates images that look exactly like the object but are flipped left-to-right.

• The image is always virtual and erect.
• The image size is equal to the object size.
• The distance of the object from the mirror equals the image distance.

If you stand 2m from a mirror, your image is 2m behind it.

Topic 3 · Spherical Mirrors

Spherical Mirrors

These mirrors have curved surfaces, like the inside or outside of a spoon. They are divided into Concave (curved inward) and Convex (bulged outward).

• Concave mirrors can form real or virtual images.
• Convex mirrors always form virtual, erect, and diminished images.
• The Center of Curvature (C) is the center of the sphere.

A dentist uses a concave mirror to see a larger image of a tooth.

Topic 4 · Lenses: Refraction and Focus

Lenses: Refraction and Focus

Lenses use refraction, which is the bending of light as it passes through glass. Think of a straw looking broken in a glass of water.

• Convex lenses are 'converging' (bring rays together).
• Concave lenses are 'diverging' (spread rays apart).
• Lenses form images based on their focal length.

A magnifying glass uses a convex lens to enlarge text.
