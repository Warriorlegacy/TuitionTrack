Introduction: Real-world Applications, Order, and Degree of Differential Equations

• Differential Equation: An equation involving an unknown function and its derivatives.
• Real-world use: Modeling population growth, cooling, and physical motion.
• Order: The order of the highest derivative present in the equation.
• Degree: The power of the highest-order derivative in polynomial form.
• Note: Degree is undefined if the equation isn't a polynomial in derivatives.

Formation of Differential Equations: Eliminating Arbitrary Constants

• Goal: Create a differential equation by removing arbitrary constants.
• Rule: Number of differentiations = Number of arbitrary constants.
• Order of the resulting equation equals the number of constants.
• Step 1: Differentiate the equation with respect to the independent variable.
• Step 2: Use substitution to eliminate constants from the expression.

Solutions of Differential Equations: General, Particular, and Concept of Families of Curve

• Solution: A function that satisfies the differential equation.
• Family of Curves: A collection of curves defined by arbitrary constants.
• General Solution: Contains arbitrary constants (n constants for order n).
• Particular Solution: A specific solution with constants determined by conditions.
• Initial Conditions: Used to find specific values for arbitrary constants.

Variable Separable Method: Techniques and Worked Examples

• Goal: Group all 'y' terms with dy and all 'x' terms with dx
• Standard Form: dy/g(y) = f(x)dx
• Always add the constant of integration 'C' immediately after integrating
• Use initial conditions to find the Particular Solution
• Watch for division by zero when separating variables

Homogeneous Differential Equations: Substitution and Solving Methods

• Definition: dy/dx = f(y/x) where all terms have equal degree
• Key Substitution: Let y = vx, then dy/dx = v + x(dv/dx)
• Goal: Transform homogeneous equations into variable separable form
• Step-by-step: Substitute, Simplify, Separate, and Integrate
• Final Step: Always substitute v = y/x back into the solution

Reducible to Variable Separable Form: Bernoulli’s Equation and Transformations

• Bernoulli's Form: dy/dx + P(x)y = Q(x)y^n
• Substitution for Bernoulli: Let v = y^(1-n)
• Transforming to Linear: dv/dx + (1-n)P(x)v = (1-n)Q(x)
• Shift of Origin: x = X + h, y = Y + k for non-homogeneous lines
• Always substitute back the original variables at the end

Linear Differential Equations: Integrating Factor and Standard Form

• Standard Form: dye/dx + P(x)y = Q(x)
• Linearity: y and dye/dx must be first degree
• Integrating Factor: I(x) = e raised to the integral of P(x) dx
• Transformation: LHS becomes d/dx [I(x) * y]
• General Solution: I(x)y = integral of [Q(x) * I(x)] dx + C

Application of Differential Equations: Growth, Decay, and Population Models

• Rate of change is proportional to the amount present: dP/dt = kP
• Growth model: k > 0; Decay model: k < 0
• General solution: P(t) = P0 * e^(kt)
• P0 represents the initial quantity at time t = 0
• Use initial conditions to solve for the constant k

Application of Differential Equations: Newton's Law of Cooling and Related Word Problems

• Newton's Law: Rate of cooling is proportional to the temperature difference
• Differential Equation: d T / d t = -k (T - S)
• General Solution: T = S + A * e^(-kt)
• S = Surrounding temperature; T = Object temperature; k = constant
• Use two given data points to solve for constants A and k

Part 11 of 12 · ~5 min

Common Pitfalls: Degree Ambiguity, Integration Errors, and Sign Mistakes

• Degree is only defined if the equation is a polynomial in its derivatives.
• Trigonometric or logarithmic functions containing derivatives make degree undefined.
• Always add the constant of integration 'C' immediately after integrating.
• In Linear DE, ensure the sign of 'P' is correct when calculating the Integrating Factor.
• Verify your final solution by differentiating it to see if it matches the original.
