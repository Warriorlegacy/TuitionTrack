Constructing Circles: Radius, Diameter, and Center-Point Accuracy

• The Center is the fixed point where the compass metal tip is placed.
• Radius is the constant distance from the center to the edge.
• Diameter is a line segment passing through the center; Diameter equals two times Radius.
• Always hold the compass by the top handle to maintain width accuracy.
• To draw a circle from a diameter, first calculate the radius by dividing by two.

• Perpendicular lines meet at exactly ninety degrees.
• Method 1: Constructing a perpendicular through a point on a line.
• Method 2: Constructing a perpendicular from a point outside the line.

Constructing Bisectors: Angle Bisectors and Line Segment Bisectors

• Bisect means to divide into two exactly equal parts
• Line Segment Bisector: Finds the midpoint and creates a perpendicular line
• Crucial Rule: Compass width must be more than half the segment length
• Angle Bisector: A ray that divides an angle into two equal smaller angles
• Precision Tip: Keep compass width steady and use a sharp pencil

Constructing Angles: Basic Angles (60°, 120°, 90°) and Their Derivations

• Construct 60° using the equilateral triangle method
• Derive 120° by adding another 60° arc segment
• Construct 90° using the perpendicular bisector technique
• Always keep compass width constant for equilateral constructions
• Use the intersection of arcs to define precise vertices

Part 8 of 12 · ~5 min

Constructing Specific Angles: 45°, 30°, and 75° via Bisecting Methods

• 45° is derived by bisecting a 90° angle
• 30° is derived by bisecting a 60° angle
• 75° is the bisector of the gap between 60° and 90°
• Always maintain consistent compass width during bisection
• Precision comes from logical derivation, not just measurement

Constructing Quadrilaterals I: Using Side Lengths and Diagonals

• A quadrilateral is defined by four sides and often requires diagonals for stability.
• Always start by constructing the base line segment using a ruler.
• Use a compass to find vertices by intersecting arcs of known side lengths.
• Diagonals are essential to fix the specific angles of the quadrilateral.
• Precision in compass width is the key to an accurate construction.

Constructing Quadrilaterals II: Using Angles and Sides (Parallelograms & Rectangles)

• Parallelogram: Requires two adjacent sides and the included angle.
• Construction Step: Use arcs to locate the final vertex using opposite side lengths.
• Rectangle: A special parallelogram where all angles are ninety degrees.
• Perpendicularity: Essential to construct the sides of a rectangle.
• Accuracy Tip: Ensure compass width remains constant for opposite sides.

Common Pitfalls: Avoiding Measurement Errors and Tool Misuse

• Align measurements with the zero mark, not the ruler's edge.
• Avoid Parallax Error by looking directly down at the scale.
• Ensure the compass hinge is tight to maintain a constant radius.
• Check the protractor scale: acute angles must be under 90 degrees.
• Always use a sharp pencil to ensure thin, precise lines.
