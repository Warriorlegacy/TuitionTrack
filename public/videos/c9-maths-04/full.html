Defining Linear Equations in Two Variables: Standard Form ax + by + c = 0

• Standard Form: ax + by + c = 0
• x and y are the two variables
• a and b are coefficients; a and b cannot both be zero
• c is the constant term
• The highest power of x and y must be 1

Finding Solutions: The Concept of Ordered Pairs (x, y)

• A solution is a pair (x, y) that makes the equation true.
• Ordered pairs are written as (x, y) where position matters.
• One equation in two variables has infinitely many solutions.
• Substitution: Replace a variable with a value to solve for the other.
• Example: For x + y = 10, if x = 2, then y = 8. Solution is (2, 8).

Mathematical Activity: Generating Multiple Solutions for a Single Equation

• Step 1: Substitute a value for one variable (e.g., x = 0)
• Step 2: Solve the resulting equation for the second variable (y)
• Step 3: Write the result as an ordered pair (x, y)
• Step 4: Repeat with different values, including negatives and decimals
• Conclusion: A linear equation has infinitely many solutions

Special Cases: Equations of Lines Parallel to X-axis and Y-axis

• Equations of the form y = k are horizontal lines parallel to the x-axis.
• Equations of the form x = k are vertical lines parallel to the y-axis.
• The equation y = 0 represents the x-axis itself.
• The equation x = 0 represents the y-axis itself.
• In these cases, one of the coefficients (a or b) is zero.

Common Pitfalls: Identifying Errors in Calculation, Graphing, and Signs

• Watch out for sign errors when substituting negative values
• Always rearrange equations into the standard form ax + by + c = 0
• Ensure you divide the entire expression when isolating a variable
• Check that plotted points form a perfectly straight line
• Remember that x = k and y = k are valid linear equations
