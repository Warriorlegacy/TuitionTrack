• Combustion: fuel + oxygen produce heat & light
• Fuels are solid, liquid, or gas — each burns
• Ignition temp: minimum heat to start fire
• Oxygen supports burning; fire dies without it

Part 3 of 12 · ~4 min

The Fire Triangle: Understanding the Three Essential Elements

• The Fire Triangle: Three essential elements required for combustion.
• Fuel: Any solid, liquid, or gas that can be burned.
• Oxygen: The oxidizing agent present in the air that supports the flame.
• Heat: The energy needed to reach a substance's ignition temperature.
• Fire Suppression: Removing any one element breaks the triangle and stops fire.

Chemical Reactions and Heat: How Fire Transforms Matter

• Combustion is an exothermic chemical reaction.
• Fuel reacts with an oxidant, typically oxygen.
• Chemical change: Matter is transformed into new substances.
• Complete combustion produces Carbon Dioxide and Water.
• Incomplete combustion produces Carbon Monoxide and Soot.

A 3D molecular animation showing carbon and hydrogen atoms breaking bonds and re-bonding with oxygen to form CO2 and H2O molecules, releasing glowing energy pulses.

Part 7 of 12 · ~5 min

Diagrammatic Analysis: Anatomy of a Flame and Heat Transfer

• Innermost Zone: Dark, cool, and contains unburnt carbon particles.
• Middle Zone: Luminous yellow zone where partial combustion occurs.
• Outermost Zone: Non-luminous blue zone; the hottest part of the flame.
• Conduction: Heat transfer through direct contact in solid materials.
• Convection and Radiation: Heat movement in fluids and through waves.

A 3D cross-section of a candle flame showing color-coded zones, transitioning into a split-screen animation demonstrating conduction, convection, and radiation.

Safety Protocols: Fire Extinguishers and Prevention Techniques

• Prevention: Removing fuel or oxygen to stop fire formation.
• Electrical Safety: Avoiding overloaded sockets to prevent sparks.
• Class A, B, and C Fires: Matching the extinguisher to the fuel type.
• The P-A-S-S Technique: Pull, Aim, Squeeze, and Sweep.
• Emergency Protocol: Evacuate immediately if fire becomes uncontrollable.

Activity Workshop: Real-world Scenarios and Fire Safety Drills

• Never use water on oil or electrical fires.
• Smother small fires by cutting off the oxygen supply.
• Stop, Drop, and Roll if your clothing catches fire.
• Stay low to the ground to avoid inhaling smoke and toxic gases.
• Always use the back of your hand to check if a door is hot.

Common Pitfalls: Misconceptions about Fire and Combustion

• Fire is a process (combustion), not a physical substance.
• Heat is energy transfer; it does not always require a flame.
• Fire requires oxygen concentration, not just 'fresh air'.
• Water is NOT suitable for grease or electrical fires.
• The outer zone of a flame is hotter than the inner zone.
