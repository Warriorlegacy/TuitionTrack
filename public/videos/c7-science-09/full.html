The Human Digestive System: Anatomy, Enzymes, and Absorption

• Alimentary canal: A continuous tube from mouth to anus
• Stomach: Secretes hydrochloric acid and pepsin for protein digestion
• Liver and Pancreas: Produce bile and pancreatic juice for chemical breakdown
• Villi: Finger-like projections in the small intestine that increase absorption
• Large Intestine: Absorbs water and salts from undigested waste

The Role of Teeth, Tongue, and Saliva in Mechanical and Chemical Digestion

• Mechanical digestion: Physical breakdown of food by teeth
• Incisors: Cutting; Canines: Tearing; Molars: Grinding
• Tongue: Mixing food and forming a bolus for swallowing
• Chemical digestion: Salivary amylase breaks starch into maltose
• Saliva: Lubricates food and aids in the sense of taste

Respiration in Animals: Aerobic vs. Anaerobic & Cellular Respiration

• Respiration is a chemical process occurring inside cells to release energy.
• ATP (Adenosine Triphosphate) is the energy currency of the cell.
• Aerobic Respiration: Uses oxygen to fully break down glucose into CO2 and Water.
• Anaerobic Respiration: Occurs without oxygen, producing less energy.
• Lactic Acid buildup in muscles during intense exercise causes cramps.

A split-screen 3D animation showing a cell using oxygen to produce high ATP (Aerobic) versus a muscle cell producing Lactic Acid under low oxygen (Anaerobic).

Part 6 of 12 · ~5 min

The Human Respiratory System: Mechanism of Breathing and Gas Exchange

• Air pathway: Nostrils -> Trachea -> Bronchi -> Bronchioles -> Alveoli
• Alveoli: Tiny air sacs where gas exchange occurs with blood capillaries
• Gas Exchange: Oxygen enters blood; Carbon dioxide leaves blood
• Inhalation: Diaphragm contracts (moves down), ribs move out, air enters
• Exhalation: Diaphragm relaxes (moves up), ribs move in, air exits

A 3D cross-section animation showing air flowing through the trachea into branching bronchioles, zooming into a single alveolus surrounded by red and blue capillaries to show oxygen and carbon dioxide swapping.

Transportation in Animals: Structure and Function of the Human Heart

• Heart: A muscular pump located in the chest cavity.
• Four Chambers: Two upper Atria and two lower Ventricles.
• Septum: A muscular wall that prevents mixing of blood types.
• Valves: One-way doors that prevent the backflow of blood.
• Left Ventricle: Has thick walls to pump blood to the whole body.

A 3D anatomical model of the human heart showing the four chambers, the septum, and rhythmic pumping action with color-coded blue (deoxygenated) and red (oxygenated) blood flow.

Circulatory System: Blood Components, Vessels, and Double Circulation

• Blood components: Plasma, Red Blood Cells, White Blood Cells, and Platelets
• Hemoglobin: The protein in RBCs that transports oxygen
• Arteries: Thick-walled vessels carrying blood away from the heart
• Veins: Vessels with valves to prevent backflow of blood
• Double Circulation: Separate Pulmonary and Systemic circuits

Excretion in Animals: The Human Urinary System and Nephron Function

• The urinary system includes kidneys, ureters, bladder, and urethra.
• Kidneys are bean-shaped organs that filter metabolic waste from blood.
• Nephrons are the microscopic functional units of the kidney.
• Glomerulus filters blood; Bowman's capsule collects the filtrate.
• Selective reabsorption in the renal tubule recovers glucose and salts.

A 3D medical animation starting with a full human torso, zooming into the kidneys, then transitioning into a microscopic view of a single nephron showing blood flowing through the glomerulus and nutrients moving back into capillaries.

Osmoregulation and Other Excretory Organs in Different Animal Groups

• Osmoregulation: Maintaining salt and water balance in the body.
• Amoeba: Uses contractile vacuoles to pump out excess water.
• Earthworms: Use tube-like nephridia for excretion and regulation.
• Insects: Malpighian tubules collect waste and conserve water.
• Fish: Adaptations differ between freshwater and marine environments.
