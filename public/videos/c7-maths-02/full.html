Introduction: Real-world Applications of Fractions and Decimals and the Concept of Parts of a Whole

• Fractions and decimals represent parts of a whole entity.
• Real-world uses: sharing food, measuring time, and weighing goods.
• The Numerator: Represents the number of parts we have.
• The Denominator: Represents the total number of equal parts in a whole.
• Crucial Rule: All fractional parts must be of equal size.

Understanding Fractions: Proper, Improper, and Mixed Fractions with Visual Models and Conversion

• Proper Fraction: Numerator is smaller than the denominator (Value < 1)
• Improper Fraction: Numerator is greater than or equal to denominator (Value >= 1)
• Mixed Fraction: A combination of a whole number and a proper fraction
• Converting Improper to Mixed: Divide numerator by denominator
• Converting Mixed to Improper: (Whole Number x Denominator) + Numerator

Equivalent Fractions and Simplest Form: The Logic of Scaling and Reducing Fractions

• Equivalent fractions represent the same value despite different numbers.
• Scaling Up: Multiply numerator and denominator by the same non-zero number.
• Scaling Down: Divide numerator and denominator by a common factor.
• Simplest Form: When the only common factor is one (co-prime numbers).
• Rule: Whatever operation you perform on the top, perform on the bottom.

Comparing and Ordering Fractions: Using LCM and Cross-Multiplication Techniques

• Comparing fractions with different denominators requires a common ground.
• LCM Method: Convert fractions to equivalent fractions using the Least Common Multiple.
• Cross-Multiplication: A shortcut for comparing two fractions by multiplying diagonally.
• To order multiple fractions, find a common denominator for all of them first.
• Always check your products in cross-multiplication to determine the larger fraction.

Addition and Subtraction of Fractions: Like vs. Unlike Denominators and Common Error Prevention

• Like Denominators: Add or subtract numerators, keep denominator the same.
• Unlike Denominators: Find the L.C.M. to create equivalent fractions first.
• Common Error: Never add the denominators together.
• Always simplify your final answer to the lowest terms.
• Step-by-step: Find L.C.M. -> Convert -> Add/Subtract -> Simplify.

Multiplication of Fractions: Fraction by Whole Number, Fraction by Fraction, and 'Of' as a

• Whole Number x Fraction: Multiply whole number by numerator only.
• Fraction x Fraction: Multiply numerators together and denominators together.
• The 'OF' Rule: In fraction problems, 'of' means multiplication.
• Result Size: Multiplying proper fractions results in a smaller value.
• Simplification: Always reduce your final answer to the simplest form.

Division of Fractions: The Reciprocal Concept and the 'Keep-Change-Flip' Method

• Reciprocal: Flipping the numerator and denominator of a fraction.
• A number multiplied by its reciprocal always equals one.
• Keep-Change-Flip: The three-step method for dividing fractions.
• Step 1: Keep the first fraction; Step 2: Change ÷ to ×; Step 3: Flip the second fraction.
• Always simplify your final answer to its lowest terms.

Introduction to Decimals: Place Value Charts, Tenths, Hundredths, and Decimal Notation

• Decimal point separates whole numbers from fractional parts.
• The Tenths place represents parts of ten (one over ten).
• The Hundredths place represents parts of a hundred (one over one hundred).
• Moving right in the chart makes the value ten times smaller.
• Decimals are a convenient way to write fractions with denominators of 10, 100, etc.

Operations with Decimals: Addition, Subtraction, and Multiplication (Aligning Decimals vs. Standard Multiplication)

• For addition and subtraction, always align the decimal points vertically.
• Use placeholder zeros to ensure all decimal places are filled.
• In multiplication, treat decimals as whole numbers first.
• Count total decimal places in both factors to place the decimal in the product.
• Addition/Subtraction = Align points; Multiplication = Count and shift points.

Part 11 of 12 · ~4 min

Division of Decimals: Dividing by Whole Numbers and Other Decimals

• Dividing by a whole number: Place the decimal point straight up in the quotient.
• Dividing by a decimal: Convert the divisor into a whole number first.
• The Golden Rule: Shift the decimal in the dividend the same number of places as the divisor.
• Use placeholder zeros if you need to shift the decimal further in the dividend.
• Shifting decimals is equivalent to multiplying both numbers by powers of ten.
