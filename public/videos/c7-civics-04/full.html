Case Studies: Growing Up in Samoa vs. Madhya Pradesh (Cultural Variations)

• Samoa (1920s): Children had freedom and learned through communal work.
• Samoan Boys: Focused on fishing, navigation, and outdoor survival.
• Madhya Pradesh: Clearer divide between public (boys) and private (girls) spheres.
• Madhya Pradesh Girls: Socialized early for domestic roles and household care.
• Conclusion: Gender roles are culturally constructed, not biologically fixed.

Analyzing Domestic Work: Definitions, Intensity, and the 'Double Burden'

• Domestic work: A collection of household maintenance and caregiving tasks.
• Intensity: Measured by duration, repetition, and constant mental load.
• Time-use disparity: Women and girls spend more daily hours on unpaid labor.
• The Double Burden: Managing both professional jobs and domestic chores.
• Social construction: Domestic roles are shaped by society, not biology.

• Socialization begins from birth through toys, colors, and clothing.
• Boys are often socialized toward strength and emotional restraint.
• Girls are often socialized toward nurturing and domestic roles.

Common Pitfalls: Distinguishing Between Sex (Biological) and Gender (Social)

• Sex: Refers to biological and physiological differences (Nature).
• Gender: Refers to social, cultural, and psychological roles (Society).
• Sex is universal and constant; Gender varies by culture and time.
• Biological traits (e.g., childbirth) vs. Social roles (e.g., housework).
• Gender is a 'social construct'—it is learned, not innate.
