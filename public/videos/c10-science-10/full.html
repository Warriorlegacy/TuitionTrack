Power of Accommodation: How the Eye Focuses and Near/Far Points

• Eye's ability to adjust focal length.
• Ciliary muscles control lens shape.
• Distant objects: thin lens, long focal length.
• Nearby objects: thick lens, short focal length.
• Far point (infinity), Near point (~25 cm).

Part 4 of 12 · ~5 min

Defects of Vision I: Myopia (Nearsightedness) - Causes and Correction

• Myopia: Nearby objects clear, distant objects blurred.
• Causes: Eyeball too long or lens too powerful.
• Image forms in front of the retina.
• Correction: Concave (diverging) lens used.
• Concave lens shifts focal point onto retina.

3D animation showing a myopic eye with light rays from a distant object converging in front of the retina, then showing the correction with a concave lens, causing the rays to converge on the retina.

Part 5 of 12 · ~5 min

Defects of Vision II: Hypermetropia (Farsightedness) and Presbyopia

• Hypermetropia: Image forms behind retina.
• Causes: Long focal length or short eyeball.
• Correction: Convex lens increases converging power.
• Presbyopia: Age-related loss of accommodation.
• Correction: Convex lens, often bifocal.

3D animation showing light rays from a nearby object entering an eye with hypermetropia, focusing behind the retina, and then showing the corrected eye with a convex lens focusing the rays on the retina.

Defects of Vision III: Astigmatism and Cataract Management

• Astigmatism: uneven corneal curvature
• Cylindrical lenses correct meridian differences
• Keratometry maps corneal shape
• Cataract: lens clouding, protein clumps
• Phacoemulsification removes cataract

Refraction through a Prism: Angle of Deviation and Dispersion

• Refraction: Light bends when entering/leaving prism.
• Angle of Deviation (δ): Bending of light ray.
• Angle of Minimum Deviation (δm): Special case for calculation.
• Dispersion: White light splits into VIBGYOR colours.
• Violet bent most, Red bent least due to refractive index.

3D animation of a triangular prism with a white light ray entering, refracting, splitting into VIBGYOR colours, and emerging as a spectrum.

Newton's Experiment: Recombination of Spectrum and the Rainbow

• Newton's experiment recombines the spectrum.
• Second prism inverts the spectrum.
• White light is a mixture of colors.
• Rainbows are formed by water droplets.
• Water droplets act as tiny prisms.

Atmospheric Refraction I: Twinkling of Stars and Advanced Sunrise/Delayed Sunset

• Stars twinkle due to irregular atmospheric refraction.
• Planets appear steady as refraction effect is averaged.
• Atmosphere refracts light, bending it towards Earth.
• Advanced sunrise: Sun seen before geometric rising.
• Delayed sunset: Sun seen after geometric setting.

3D animation showing starlight bending through turbulent atmospheric layers, then a separate animation showing sunlight rays from below the horizon bending towards an observer.

Part 10 of 12 · ~6 min

Atmospheric Refraction II: Apparent Position of Stars and Tyndall Effect

• Stars appear higher due to atmospheric refraction.
• Light bends continuously through atmospheric layers.
• Brain perceives light as traveling straight.
• Tyndall effect: scattering of light by particles.
• Visible light beams in fog, dust, and smoke.

3D animation showing light rays from a star bending as they enter Earth's atmosphere, with the apparent position of the star shown higher than its actual position. Then, a scene transitions to a dark room with a beam of sunlight entering, illuminating dust particles, demonstrating the Tyndall effect.

Scattering of Light: Why the Sky is Blue and Sunsets are Red

• Rayleigh scattering ∝ 1/λ⁴, blue scatters more
• Sunset: long path removes blue, leaves red
• Mie scattering: larger particles, color‑independent
• Tyndall effect: blue line in milk or fog
• Pollution increases Mie, dulling sky color
