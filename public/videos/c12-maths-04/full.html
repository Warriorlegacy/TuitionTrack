Properties of Determinants I: Switching Rows/Columns, Identity, and Scalar Multiplication

• Interchanging two rows or columns changes the sign of the determinant.
• The determinant of an Identity Matrix of any order is always 1.
• Multiplying a single row by a scalar 'k' multiplies the determinant by 'k'.
• For an n-by-n matrix, det(kA) = k to the power of n times det(A).
• Properties allow for easier calculation without full expansion.

Properties of Determinants II: Row/Column Operations and Invariance under Elementary Operations

• Three types of elementary operations: Swapping, Scaling, and Row Addition
• Invariance: Adding a multiple of one row to another does not change the value
• Row Addition Rule: Row i becomes Row i + k times Row j (where i is not j)
• Symmetry: Column operations follow the exact same rules as row operations
• Strategic Goal: Use operations to create zeros for easier expansion

Area of a Triangle using Determinants: Formula, Derivation, and Collinearity Applications

• Area = 1/2 |det(Matrix)| where rows are (x1, x2, x3), (y1, y2, y3), and (1, 1, 1)
• Absolute value is used because area must always be a non-negative quantity
• Derivation is linked to the cross product of vectors in a 2D plane
• Three points are collinear if the area of the triangle they form is zero
• To find unknown coordinates for collinearity, set the determinant to zero

Minors and Cofactors: Detailed Calculation, Sign Conventions, and Relationship with Elements

• Minor (M ij): Determinant after deleting row i and column j
• Cofactor (A ij): Formula is (-1)^(i+j) multiplied by Minor M ij
• Sign Convention: Follows a checkerboard pattern of +/-
• Relationship: Sum of elements times cofactors equals the determinant
• Cofactor sign depends on whether (i + j) is even or odd

Adjoint of a Matrix: Step-by-Step Construction and Properties of Adjoint

• Definition: Adjoint is the transpose of the cofactor matrix
• Step 1: Calculate all cofactors; Step 2: Form cofactor matrix; Step 3: Transpose it
• Identity: A times adj A equals det A times Identity matrix
• Determinant Property: det(adj A) equals det(A) to the power of n minus 1
• Product Property: adj(AB) equals adj(B) times adj(A)

Inverse of a Matrix: Derivation via Adjoint Method and Condition for Existence

• Definition: A times A inverse equals the Identity matrix (I)
• The Formula: A inverse equals (1 / det A) times adjoint of A
• Condition for Existence: Determinant of A must be non-zero
• Singular Matrix: A matrix where determinant equals zero (No inverse)
• Non-Singular Matrix: A matrix where determinant is not zero (Invertible)

• Delta: Determinant of the coefficient matrix
• Matrix Equation form: A times X equals B
• Matrix Method solution: X equals A inverse times B

Solving Systems of Linear Equations II: Practical Word Problems and Consistency/Inconsistency

• Consistent system: Has at least one solution (Unique or Infinite)
• Inconsistent system: Has no solution
• If det(A) is not zero: Unique solution exists
• If det(A) is zero and adj(A)B is zero: Infinitely many solutions
• If det(A) is zero and adj(A)B is not zero: No solution
