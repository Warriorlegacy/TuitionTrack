Defining Rational Numbers: Properties, Form, and the Role of Zero

• Rational Number Form: p/q where p and q are integers
• Crucial Condition: The denominator q must NOT be zero
• Integers as Rational Numbers: Any integer n can be written as n/1
• Sign Rules: Same signs result in positive; different signs result in negative
• The Role of Zero: Zero is a rational number because it can be 0/q

The Number Line: Representing Rational Numbers Visually

• The number line is a horizontal line with zero as the central origin.
• Positive rational numbers are placed to the right of zero.
• Negative rational numbers are placed to the left of zero.
• Divide the interval between integers into parts equal to the denominator.
• Use the numerator to count the specific position from the left integer.

Properties of Rational Numbers I: Closure and Commutativity

• Closure Property: Result stays within the same set of numbers.
• Rational numbers are closed under Addition, Subtraction, and Multiplication.
• Division is NOT closed because division by zero is undefined.
• Commutative Property: The order of numbers does not change the result.
• Commutativity holds for Addition and Multiplication, but not Subtraction or Division.

Part 5 of 12 · ~4 min

Properties of Rational Numbers II: Associativity and Distributivity

• Associativity: Grouping numbers does not change the result in addition and multiplication.
• Addition: a + (b + c) = (a + b) + c
• Multiplication: a * (b * c) = (a * b) * c
• Distributivity: a * (b + c) = (a * b) + (a * c)
• Note: Subtraction and Division are NOT associative.

The Identity Elements: Additive Identity (0) and Multiplicative Identity (1)

• Identity Element: A number that leaves the original value unchanged during an operation.
• Additive Identity: Adding zero to any rational number results in the same number.
• Formula: a + 0 = a
• Multiplicative Identity: Multiplying any rational number by one results in the same number.
• Formula: a * 1 = a

Part 7 of 12 · ~4 min

The Inverse Elements: Additive Inverse and Multiplicative Inverse (Reciprocals)

• Additive Inverse: The number that, when added to 'a', results in zero.
• To find the additive inverse, simply change the sign of the number.
• Multiplicative Inverse (Reciprocal): The number that, when multiplied by 'a', results in one.
• To find the reciprocal, flip the numerator and the denominator.
• Important: Zero has no multiplicative inverse (it is undefined).

Operations on Rational Numbers: Addition and Subtraction Techniques

• Same denominators: Add or subtract numerators, keep denominator the same.
• Different denominators: Use the L.C.M. method to find a common denominator.
• Step 1: Find L.C.M. of denominators. Step 2: Convert to equivalent fractions.
• Step 3: Perform operation on numerators and simplify the final result.
• Pro-tip: Convert mixed numbers to improper fractions before calculating.

Operations on Rational Numbers: Multiplication and Division Mastery

• Multiplication: Multiply numerators together and denominators together.
• Always simplify the resulting fraction to its lowest terms.
• Division: Multiply the first number by the reciprocal of the second.
• The 'Keep, Change, Flip' method for easy division.
• A non-zero rational number multiplied by its reciprocal equals one.

Rational Numbers Between Two Rational Numbers: Density Property & Methods

• Density Property: Infinitely many rational numbers exist between any two numbers.
• Method 1: The Mean Method. Formula: (a + b) divided by 2.
• Method 2: Common Denominator Method. Use equivalent fractions to find more numbers.
• The Mean Method is best for finding a single midpoint.
• The Common Denominator Method is best for finding multiple numbers quickly.

Common Mistakes, Pitfalls, and Step-by-Step Word Problem Solving

• Watch out for sign errors: Subtracting a negative is adding a positive.
• Zero has no multiplicative inverse; division by zero is undefined.
• Always find the Least Common Multiple before adding or subtracting.
• Always simplify your final fraction to its lowest terms.
• Use the 4-step method: Read, Translate, Execute, and Interpret.
