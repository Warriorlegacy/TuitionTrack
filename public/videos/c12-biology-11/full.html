• Organism as a functional unit interacting with surroundings

• Water: Crucial for metabolic processes and osmotic balance
• Light: Influences photosynthesis and photoperiodism

Organism and its Environment: Biotic Factors & Physiological Adaptations

• Biotic Factors: Living components like plants, animals, and microbes.
• Adaptation: Features or behaviors enabling survival and reproduction.
• Physiological Adaptation: Internal chemical and metabolic responses.
• Homeostasis: Maintaining a stable internal state despite external changes.
• Examples: Antifreeze proteins, CAM photosynthesis, and osmotic regulation.

Adaptations: Structural, Physiological, and Behavioral Examples (Allen's & Gloger's Rules)

• Adaptations: Structural, Physiological, and Behavioral types
• Structural: Physical body features (e.g., waxy cuticles)
• Physiological: Internal processes (e.g., concentrated urine)
• Behavioral: Actions for survival (e.g., nocturnal habits)

Population Attributes: Density, Natality, Mortality, Age Pyramids & Sex Ratio

• Population Density: Number of individuals per unit area or volume
• Natality (Birth Rate) increases density; Mortality (Death Rate) decreases it
• Age Groups: Pre-reproductive, Reproductive, and Post-reproductive
• Expanding Pyramid: Broad base, high growth; Stable: Bell-shaped; Declining: Narrow base
• Sex Ratio: The proportion of males to females in a population

Part 6 of 12 · ~4 min

Population Growth Models: Exponential Growth & The Logistic Growth Equation

• Exponential Growth: Occurs under ideal conditions with unlimited resources.
• J-shaped Curve: Characterizes rapid, explosive population increase.
• Intrinsic Rate of Increase (r): Per capita birth rate minus per capita death rate.
• Logistic Growth: Realistic model accounting for resource limitation.
• S-shaped (Sigmoid) Curve: Population stabilizes at the Carrying Capacity (K).

A split-screen 3D animation showing a J-shaped curve rising infinitely on the left, and an S-shaped curve leveling off at a horizontal line labeled 'K' on the right.

Part 7 of 12 · ~4 min

Mathematical Derivations: Solving the Logistic Growth Model & Verhulst-Pearl Equation

• Logistic Growth Equation: dN/dt = rN [(K - N) / K]
• K represents Carrying Capacity: the environmental limit
• The term (K - N)/K represents environmental resistance
• Solving via integration yields the Sigmoid or S-shaped curve
• Final Verhulst-Pearl Formula: N(t) = K / [1 + ((K-N0)/N0)e^-rt]

Population Interactions I: Mutualism, Competition, and Predation Dynamics

• Interaction Notation: (+) Benefit, (-) Harm, (0) Neutral
• Mutualism (+/+): Symbiotic cooperation like Lichens (Fungus + Algae)
• Competition (-/-): Struggle for limited resources; Competitive Exclusion Principle
• Resource Partitioning: Species evolving to use different niches to coexist
• Predation (+/-): Predator-prey dynamics and evolutionary adaptations

A split-screen 3D animation showing a lichen growing on a rock, followed by a forest scene where two bird species occupy different canopy levels to avoid competition.

Part 9 of 12 · ~3 min

Population Interactions II: Parasitism, Commensalism, and Amensalism

• Parasitism: One benefits (+), one is harmed (-).
• Ectoparasites live outside; Endoparasites live inside the host.
• Commensalism: One benefits (+), the other is unaffected (0).
• Example: Cattle egrets following grazing cattle.
• Amensalism: One is harmed (-), the other is unaffected (0).

Part 11 of 12 · ~4 min

Common Mistakes: Misinterpreting Graphs, Formulas, and Ecological Terms

• Carrying Capacity (K) is the horizontal asymptote in Logistic Growth.
• Logistic Growth follows an S-shaped curve; Exponential follows a J-shaped curve.
• Environmental Resistance is represented by the term (1 - N/K).
• Commensalism is (+/0) while Mutualism is (+/+).
• Distinguish between Intraspecific (same species) and Interspecific (different species) competition.
