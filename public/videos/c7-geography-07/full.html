The Ecosystem: Understanding Biotic and Abiotic Interactions

• Ecosystem: A community of interacting living and non-living components.
• Biotic Factors: All living organisms like plants, animals, and microbes.
• Abiotic Factors: Non-living elements like sunlight, water, soil, and air.
• Energy Flow: The continuous cycle of nutrients between biotic and abiotic parts.
• Interdependence: Life depends on the balance between living and non-living factors.

Human Adaptation to Natural Environments: Extreme Climates and Landscapes

• Adaptation: Changing lifestyles to suit environmental conditions.
• Polar Regions: Thick clothing and high-fat diets for extreme cold.
• Hot Deserts: Loose, light clothing and water conservation strategies.
• Mountainous Regions: Terrace farming and adaptation to low oxygen.
• Rainforests: Stilt houses to manage humidity and damp ground.

The Impact of Human Activities on the Environment: Case Studies and Consequences

• Urban Heat Island Effect: Cities are warmer than rural areas due to concrete.
• Industrial Water Pollution: Chemical discharge disrupts aquatic ecosystems.
• Deforestation: Loss of biodiversity and increased carbon dioxide levels.
• Fossil Fuel Impact: Air pollution and the formation of acid rain.
• The Ripple Effect: How one human action disrupts the entire food chain.

Common Pitfalls: Distinguishing Between Natural and Humanized Environments

• Natural Environment: Driven by self-regulating biological and geological cycles.
• Humanized Environment: Modified by human technology, planning, and agency.
• The Managed Ecosystem Trap: Parks and plantations are humanized, not purely natural.
• Key Test: Ask if the feature is self-regulating or managed by human intervention.
• The Hybrid Trap: Look for dominant influences like dams or irrigation in landscapes.
