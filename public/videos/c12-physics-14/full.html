Energy Bands in Solids: Conductors, Insulators, and Semiconductors

• Discrete energy levels split into continuous Energy Bands in solids
• Valence Band: Highest occupied energy band
• Conduction Band: Band where electrons move freely to conduct current
• Forbidden Energy Gap (E-G): The gap between Valence and Conduction bands
• Conductors: Overlapping bands; Insulators: Large E-G; Semiconductors: Small E-G

Intrinsic Semiconductors: Charge Carriers, Conductivity, and Temperature Dependence

• Intrinsic semiconductors are chemically pure (e.g., pure Silicon or Germanium).
• At absolute zero, they act as insulators; at higher temperatures, they conduct.
• Charge carriers are created in pairs: free electrons and holes.
• In intrinsic semiconductors, electron concentration (n) equals hole concentration (p).
• Conductivity increases with temperature due to exponential carrier growth.

Extrinsic Semiconductors: N-type and P-type Doping & Carrier Concentration

• Doping: Adding impurities to increase conductivity
• N-type: Pentavalent impurities (Group 15) create extra electrons
• P-type: Trivalent impurities (Group 13) create holes
• Majority carriers: Electrons in N-type, Holes in P-type
• Law of Mass Action: n times p equals n-i squared

A 3D crystal lattice animation showing a Phosphorus atom donating an electron and a Boron atom creating a hole vacancy.

p-n Junction Formation: Depletion Layer, Barrier Potential, and Band Diagrams

• Diffusion occurs due to concentration gradient of electrons and holes
• Depletion layer is formed by immobile positive and negative ions
• Barrier potential is the internal electric field opposing diffusion
• Equilibrium is reached when diffusion force equals electric field force
• Fermi levels align across the junction, causing energy band bending

A 3D split-screen animation showing ions forming at the junction and energy bands bending to meet at a single horizontal Fermi level.

Part 6 of 12 · ~5 min

p-n Junction Diode: Forward and Reverse Biasing Dynamics

• Forward Bias: P-type to Positive, N-type to Negative
• Forward Bias reduces depletion layer width and barrier height
• Reverse Bias: P-type to Negative, N-type to Positive
• Reverse Bias increases depletion layer width and barrier height
• Reverse Saturation Current is due to minority charge carriers

A split-screen 3D animation showing a p-n junction; left side shows carriers rushing to the junction under forward bias, right side shows carriers fleeing the junction under reverse bias.

Part 7 of 12 · ~5 min

Diode Characteristics: I-V Curves, Knee Voltage, and Breakdown Mechanisms

• Forward Bias: Current increases exponentially after reaching the Knee Voltage.
• Knee Voltage: Approximately 0.7V for Silicon and 0.3V for Germanium.
• Reverse Bias: Only a tiny Reverse Saturation Current flows due to minority carriers.
• Zener Breakdown: Occurs in thin depletion layers due to strong electric fields.
• Avalanche Breakdown: Occurs due to impact ionization and carrier multiplication.

Application of Diodes: Half-Wave and Full-Wave Rectifiers (Step-by-Step)

• Rectification: Converting A-C to D-C using p-n junction diodes
• Half-Wave Rectifier: Uses one diode; conducts only during positive half-cycle
• Full-Wave Rectifier: Uses two or four diodes to utilize both A-C cycles
• Center-Tapped vs. Bridge Rectifier: Efficiency and transformer requirements
• Output Waveform: From disconnected pulses to continuous D-C pulses

Special Purpose Diodes: Zener Diode, LED, Photodiode, and Solar Cells

• Zener Diode: Operates in reverse breakdown for voltage regulation
• LED: Converts electrical energy into light via electron-hole recombination
• Photodiode: Reverse-biased device where current increases with light intensity
• Solar Cell: Converts solar energy into electricity using the photovoltaic effect
• Key Difference: LEDs emit light; Photodiodes and Solar Cells detect light

Logic Gates: OR, AND, NOT, NAND, and NOR (Truth Tables & Symbols)

• Binary Logic: 1 represents High/True, 0 represents Low/False
• OR Gate: Output is 1 if any input is 1 (Y = A + B)
• AND Gate: Output is 1 only if all inputs are 1 (Y = A . B)
• NOT Gate: Inverts the input (Y = A bar)
• Universal Gates: NAND and NOR can implement any logic function

Common Mistakes, Numerical Problem-Solving Strategies & Derivation Tips

• Avoid mixing N-type (Pentavalent) and P-type (Trivalent) properties.
• Always label I-V characteristic axes with correct units (mA/uA).
• Use n times p equals n-zero squared for carrier concentration math.
• In rectifiers, remember output frequency is double the input frequency.
• Follow the Three-Step Rule: Diagram, Technical Theory, and Formula.
