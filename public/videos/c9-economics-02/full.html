Introduction: Why People are the Greatest Asset (The Concept of Human Capital)

• Resources are not just land and machines; people are the ultimate resource.
• Human Capital: The stock of skill and productive knowledge embodied in people.
• Difference between Population (liability) and Human Capital (asset).
• Human Capital Formation: Turning people into productive economic contributors.
• Productive people increase the Gross National Product (G N P) of a nation.

Economic Activities by Men and Women: Primary, Secondary, and Tertiary Sectors

• Primary Sector: Activities directly using natural resources like agriculture and mining.
• Secondary Sector: Manufacturing and industrial activities that process raw materials.
• Tertiary Sector: Service-based activities that support production and trade.
• Primary sector forms the base; Secondary sector adds value; Tertiary sector facilitates.
• Economic flow: Raw material (Primary) -> Manufacturing (Secondary) -> Services (Tertiary).

A 3D flowchart showing a cotton plant (Primary) transforming into a shirt in a factory (Secondary) and then being delivered by a truck to a store (Tertiary).

Part 4 of 12 · ~4 min

Market vs. Non-Market Activities: Definitions, Differences, and Real-world Examples

• Market Activities: Performed for pay, wages, or profit; adds to National Income.
• Non-Market Activities: Performed for self-consumption; not included in G.D.P.
• Economic Value: Market activities are monetized; non-market activities are not.
• Gender Dimension: Women often perform more unpaid non-market domestic labor.
• Example: A chef in a restaurant (Market) vs. a mother cooking at home (Non-Market).

Unemployment: Concept, Types, and Identification

• Unemployment: Willing and able workers unable to find jobs.
• Seasonal Unemployment: Work available only during specific months.
• Disguised Unemployment: More people working than required; zero marginal productivity.
• Educated Unemployment: Mismatch between degree holders and job availability.
• Identification: Differentiating between rural and urban unemployment patterns.

A split-screen 3D animation showing a farmer waiting during a dry season, a crowded farm with 'extra' workers highlighted in red, and a graduate holding a degree in a city queue.

The Economic Consequences of Unemployment: Impact on Society and Economy

• Wastage of human resources and investment in education/health
• Decline in the overall quality of the population
• Reduction in the economic productive capacity and G.D.P.
• Social unrest, increased crime rates, and mental stress
• Brain Drain: Loss of skilled talent to other nations
