Introduction: The Real-World Necessity of Integers and the Number Line Concept

• Natural numbers and whole numbers are not enough for all real-life scenarios.
• Integers include positive numbers, negative numbers, and zero.
• Zero acts as the central reference point with no sign.
• Positive values represent direction like height, profit, or temperature rise.

Defining Integers: Positive, Negative, and Zero with Visual Representations

• Integers consist of Positive Integers, Negative Integers, and Zero.
• Positive Integers: Numbers greater than zero (e.g., +1, +2, +3).
• Negative Integers: Numbers less than zero (e.g., -1, -2, -3).
• Zero: The neutral origin; neither positive nor negative.
• Number Line: Positive to the right, Negative to the left, Zero in the center.

Absolute Value and Comparing Integers: Rules for Magnitude and Direction

• On a number line, numbers to the right are always greater than numbers to the left.
• Positive integers > Zero > Negative integers.
• For negative integers, the number with the smaller digit is actually greater.
• Absolute Value is the distance of an integer from zero, always non-negative.
• Symbol for Absolute Value: |x| represents the magnitude of x.

Addition of Integers: Rules for Like and Unlike Signs with Number Line Activity

• Like Signs: Add the values and keep the common sign
• Unlike Signs: Find the difference and use the sign of the larger absolute value
• Number Line: Positive means move right, Negative means move left
• Example: (-4) + (-2) = -6 (Same signs, add and keep negative)
• Example: (-7) + (+3) = -4 (Different signs, subtract and keep negative)

Subtraction of Integers: The Additive Inverse Concept and Visualizing Movement

• Subtraction is adding the additive inverse
• Additive Inverse: The number that sums to zero with the original
• Rule: a minus b is the same as a plus (negative b)
• Subtracting a negative integer is equivalent to adding a positive
• Number Line: Adding negative moves left; adding positive moves right

Multiplication of Integers: Sign Rules and Pattern-Based Derivations

• Multiplication is repeated addition of the same integer.
• Positive × Negative = Negative (e.g., 3 × -2 = -6).
• Negative × Negative = Positive (derived via patterns).
• Rule 1: Same signs result in a positive product.
• Rule 2: Different signs result in a negative product.

Division of Integers: Properties and Relationship with Multiplication

• Division is the inverse operation of multiplication.
• Same signs result in a positive quotient.
• Different signs result in a negative quotient.
• If a divided by b equals c, then b multiplied by c equals a.
• Division by zero is undefined in mathematics.

Part 8 of 12 · ~5 min

Properties of Addition and Subtraction: Closure, Commutative, and Associative Laws

• Closure Property: The sum or difference of two integers is always an integer.
• Commutative Property (Addition): a + b = b + a. Order does not change the sum.
• Commutative Property (Subtraction): a - b is NOT equal to b - a.
• Associative Property (Addition): (a + b) + c = a + (b + c). Grouping does not change the sum.
• Associative Property (Subtraction): Grouping changes the result; it is not associative.

Properties of Multiplication: Identity, Zero, Commutative, Associative, and Distributive Laws

• Multiplicative Identity: Any integer multiplied by 1 remains unchanged.
• Zero Property: The product of any integer and 0 is always 0.
• Commutative Property: Order of multiplication does not change the product (a x b = b x a).
• Associative Property: The grouping of integers does not change the product.
• Distributive Property: a x (b + c) = (a x b) + (a x c)

Order of Operations with Integers: BODMAS/PEMDAS Application

• BODMAS: Brackets, Of, Division, Multiplication, Addition, Subtraction
• Always solve innermost brackets first (Parentheses, then Square, then Curly)
• Division and Multiplication have equal priority; solve left to right
• Addition and Subtraction have equal priority; solve left to right
• The 'Of' operator acts as a multiplication instruction
