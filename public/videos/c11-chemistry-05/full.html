Introduction: The Power of Energy, Systems, and Surroundings

• Thermodynamics: The study of energy transformations and heat/work exchange.
• System: The specific part of the universe under observation.
• Surroundings: Everything else in the universe outside the system.
• Open System: Exchange of both energy and matter with surroundings.
• Closed System: Exchange of energy only; matter is contained.

State Functions, Path Functions, and Extensive vs Intensive Properties

• State Functions: Depend only on initial and final states (e.g., P, V, T, U, H)
• Path Functions: Depend on the route taken (e.g., Work 'w' and Heat 'q')
• Extensive Properties: Depend on the amount of matter (e.g., Mass, Volume, Enthalpy)
• Intensive Properties: Independent of the amount of matter (e.g., Temperature, Density)
• Pro-tip: The ratio of two extensive properties is always an intensive property

Part 3 of 12 · ~4 min

Internal Energy, Work (Pressure-Volume), and Heat (q)

• Internal Energy (U): Sum of all microscopic kinetic and potential energies.
• Heat (q): Energy transfer due to temperature difference; q > 0 is endothermic.
• Work (w): P-V work is defined as w = minus P times delta V.
• First Law of Thermodynamics: delta U = q + w.
• Sign Convention: Work done BY the system is negative; work ON the system is positive.

Part 4 of 12 · ~5 min

Enthalpy (H) and Calorimetry: Measuring Heat Changes

• Enthalpy (H) is defined as H = U + PV
• At constant pressure, change in enthalpy (ΔH) equals heat (qP)
• Exothermic reactions have negative ΔH; Endothermic have positive ΔH
• Calorimetry formula: q = m × c × ΔT
• Specific heat capacity (c) is heat required to raise 1g by 1°C

Part 5 of 12 · ~4 min

Thermochemistry: Standard Enthalpies of Formation, Combustion, and Atomization

• Standard Enthalpy (Delta H-standard) is measured at 1 bar and 25 degrees Celsius.
• Standard Enthalpy of Formation: 1 mole of compound from its elements in stable states.
• The standard enthalpy of formation for an element in its stable state is zero.
• Standard Enthalpy of Combustion: 1 mole of substance burned in excess oxygen.
• Standard Enthalpy of Atomization: 1 mole of substance broken into gaseous atoms.

Part 6 of 12 · ~4 min

Hess's Law of Constant Heat Summation and Bond Enthalpies

• Hess's Law: Total enthalpy change is independent of the reaction path.
• Enthalpy is a state function; only initial and final states matter.
• Reversing a reaction reverses the sign of Delta H.
• Bond Enthalpy: Energy required to break one mole of a bond in gaseous state.
• Delta H = Sum of bond enthalpies of reactants - Sum of bond enthalpies of products.

Part 7 of 12 · ~4 min

Spontaneity and the Second Law of Thermodynamics

• Spontaneous process: Occurs without continuous external intervention.
• The Second Law: Total entropy of the universe must increase for spontaneity.
• Delta S of universe = Delta S of system + Delta S of surroundings.
• Spontaneity is a thermodynamic feasibility, not a measure of reaction rate.
• Equilibrium occurs when the change in total entropy is zero.

Part 8 of 12 · ~5 min

Entropy (S): Disorder, Microstates, and the Third Law

• Entropy (S) measures the degree of disorder or energy dispersal in a system.
• Microstates (W) are the specific ways energy can be distributed among particles.
• Boltzmann's Equation: S = k sub B times ln W.
• Entropy increases from solid to liquid to gas due to increased microstates.
• Third Law: Entropy of a perfect crystal at zero Kelvin is exactly zero.

A 3D transition showing particles moving from a rigid, ordered crystal lattice to a chaotic, high-velocity gas cloud, with a counter displaying the rising number of microstates (W).

Part 9 of 12 · ~4 min

Gibbs Free Energy (G): The Criterion for Spontaneity

• Gibbs Free Energy (G) is a state function used to predict spontaneity.
• Formula: Delta G = Delta H - T times Delta S.
• Delta G < 0: Spontaneous process.
• Delta G > 0: Non-spontaneous process.
• Delta G = 0: System is at equilibrium.

Enthalpy, Entropy, and Gibbs: Mathematical Derivations and Relationships

• Enthalpy definition: H = U + PV
• Gibbs Free Energy equation: ΔG = ΔH - TΔS
• Spontaneity criteria: ΔG < 0 is spontaneous
• Temperature dependence of ΔH and ΔS signs
• Link to equilibrium: ΔG° = -RT ln K

• Step 2: Map variables and always convert units (kJ to J, Celsius to Kelvin).
• Step 3: Write the symbolic equation before substituting numerical values.

• Unit Consistency: Always convert Celsius to Kelvin and check J vs kJ.
