Introduction: Real-world applications and the concept of Degree 2 polynomials

• Quadratic equations model real-world curves like bridges and projectile motion.
• The degree of a polynomial is the highest power of its variable.
• A polynomial of degree two is specifically called a Quadratic Polynomial.
• Linear equations have degree one; Quadratic equations have degree two.
• The term 'Quadratic' is derived from 'quadratus', meaning square.

Standard Form of a Quadratic Equation and Identifying Coefficients

• Standard Form: a x squared + b x + c = 0
• Crucial Rule: The coefficient 'a' must never be zero
• Rearrange all terms to one side to set the equation to zero
• If a term is missing, its coefficient is zero
• Always include the sign (positive or negative) with the coefficient

Solution of a Quadratic Equation: Concept of Roots and Zeroes

• Solving an equation means finding values of x that make the equation true.
• The terms 'Solution' and 'Root' are used interchangeably.
• A quadratic equation typically has two roots.
• Roots are the x-intercepts where the graph crosses the x-axis.
• A 'Zero' is the value of x that makes a polynomial expression equal to zero.

Methods of Solving: Factorization Method (Splitting the Middle Term)

• Standard form: ax^2 + bx + c = 0
• Find p and q such that: p * q = a * c and p + q = b
• Rewrite the middle term 'bx' as 'px + qx'
• Group terms and extract common binomial factors
• Apply Zero Product Property to find the roots

Part 5 of 12 · ~4 min

Methods of Solving: Completing the Square Method (Conceptual Derivation)

• Standard form: ax^2 + bx + c = 0 (where a ≠ 0)
• Step 1: Divide by 'a' to make the x^2 coefficient equal to 1
• Step 2: Move the constant term to the right-hand side
• Step 3: Add (half of the x-coefficient)^2 to both sides
• Step 4: Rewrite the left side as a perfect square binomial

The Quadratic Formula: Derivation and direct application

• Standard Form: ax^2 + bx + c = 0 (a ≠ 0)
• Derivation: Using the Completing the Square method
• The Quadratic Formula: x = [-b ± √(b^2 - 4ac)] / 2a
• The 'plus-minus' sign accounts for two possible roots
• Direct Application: Substitute a, b, and c to find x

Part 7 of 12 · ~4 min

Nature of Roots: The Discriminant (D) and its significance

• The Discriminant (D) is defined as: D = b² - 4ac
• The Discriminant determines the 'nature' or type of roots
• D > 0: Two distinct (different) real roots
• D = 0: Two equal (repeated) real roots
• D < 0: No real roots

Part 8 of 12 · ~6 min

Relationship between Discriminant and the types of real roots

• Discriminant (D) = b² - 4ac
• If D > 0: Two distinct real roots
• If D = 0: Two equal real roots
• If D < 0: No real roots
• The nature of roots depends on the value under the square root
