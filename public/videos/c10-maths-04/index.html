Topic 1 · Introduction to Quadratic Equations

Introduction to Quadratic Equations

A quadratic equation is a polynomial equation of the second degree. Think of it like a roller coaster track that curves up and down rather than a straight line.

• Standard form: ax² + bx + c = 0
• The coefficient 'a' must never be zero
• The highest power (degree) is always 2

3x² + 2x - 5 = 0 is quadratic; 2x + 3 = 0 is not.

Topic 2 · Solution of a Quadratic Equation

Solution of a Quadratic Equation

The solutions, also called roots, are the values of x that make the equation true. It is like finding the exact keys that unlock a specific door.

• A quadratic equation can have at most two real roots
• Roots are the x-intercepts on a graph
• Finding roots is called solving the equation

For x² - 9 = 0, roots are x = 3 and x = -3.

Topic 3 · Relationship between Coefficients and Roots

Relationship between Coefficients and Roots

There is a hidden mathematical link between the numbers in the equation and its roots. It's like knowing a recipe's ingredients tells you exactly how the cake will taste.

• Sum of roots (α + β) = -b/a
• Product of roots (αβ) = c/a
• Use these to form an equation: x² - (sum)x + product = 0

If roots are 2 and 3, equation is x² - 5x + 6 = 0.

Topic 4 · Nature of Roots (Discriminant)

Nature of Roots (Discriminant)

The Discriminant (D) tells us what kind of roots we will get without actually solving the equation. It acts like a weather forecast for your math problem.

• D = b² - 4ac
• D > 0: Two distinct real roots
• D = 0: Two equal real roots

In x² + x + 1 = 0, D = 1² - 4(1)(1) = -3. No real roots.
