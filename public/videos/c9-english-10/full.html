Literary Devices: Imagery, Simile, and Personification in the Text

• Imagery: Using sensory details (sight, sound, smell) to create mental pictures.
• Simile: Comparing two unlike things using 'like' or 'as' to clarify descriptions.
• Personification: Giving human traits to non-human objects to bring settings to life.
• Purpose: These devices transform a simple report into an immersive experience.
• Exam Tip: Use these terms to explain 'how' the author creates atmosphere.

Common Pitfalls: Avoiding Errors in Character and Setting Analysis

• Avoid Generalization: Use specific sensory adjectives instead of vague terms.
• The Narrator's Role: View Seth as an observant philosopher, not just a tourist.
• Connect Setting to Mood: Link the atmosphere of locations to the narrative theme.
• Avoid Binary Thinking: Don't label settings as 'good' or 'bad'; see them as contrasts.
• The Evidence Formula: Always support your claims with specific textual details.
