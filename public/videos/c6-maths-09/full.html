Tally Marks: The Art of Systematic Counting and Frequency Tables

• Tally marks use vertical lines to record data quickly.
• The fifth item is marked with a diagonal slash to create a bundle of five.
• A Frequency Table organizes data into Items, Tally Marks, and Frequency.
• Frequency represents the total count of a specific observation.
• Grouping by fives makes large data sets easy to read and sum up.

Pictographs: Representing Data through Visual Symbols and Scales

• Pictographs use pictures or symbols to represent data visually.
• The 'Key' or 'Scale' defines the value of each symbol.
• Scales prevent drawings from becoming too large or messy.
• Partial symbols are used to represent fractions of the scale.
• Always write the Key clearly at the bottom of your pictograph.

Introduction to Bar Graphs: Understanding Bars, Axes, and Scales

• Bar graphs use rectangular bars of equal width to represent data
• The horizontal line is the x-axis and the vertical line is the y-axis
• Bars must have equal width and consistent gaps between them
• The Scale determines the value represented by each unit on the axis
• Always label both axes to provide context to your data

A 3D coordinate system emerges from a blank page, with colorful bars rising from the x-axis to meet specific values on the y-axis.

Part 7 of 12 · ~4 min

Constructing Bar Graphs: Step-by-Step Drawing and Labeling

• Draw perpendicular lines: the horizontal x-axis and vertical y-axis.
• Label axes clearly: define what the x and y axes represent.
• Select a uniform scale: ensure intervals are equal and consistent.
• Draw bars: use equal width for bars and equal gaps between them.
• Add a title: always give your graph a descriptive heading.

Part 8 of 12 · ~4 min

Interpreting Bar Graphs: Reading Data and Answering Questions

• Locate the category on the x-axis and trace to the top of the bar.
• Move horizontally to the y-axis to read the exact value.
• Use the scale intervals to estimate values between marked numbers.
• Identify 'Maximum' as the tallest bar and 'Minimum' as the shortest.
• Perform addition for totals and subtraction for differences.

• Pictographs: Always check the 'Key' before drawing symbols.
