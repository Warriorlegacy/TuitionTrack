Formal Definition of Continuity: Left-Hand Limit, Right-Hand Limit, and Continuity at a Point

• Left-Hand Limit: Approach 'a' from values smaller than 'a'
• Right-Hand Limit: Approach 'a' from values larger than 'a'
• Condition 1: The function value f(a) must be defined
• Condition 2: The limit must exist (LHL = RHL)
• Condition 3: The limit must equal the function value: lim f(x) = f(a)

Part 3 of 12 · ~5 min

Continuity in Intervals and Types of Discontinuity

• Continuity in Open Intervals: Continuous at every point within (a, b)
• Closed Interval Conditions: Continuous on (a, b) plus left/right limits at endpoints
• Removable Discontinuity: Limit exists, but does not equal the function value
• Jump Discontinuity: Left-hand limit and right-hand limit are finite but unequal
• Infinite Discontinuity: Function approaches infinity at the point of discontinuity

Part 4 of 12 · ~5 min

Algebra of Continuous Functions and Continuity of Composite Functions

• Sum and difference of continuous functions are continuous.
• Product of continuous functions is continuous.
• Quotient is continuous provided the denominator is non-zero.
• Composite function f(g(x)) is continuous if f and g satisfy specific conditions.
• Continuity of f(g(x)) requires g to be continuous at 'c' and f to be continuous at g(c).

Part 5 of 12 · ~5 min

Differentiability: Geometric Interpretation, Tangents, and the Limit Definition

• Differentiability means the graph is smooth and has no sharp corners.
• Geometric meaning: The derivative is the slope of the tangent line.
• Limit Definition: f prime of x equals the limit as h approaches zero of [f(x+h)-f(x)] / h.
• A function is differentiable only if Left-Hand Derivative equals Right-Hand Derivative.
• Continuity is a necessary but not sufficient condition for differentiability.

Part 6 of 12 · ~5 min

Relationship between Continuity and Differentiability: The 'Differentiable implies Continuous' Theorem

• Theorem: Differentiability at a point implies continuity at that point.
• Mathematical Goal: Prove that the limit of f(x) as x approaches c equals f(c).
• The Proof: Use the identity f(x) - f(c) = [(f(x) - f(c))/(x - c)] * (x - c).
• Crucial Result: The limit of the difference quotient multiplied by zero equals zero.
• The Converse: Continuity does NOT guarantee differentiability (e.g., sharp corners).

Part 7 of 12 · ~5 min

Rules of Differentiation: Power Rule, Product Rule, Quotient Rule, and Constant Multiple Rule

• Constant Multiple Rule: d/dx [c * f(x)] = c * f'(x)
• Power Rule: d/dx [x^n] = n * x^(n-1)
• Product Rule: d/dx [u * v] = u * dv/dx + v * du/dx
• Quotient Rule: d/dx [u/v] = (v * du/dx - u * dv/dx) / v^2
• Always apply the rules step-by-step to avoid algebraic errors

The Chain Rule: Differentiating Composite Functions and Step-by-Step Derivations

• Definition of Composite Functions: f of g of x
• The Chain Rule Formula: dy/dx = dy/du * du/dx
• Step 1: Differentiate the outer function
• Step 2: Multiply by the derivative of the inner function
• The 'Peeling the Onion' method for multiple layers

Implicit Differentiation and Logarithmic Differentiation: Techniques and Complex Forms

• Implicit Functions: When y is not isolated on one side of the equation.
• Implicit Differentiation: Differentiate both sides and solve for dy/dx.
• Logarithmic Differentiation: Use logs to handle variable-to-variable powers.
• Log Power Rule: log of a raised to b equals b times log of a.
• Strategy: Simplify using log laws before applying Product and Chain rules.

Part 10 of 12 · ~5 min

Parametric Forms and Second-Order Derivatives: Higher-Order Calculus

• Parametric form: x and y are expressed as functions of a parameter t
• Formula for parametric differentiation: dy/dx = (dy/dt) / (dx/dt)
• Second-order derivative represents the rate of change of the slope
• Second derivative in parametric form: d2y/dx2 = [d/dt(dy/dx)] / (dx/dt)
• Physical significance: First derivative is velocity, second is acceleration

Part 11 of 12 · ~5 min

Mean Value Theorems: Rolle’s Theorem and Lagrange’s Mean Value Theorem (LMVT)

• Rolle's Theorem: Requires continuity, differentiability, and f(a) = f(b)
• Rolle's Result: There exists c in (a, b) such that f'(c) = 0
• LMVT: Requires continuity on [a, b] and differentiability on (a, b)
• LMVT Formula: f'(c) = [f(b) - f(a)] / (b - a)
• Geometric Meaning: Tangent is parallel to the secant line connecting endpoints
