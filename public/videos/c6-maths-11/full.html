Understanding Variables: From Numbers to Letters and Symbols

• Variable: A symbol that can take different values (e.g., x, y, n).
• Constant: A value that remains fixed and never changes (e.g., 5, 10, -2).
• The term 'Variable' comes from 'Vary', meaning to change.
• Letters are used as shorthand for unknown numbers or measurements.
• Variables act as placeholders or 'empty containers' for numbers.

Introduction to Equations: The Concept of Equality and Balance

• An equation is a mathematical sentence stating two expressions are equal.
• The equals sign (=) acts as the fulcrum of a balanced scale.
• Equality means the value on the left side is identical to the right side.
• To maintain balance, any operation performed on one side must be done to the other.
• Solving an equation means finding the value of the variable that keeps it balanced.

Solving Simple Equations: The Principle of Inverse Operations

• An equation is a balanced scale where both sides must remain equal.
• Solving an equation means isolating the variable on one side.
• Inverse operations 'undo' mathematical actions to find the unknown.
• Addition and Subtraction are inverse operations of each other.
• Multiplication and Division are inverse operations of each other.

Common Pitfalls: Avoiding Mistakes in Signs, Variables, and Operations

• The Sign Trap: A minus outside a bracket flips all signs inside.
• Like Terms Only: You cannot add constants directly to variables.
• The Invisible One: Remember that x is actually 1 times x.
• The See-Saw Rule: Whatever you do to one side, do to the other.
• Distributive Property: Multiply the outer term by every term inside.
