Fundamental Rights in the Indian Constitution: An Overview

• Fundamental Rights are essential for individual dignity and development.
• Guaranteed by the Constitution and protected by the Judiciary.
• Six core rights: Equality, Freedom, Against Exploitation, Religion, Culture, and Remedies.
• Rights are not absolute; they are subject to reasonable restrictions.
• Right to Constitutional Remedies is the 'heart and soul' of the Constitution.

Right to Equality: Articles 14 to 18 and Real-world Applications

• Article 14: Equality before law and equal protection of laws.
• Article 15: Prohibition of discrimination on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex, or birth.
• Article 16: Equality of opportunity in public employment.
• Article 17: Abolition of untouchability and its practice.
• Article 18: Abolition of titles to prevent social hierarchies.

Right to Freedom: Articles 19 to 22 and Protections against State Power

• Article 19: Six fundamental freedoms including speech, assembly, and movement.
• Reasonable Restrictions: Liberty is subject to national security and public order.
• Article 20: Protection against arbitrary conviction and double jeopardy.
• Article 21 & 21-A: Right to life, dignity, and compulsory education.

Right against Exploitation and Right to Freedom of Religion

• Article 25: Freedom to profess, practice, and propagate any religion.

Protecting Rights: The Role of the Judiciary and Writ Jurisdiction

• Judiciary acts as the independent guardian of Fundamental Rights.
• Citizens can approach Supreme Court or High Courts directly for rights violations.
• Writ Jurisdiction: Special powers of courts to issue formal orders.
• Habeas Corpus: Protects against unlawful detention of a person.
• Mandamus: Commands public officials to perform their legal duties.
