The Old Regime: Social Hierarchy, The Three Estates, and Economic Inequality

• The Old Regime: A rigid social hierarchy based on birth.
• First Estate: The Clergy; owned land and paid no taxes.
• Second Estate: The Nobility; held high offices and tax exemptions.
• Third Estate: 98% of the population; paid all state taxes.
• Systemic Inequality: Wealth concentrated in the top two estates.

The Struggle to Survive: Subsistence Crisis and the Rise of the Middle Class

• Population growth: 23 million in 1715 to 28 million in 1789
• Subsistence Crisis: Rapid demand vs. low food production
• Impact of bad harvests: Droughts and winters leading to high bread prices
• Rise of the Middle Class: Educated merchants, lawyers, and officials
• Philosophical shift: Demand for merit-based rights over birth privileges

Part 4 of 12 · ~5 min

The Outbreak of Revolution: Estates General, National Assembly, and the Tennis Court Oath

• May 5, 1789: Louis XVI calls the Estates General to approve new taxes.
• Voting Dispute: Third Estate demands 'one member, one vote' instead of 'one estate, one vote'.
• June 20, 1789: The Tennis Court Oath—Third Estate declares itself the National Assembly.
• July 14, 1789: The Storming of the Bastille marks the start of violent revolution.
• Shift in Power: Authority moves from the Absolute Monarchy to the National Assembly.

Part 5 of 12 · ~5 min

France Becomes a Constitutional Monarchy: The Declaration of the Rights of Man and Citizen

• The National Assembly drafted a constitution to limit the King's absolute power.
• France transitioned from an Absolute Monarchy to a Constitutional Monarchy.
• The Declaration of Rights established natural, inalienable rights for citizens.
• Distinction between Active Citizens (voters) and Passive Citizens (non-voters).
• Legislative and Executive powers were separated to prevent tyranny.

The Radical Phase: The Jacobins, The Reign of Terror, and Maximilien Robespierre

• Rise of the Jacobin Club led by Maximilien Robespierre
• Sans-culottes: The radical supporters of the revolution
• 1792: Abolition of Monarchy and declaration of France as a Republic
• The Reign of Terror (1793-1794): Policy of severe control and punishment
• The Guillotine: Execution method for perceived enemies of the state

The Directory and the Rise of Napoleon Bonaparte: From Chaos to Empire

• The Directory: Five members and two legislative councils to prevent dictatorship.
• Political Instability: Constant clashes between the Directory and legislative councils.
• Rise of Napoleon: A military general who seized power during political chaos.
• The Napoleonic Code: Reforms in law, property rights, and administrative efficiency.
• From Republic to Empire: Napoleon's coronation as Emperor in 1804.

A 3D timeline animation showing the transition from the chaotic Directory chambers to the grand, imperial coronation of Napoleon Bonaparte.

Part 8 of 12 · ~5 min

Women and the Revolution: Their Role, Struggles, and Legal Rights

• Women were active participants in revolutionary struggles from the start.
• Most women worked as seamstresses, laundresses, or street vendors.
• Lack of access to education and professional training was a major issue.
• The Society of Revolutionary and Republican Women was a key political club.
• Demands included the right to vote, hold office, and legal marriage rights.

The Abolition of Slavery: Colonialism, Trade, and the Revolutionary Impact

• The role of Caribbean colonies: Martinique, Guadeloupe, and San Domingo.
• The Triangular Trade: Europe, Africa, and the Americas.
• The contradiction between 'Rights of Man' and the institution of slavery.
• The 1794 Convention: The first major abolition of slavery.
• Napoleon's reversal in 1802 and the final abolition in 1848.

Common Pitfalls: Chronology Errors, Concept Confusion, and Terminology Mistakes

• Chronology: Estates General -> National Assembly -> Convention -> Directory
• Concept: Constitutional Monarchy (Limited King) vs. Republic (No King)
• Concept: Estates General (Old Body) vs. National Assembly (New Body)
• Terminology: Distinguish between Liberty, Equality, and Fraternity
• Terminology: Use 'Reign of Terror' instead of just 'The Terror'
