Introduction: The Flow of Charge, Electric Current, and Real-World Applications

• Electrostatics deals with stationary charges; Current Electricity deals with moving charges.
• Electric Current (I) is the rate of flow of charge: I = Q / t.
• The SI unit of electric current is the Ampere (A).
• Conventional current flows from positive to negative terminal.
• Electrons flow from negative to positive, but current direction is opposite.

Electric Current, Drift Velocity, and the Microscopic Model of Conduction

• Random thermal motion of electrons results in zero net velocity.
• Electric field E exerts force F = eE on free electrons.
• Drift velocity v-sub-d is the average velocity due to an electric field.
• Relaxation time tau is the average time between electron collisions.
• Fundamental relation: I = n * e * A * v-sub-d

Ohm's Law, Resistance, Resistivity, and Temperature Dependence

• Ohm's Law: V = I × R (at constant temperature)
• Resistance (R) depends on length, area, and material
• Resistivity (ρ) is an intrinsic property of the material
• Formula: R = ρL / A
• Temperature effect: R increases with T for metals (positive alpha)

Drift Velocity Derivation, Current Density, and Mobility

• Drift Velocity: v subscript d equals e E tau divided by m
• Relation between Current and Drift Velocity: I equals n A e v subscript d
• Current Density: J equals I divided by A or J equals n e v subscript d
• Mobility: mu equals v subscript d divided by E or mu equals e tau divided by m
• Drift velocity is the average velocity gained by electrons due to an electric field

Part 5 of 12 · ~5 min

Electrical Energy, Power, and Internal Resistance of a Cell

• Electrical Power P equals I squared times R or V squared divided by R
• E-M-F is the maximum potential difference when no current flows
• Terminal Voltage V is the actual voltage when current is flowing
• Discharging formula: E-M-F equals V plus I times r
• Internal resistance r equals (E-M-F minus V) divided by I

Part 6 of 12 · ~5 min

Electromotive Force (EMF), Terminal Voltage, and Cell Combinations (Series & Parallel)

• EMF is the energy per unit charge provided by the cell.
• Terminal Voltage V = E - Ir (during discharge).
• Series Cells: E_total = E1 + E2; r_total = r1 + r2.
• Parallel Cells: E_total = E; r_total = r/n (for identical cells).
• Series increases voltage; Parallel increases current capacity.

Kirchhoff's Laws: Junction Rule and Loop Rule Applications

• Junction Rule: Sum of currents at a junction is zero (Charge Conservation)
• Loop Rule: Sum of potential changes in a closed loop is zero (Energy Conservation)
• Sign Convention: -I*R when moving with current; +E when moving negative to positive
• Step-by-step: Assign currents, apply Junction Rule, then apply Loop Rule
• Ensure equations are linearly independent for a unique solution

Wheatstone Bridge Principle and Meter Bridge Experimental Setup

• Wheatstone Bridge Condition: P/Q = R/S for zero galvanometer current
• Meter Bridge uses a uniform 1-meter wire of constantan or manganin
• Null Point: The position where galvanometer deflection is zero
• Formula: Unknown Resistance S = R * (1 - l) / l
• Accuracy depends on uniform wire cross-section and tight connections

Part 9 of 12 · ~5 min

Potentiometer: Principle, Comparison of EMF, and Internal Resistance Determination

• Potentiometer: An ideal voltmeter that draws no current at the null point.
• Principle: Potential drop V is proportional to length l (V = kl).
• Potential Gradient (k): The potential drop per unit length of the wire.
• Comparison of EMF: E1/E2 = l1/l2.
• Internal Resistance: r = (l1 - l2) / l2.

Part 10 of 12 · ~5 min

Complex Circuit Analysis: Mastery of Nodal and Mesh Methods

• Mesh Analysis: Uses Kirchhoff's Loop Rule to find loop currents.
• Shared Resistors: Use the difference between mesh currents (I1 - I2).
• Nodal Analysis: Uses Kirchhoff's Junction Rule to find node voltages.
• Reference Node: Always assign one node as zero volts (Ground).
• Current Calculation: I = (V_start - V_end) / R

• Maintain sign consistency in Kirchhoff's Loop Rule
