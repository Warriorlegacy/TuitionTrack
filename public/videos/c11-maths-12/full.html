Intuitive Understanding of Limits: Numerical and Graphical Approaches

• Limits focus on the behavior near a point, not at the point itself.
• Numerical Approach: Observe values as x approaches the target from both sides.
• Graphical Approach: Check if the path from the left and right meet at the same height.
• A limit exists only if the left-hand approach equals the right-hand approach.
• Distinguish between the function's value f(x) and its limit.

Formal Definition of Limits: Left-Hand and Right-Hand Limits

• Left-Hand Limit: Approaching 'a' from values smaller than 'a'
• Right-Hand Limit: Approaching 'a' from values larger than 'a'
• Existence Condition: Left-Hand Limit must equal Right-Hand Limit
• If L is not equal to M, the limit at 'a' does not exist
• Limits describe behavior 'near' a point, not necessarily 'at' the point

Algebraic Methods for Evaluating Limits: Factorization and Rationalization

• Direct substitution can lead to the indeterminate form 0/0.
• Factorization: Use algebraic identities to cancel common terms.
• Rationalization: Multiply numerator and denominator by the conjugate.
• The goal is to eliminate the term causing the zero in the denominator.
• Always re-evaluate the limit using direct substitution after simplifying.

Limits of Trigonometric Functions: Standard Formulas and Derivations

• Standard Limit: limit as x approaches 0 of sin(x)/x = 1
• Standard Limit: limit as x approaches 0 of tan(x)/x = 1
• Technique: Match the denominator to the function argument
• Important Identity: limit as x approaches 0 of (1 - cos(x))/x^2 = 1/2
• Continuous Function: limit as x approaches 0 of cos(x) = 1

Part 6 of 12 · ~5 min

Limits at Infinity and Sandwich Theorem Applications

• Limits at infinity describe the end behavior of a function as x grows without bound.
• Divide by the highest power of x to evaluate limits of rational functions.
• Sandwich Theorem: If f(x) ≤ g(x) ≤ h(x) and limits of f and h are equal, then g's limit is the same.
• The Squeeze Theorem is ideal for functions involving oscillating sine or cosine terms.
• End behavior helps identify horizontal asymptotes in a graph.

Introduction to Derivatives: The Concept of Instantaneous Rate of Change

• Average rate of change is the total change divided by the interval.
• Instantaneous rate of change is the rate at a specific moment.
• The derivative bridges the gap between average and instantaneous rates.
• The derivative is mathematically defined as the limit of the difference quotient.
• Geometrically, the derivative represents the slope of the tangent line.

The First Principle of Derivatives: Formal Derivation and Application

• Definition: Derivative is the limit of the difference quotient as h approaches zero
• Formula: f prime of x equals the limit as h approaches zero of [f(x+h) - f(x)] / h
• Geometric meaning: The slope of the tangent line at a specific point x
• Step 1: Substitute the function into the formal limit definition
• Step 2: Simplify the numerator and cancel the h term to evaluate the limit

Part 9 of 12 · ~5 min

Rules of Differentiation: Power Rule, Constant Rule, and Sum/Difference Rules

• Constant Rule: The derivative of a constant value is always zero.
• Power Rule: d/dx of x^n is n times x to the power of n minus one.
• Coefficient Rule: Multiply the exponent by the existing coefficient.
• Sum/Difference Rule: Differentiate each term of a polynomial individually.
• Fractional/Negative Exponents: Apply the Power Rule as usual.

Part 10 of 12 · ~4 min

Advanced Differentiation: Product Rule and Quotient Rule

• Product Rule: (u * v)' = u * (dv/dx) + v * (du/dx)
• Quotient Rule: (u/v)' = [v * (du/dx) - u * (dv/dx)] / v^2
• Product Rule handles functions multiplied together
• Quotient Rule handles functions divided by each other
• Watch the subtraction sign in the Quotient Rule formula

Higher-Order Derivatives and Tangent/Normal Applications

• Higher-order derivatives: Differentiating a derivative to find acceleration and beyond.
• The second derivative is denoted as d squared y dx squared.
• The slope of the tangent at a point is the value of the first derivative at that point.
• Equation of Tangent: y minus y1 equals m times open parenthesis x minus x1 close parenthesis.
• Equation of Normal: y minus y1 equals negative one divided by m times open parenthesis x minus x1 close parenthesis.

Exam Masterclass: Common Mistakes, Word Problems, and Full Chapter Recap

• Check if Left-Hand Limit equals Right-Hand Limit before concluding.
• Never stop at indeterminate forms like zero over zero; simplify first.
• Product Rule: u dv/dx plus v du/dx. Quotient Rule: requires subtraction.
• Word Problems: Identify variables and look for 'instantaneous rate of change'.
• Recap: Limits approach values; Derivatives measure instantaneous change.
