Introduction: Why Rotational Motion Matters, Center of Mass Concept, and Particle Systems

• Transition from single particle motion to extended systems of particles.
• Definition of Center of Mass: The average position of all mass in a system.
• The Center of Mass follows a simple path even if the object rotates.
• Discrete Systems: Distinct particles like a collection of spheres.
• Formula: X-bar = Sum of (m sub i times x sub i) divided by Total Mass M.

Mathematical Derivations of Center of Mass for Discrete and Continuous Systems

• Discrete System: R = (1/M) * sum of (m_i * r_i)
• Continuous System: R = (1/M) * integral of (r * dm)
• One-dimensional X-coordinate: x_bar = (1/M) * sum of (m_i * x_i)
• Mass element dm for uniform rod: dm = (M/L) * dx
• Center of mass of a uniform rod is at L/2

Motion of the Center of Mass: Velocity, Acceleration, and Internal vs External Forces

• Velocity of CM is the mass-weighted average of all particle velocities.
• Acceleration of CM depends solely on the net external force.
• Internal forces occur in equal and opposite pairs and cancel out.
• Net External Force equals Total Mass times Acceleration of CM.
• If net external force is zero, CM velocity remains constant.

Linear Momentum vs Angular Momentum: The Bridge between Translational and Rotational Motion

• Linear Momentum (P) = mass (m) × velocity (v)
• Total Linear Momentum = Total Mass × Velocity of Center of Mass
• Angular Momentum (L) = position vector (r) cross linear momentum (p)
• Magnitude of L = r × m × v × sin(theta)
• The Bridge: Rate of change of L equals the applied Torque

Torque (Moment of Force): Vector Definition, Mathematical Derivation, and Relation to Angular Acceleration

• Torque (Moment of Force) is the turning effect of a force about an axis.
• Vector Definition: Tau equals r cross F.
• Magnitude: Tau equals r F sin theta.
• Direction: Determined by the Right-Hand Rule.
• Rotational Dynamics: Tau equals I alpha (Moment of Inertia times Angular Acceleration).

Equilibrium of Rigid Bodies: Translational and Rotational Equilibrium Conditions

• Translational Equilibrium: Net external force equals zero.
• Rotational Equilibrium: Net external torque equals zero.
• Static Equilibrium requires both conditions to be met.
• Force balance prevents linear acceleration.
• Torque balance prevents angular acceleration.

Part 7 of 12 · ~5 min

Moment of Inertia: Concept, Radius of Gyration, and Parallel/Perpendicular Axis Theorems

• Moment of Inertia (I) depends on mass distribution relative to the axis.
• Formula for discrete particles: I = sum of m times r-squared.
• Radius of Gyration (k): k = square root of (I / M).
• Parallel Axis Theorem: I = I-com + M-d-squared.
• Perpendicular Axis Theorem for planar objects: I-z = I-x + I-y.

Calculating Moment of Inertia for Standard Geometric Shapes: Derivations and Applications

• Thin Rod (Center): I = (1/12) M L squared
• Thin Ring (Central Axis): I = M R squared
• Solid Disc (Central Axis): I = (1/2) M R squared
• Solid Sphere: I = (2/5) M R squared
• Hollow Spherical Shell: I = (2/3) M R squared

Kinematics of Rotational Motion: Angular Displacement, Velocity, Acceleration, and Analogy

• Angular Displacement (theta): The angle swept by a particle in radians.
• Angular Velocity (omega): The rate of change of angular displacement.
• Angular Acceleration (alpha): The rate of change of angular velocity.
• The Bridge: Linear velocity v = R * omega and Linear acceleration a = R * alpha.
• Kinematic Analogies: Rotational equations mirror linear equations exactly.

Dynamics of Rotational Motion: Newton's Second Law for Rotation and Rotational Kinetic Energy

• Newton's Second Law for Rotation: Torque = Moment of Inertia × Angular Acceleration
• Moment of Inertia (I) is the rotational analogue of mass
• Torque (Tau) is the rotational analogue of Force
• Rotational Kinetic Energy formula: K = one-half I omega squared
• Rotational energy depends on mass distribution and angular velocity

Rolling Motion: Combined Translational and Rotational Kinetic Energy and Rolling on Incline

• Rolling without slipping condition: v = omega * R
• Total Kinetic Energy: K = 1/2 m v^2 + 1/2 I omega^2
• Simplified Energy: K = 1/2 m v^2 (1 + I / m R^2)
• Acceleration on incline: a = (g sin theta) / (1 + I / m R^2)
• Rolling acceleration is always less than sliding acceleration
