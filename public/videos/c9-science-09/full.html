Introduction: The Universal Force of Attraction and Real-world Significance

• Gravity is the invisible force of attraction between all objects with mass.
• It is a universal phenomenon affecting everything from pebbles to planets.
• Gravity keeps the Moon in orbit and the Earth orbiting the Sun.
• It is responsible for Earth's atmosphere and the rising and falling of tides.
• Gravity acts as the fundamental 'glue' that holds the universe together.

Universal Law of Gravitation: Concept, Mathematical Derivation, and Importance

• Force is proportional to the product of the masses of the two objects.
• Force is inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them.
• Mathematical Formula: F = G * (m1 * m2) / r^2
• The Inverse-Square Law: Increasing distance significantly weakens the force.
• Universal Application: Governs everything from falling apples to planetary orbits.

The Gravitational Constant (G): Value, Units, and Dimensional Analysis

• G is the Universal Gravitational Constant; its value is constant everywhere.
• Value of G: 6.67 x 10^-11 N m^2 / kg^2.
• The tiny value of G explains why we don't feel gravity between small objects.
• SI Unit of G: Newton meter squared per kilogram squared (N m^2 kg^-2).
• Dimensional Formula of G: [M^-1 L^0 T^-2].

Free Fall and Acceleration due to Gravity (g): Concept and Derivation

• Free Fall: Motion under the sole influence of gravity.
• Acceleration due to gravity (g) is the change in velocity per unit time.
• g is not constant; it varies with location and planetary mass.
• Derivation: g = (G * M) / r^2
• The value of 'g' is independent of the mass of the falling object.

Difference between Mass and Weight: Definitions, Units, and Key Distinctions

• Mass: The quantity of matter in an object (Constant everywhere)
• Weight: The force of gravitational attraction (Changes with location)
• SI Units: Mass is measured in Kilograms (kg); Weight in Newtons (N)
• Mathematical Formula: Weight equals mass times acceleration due to gravity (W = m × g)
• Type: Mass is a scalar quantity; Weight is a vector quantity

Weight of an Object on the Moon: Mathematical Relationship and Calculation

• Mass remains constant regardless of location.
• Weight depends on the local acceleration due to gravity (g).
• Moon's gravity is approximately 1/6th of Earth's gravity.
• Formula: Weight on Moon = 1/6 × Weight on Earth.
• Example: 60N on Earth becomes 10N on the Moon.

Thrust and Pressure: Definitions, SI Units, and Conceptual Understanding

• Thrust: The total force acting perpendicularly on a surface (Unit: Newton)
• Pressure: Thrust acting per unit area (Formula: P = Force / Area)
• SI Unit of Pressure: Newton per square meter (N/m²) or Pascal (Pa)
• Inverse Relationship: Smaller area results in higher pressure for the same force
• Applications: Wide straps reduce pressure; sharp edges increase pressure

Buoyancy: The Upward Force and Factors Affecting it

• Buoyancy is the upward force exerted by a fluid on an immersed object.
• Buoyant force acts in the direction opposite to the force of gravity.
• An object floats if Buoyant Force is greater than its Weight.
• Factor 1: Higher fluid density increases the buoyant force.
• Factor 2: Larger submerged volume increases the buoyant force.

Archimedes' Principle: Statement, Experimental Verification, and Applications

• Archimedes' Principle: Upward force equals weight of displaced fluid.
• Experimental proof: Loss in weight = weight of displaced liquid.
• Why ships float: Large volume displaces more water weight.
• Submarine control: Ballast tanks adjust weight for diving and surfacing.
• Applications: Hydrometers for density and Lactometers for milk purity.

Relative Density: Concept, Formula, and Practical Uses

• Relative Density = Density of Substance / Density of Water
• Relative Density is a dimensionless quantity (no units)
• If Relative Density > 1, the object sinks in water
• If Relative Density < 1, the object floats on water
• Practical uses: Mining, food purity testing, and mineralogy

Worked Examples: Mastering Numerical Problems and Common Calculation Pitfalls

• Always convert units to SI: mass to kg, distance to m, and volume to m cubed.
• Mass is constant everywhere; Weight changes based on local gravity (g).
• In Gravitation, remember to square the distance (r squared) in the denominator.
• Buoyant force depends on the density of the fluid and the volume displaced.
• Double-check your powers of ten when working with the Gravitational Constant G.
