Foundations: Definite Integrals as Area and the Fundamental Theorem

• Definite integrals result in a single numerical value, not a function.
• The integral represents the net signed area between the curve and x-axis.
• Area below the x-axis is treated as negative in definite integration.
• Fundamental Theorem: Integral of f(x) from a to b is F(b) minus F(a).
• The antiderivative F(x) is the key to evaluating definite integrals.

The Area Under a Simple Curve: Vertical Strips and Basic Formulas

• Area is calculated by summing infinitely thin vertical strips
• Formula: Area = Integral from a to b of f(x) dx
• Height of the strip is f(x) and width is dx
• If f(x) is below the x-axis, use the absolute value of the integral
• Split integrals at x-intercepts when the curve crosses the x-axis

Part 4 of 12 · ~4 min

Area Between a Curve and a Line: Horizontal vs. Vertical Strips

• Vertical Strips: Use dx, integrate (Upper Function - Lower Function) dx
• Horizontal Strips: Use dy, integrate (Right Function - Left Function) dy
• Vertical Method: Best when functions are y = f(x) and limits are x-values
• Horizontal Method: Best when functions are x = g(y) and limits are y-values
• Strategic Choice: Pick the orientation that avoids splitting the integral

Area Between Two Curves: Identifying Upper and Lower Boundaries

• Area between curves is the integral of (Upper Curve - Lower Curve)
• Step 1: Find intersection points by setting f(x) = g(x)
• Step 2: Use a test point to identify which function is the ceiling
• Always ensure the integrand results in a positive value for area
• Split the integral if curves intersect within the interval

Advanced Area Calculations: Symmetry, Rotation, and Multiple Regions

• Use symmetry to simplify limits: Area = 2 times integral from 0 to a
• Identify all intersection points to define sub-regions
• For multiple regions, sum the integrals of (Upper Curve - Lower Curve)
• Always sketch the graph to identify upper and lower boundaries
• Area under rotation forms the basis for volumes of solids

Step-by-Step Derivation: From Riemann Sums to Integral Area

• Divide interval [a, b] into n sub-intervals of width delta x = (b - a) / n
• Approximate area using n rectangles with height f(x sub i)
• Riemann Sum formula: Summation of f(x sub i) times delta x
• As n approaches infinity, delta x approaches zero
• The limit of the Riemann Sum is defined as the Definite Integral
