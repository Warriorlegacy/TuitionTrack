Introduction: The Importance of Kinematics, Frame of Reference, and Point Particle Concept

• Kinematics: Study of motion without considering its causes
• Motion is relative: Depends on the observer's perspective
• Frame of Reference: A coordinate system used to measure position
• Point Particle: Treating an object as a single point for simplicity
• Scale matters: Size of object must be much smaller than distance traveled

Describing Motion: Position, Path Length vs. Displacement, and Uniform/Non-Uniform Motion

• Position is defined relative to a chosen origin.
• Distance (Path Length) is a scalar; it is the total path covered.
• Displacement is a vector; it is the change in position (x-final minus x-initial).
• Uniform Motion: Equal distances in equal intervals of time.
• Non-Uniform Motion: Unequal distances in equal intervals of time.

Part 3 of 12 · ~5 min

Speed and Velocity: Average vs. Instantaneous Speed and Velocity with Mathematical Definition

• Average Speed = Total Distance / Total Time (Scalar)
• Average Velocity = Displacement / Time Interval (Vector)
• Instantaneous Velocity is the limit of average velocity as delta t approaches zero
• Mathematical Definition: v = dx / dt (Derivative of position)
• Instantaneous Speed is the magnitude of Instantaneous Velocity

Part 4 of 12 · ~5 min

Acceleration: Uniform and Non-Uniform Acceleration, Instantaneous Acceleration, and Graphs

• Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity: a = delta v / delta t
• Uniform Acceleration: Velocity changes by equal amounts in equal time intervals
• Non-Uniform Acceleration: Velocity changes by unequal amounts in equal time intervals
• Instantaneous Acceleration: The derivative of velocity with respect to time (a = dv/dt)
• Graphically: The slope of a Velocity-Time graph represents acceleration

Kinematic Equations for Uniformly Accelerated Motion: Derivations via Calculus and Graphic

• First Equation: v = u + at (Velocity-Time relation)
• Second Equation: s = ut + 1/2 at^2 (Displacement-Time relation)
• Third Equation: v^2 = u^2 + 2as (Velocity-Displacement relation)
• Graphical Method: Uses slope for acceleration and area for displacement
• Calculus Method: Uses integration of dv/dt and ds/dt for derivations

Free Fall and Motion Under Gravity: Acceleration due to Gravity, Sign Conventions, and Velocity

• Free fall: Motion under the sole influence of gravity (ignoring air resistance).
• Acceleration due to gravity (g) is approximately 9.8 m/s² downwards.
• Sign Convention: Upward direction is positive (+), Downward is negative (-).
• In kinematic equations, replace 'a' with '-g' for downward acceleration.
• At the highest point of upward motion, instantaneous velocity is zero, but 'g' is not.

Relative Velocity in One Dimension: Concept, Mathematical Formulation, and Solving Complex

• Motion is relative to the chosen frame of reference
• Formula: Velocity of A relative to B is v-sub-A minus v-sub-B
• Same direction: Subtract the speeds (v-sub-A minus v-sub-B)
• Opposite directions: Add the speeds (v-sub-A plus v-sub-B)
• Always define a positive direction before solving

Graphical Analysis Masterclass: Interpreting x-t, v-t, and a-t Graphs, Slopes, and Areas

• Slope of x-t graph = Instantaneous Velocity
• Slope of v-t graph = Acceleration
• Area under v-t graph = Displacement
• Area under a-t graph = Change in Velocity
• Curved x-t graphs indicate non-uniform motion

Calculus in Kinematics: Using Differentiation and Integration to Solve Non-Uniform Motion

• Differentiation: Finding instantaneous rates of change
• Velocity is the first derivative of position (dx/dt)
• Acceleration is the derivative of velocity (dv/dt)
• Integration: Summing small changes to find total displacement
• The constant of integration represents initial conditions

Common Pitfalls and Problem-Solving Strategies: Avoiding Sign Errors, Unit Conversions, and Mathematical Accuracy

• Define a consistent coordinate system (e.g., Up = Positive) before solving.
• Always convert all given values to SI units (m, s, m/s) before calculation.
• Distinguish clearly between constant velocity and instantaneous acceleration.
• Identify 'rest' as zero velocity and 'dropped' as zero initial velocity.
• Use the 'Knowns and Unknowns' method to select the correct kinematic equation.
