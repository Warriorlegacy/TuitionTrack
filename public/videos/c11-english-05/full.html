Concept 1: The Shift from Anthropocentric to Ecocentric Worldview

• Anthropocentric: A human-centered worldview where nature is a mere resource.
• Ecocentric: An ecology-centered view recognizing the intrinsic value of all life.
• Intrinsic Value: The idea that nature has worth independent of human utility.
• Dominion vs. Stewardship: Moving from ruling nature to caring for it.
• Interconnectedness: Human survival depends on the health of the entire ecosystem.

• The Earth functions as a living organism with integrated systems.
• Fisheries: Essential for protein and aquatic life cycles.
• Forests: The planet's lungs, regulating climate and oxygen.
• Pastures: Grasslands that support the global food chain.
• Nutrient Cycling: The vital role of aerobic and anaerobic processes.

The Concept of Sustainable Development: Definitions and Principles

• Definition: Meeting present needs without compromising future generations.
• The Brundtland Commission: The origin of the sustainable development concept.
• Inter-generational Equity: Treating Earth's resources as a legacy, not a commodity.
• Three Pillars: Economic Viability, Social Equity, and Environmental Protection.
• Carrying Capacity: Living within the limits of the Earth's natural regeneration.

• The Gap: High birth rates vs. low death rates create a population surplus
• Exponential vs. Linear Growth: Why population acceleration is dangerous
• Resource Depletion: Per capita availability shrinks as population rises
• Carrying Capacity: The limit of what the Earth can sustainably support
