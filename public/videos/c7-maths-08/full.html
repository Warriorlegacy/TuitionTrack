Defining Rational Numbers: Formal Definition, Positive vs Negative, and the Role of Zero

• Rational Number: p/q where p and q are integers and q is not zero
• Denominator (q) can never be zero because division by zero is undefined
• Positive Rational: Numerator and Denominator have the same sign
• Negative Rational: Numerator and Denominator have different signs
• Zero is a rational number, but it is neither positive nor negative

Rational Numbers vs Integers and Fractions: Identifying Differences and Similarities

• Every integer is a rational number (e.g., 5 = 5/1)
• Not all rational numbers are integers (e.g., 2/3 is not an integer)
• Rational numbers include both positive and negative values
• Fractions typically represent parts of a whole and are usually positive
• Hierarchy: Integers ⊂ Rational Numbers

Standard Form of Rational Numbers: Simplification and Uniqueness

• Standard Form Condition 1: The denominator must be a positive integer.
• Standard Form Condition 2: Numerator and denominator have no common factor except 1.
• Simplification: Divide both numerator and denominator by their Highest Common Factor (HCF).
• Uniqueness: Every rational number has only one unique standard form.
• Example: 12/18 simplifies to 2/3, which is its standard form.

Rational Numbers between Two Rational Numbers: Density Property & Methods

• Density Property: Infinitely many rational numbers exist between any two numbers.
• Mean Method Formula: (a + b) divided by 2.
• Mean Method: Best for finding a single rational number exactly in the middle.
• Common Denominator Method: Best for finding multiple rational numbers quickly.
• Expansion Technique: Multiply numerator and denominator to increase the gap.

Equivalent Rational Numbers: Finding and Verifying through Multiplication/Division

• Equivalent rational numbers represent the same value or point on a number line.
• Finding equivalents: Multiply numerator and denominator by the same non-zero integer.
• Simplifying equivalents: Divide numerator and denominator by a common factor.
• Verification Method 1: Reduce both numbers to their simplest standard form.
• Verification Method 2: Use Cross-Multiplication (a times d must equal b times c).

Comparison of Rational Numbers: Cross-Multiplication and Common Denominator Methods

• Goal: Determine if one rational number is greater, smaller, or equal to another.
• Common Denominator Method: Convert both to equivalent fractions using L.C.M.
• Cross-Multiplication Method: Compare products of (numerator 1 × denominator 2) and (numerator 2 × denominator 1).
• Comparison Rule: If a times d > c times b, then a/b > c/d.
• Pro-Tip: Always ensure denominators are positive before cross-multiplying.

Operations Part 1: Addition and Subtraction of Rational Numbers (Like and Unlike)

• Like Rational Numbers: Same denominators; add/subtract numerators only.
• Unlike Rational Numbers: Different denominators; require a common denominator.
• Step 1: Find the Least Common Multiple (L.C.M.) of the denominators.
• Step 2: Convert to equivalent rational numbers using the L.C.M.
• Step 3: Perform addition/subtraction and simplify to standard form.

Operations Part 2: Multiplication and Division of Rational Numbers & Reciprocals

• Multiplication: Multiply numerators together and denominators together.
• Sign Rules: Same signs result in positive; different signs result in negative.
• Reciprocal: Flip the numerator and denominator of a rational number.
• Division Rule: Multiply the first number by the reciprocal of the second.
• Important: Zero has no reciprocal; division by zero is undefined.
