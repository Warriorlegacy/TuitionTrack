Nutrition Part I: Autotrophic Nutrition, Photosynthesis, and Stomatal Mechanisms

• Autotrophic Nutrition: Synthesizing food from inorganic substances
• Photosynthesis Equation: Carbon dioxide + Water -> Glucose + Oxygen
• Role of Chlorophyll: Capturing solar energy in chloroplasts
• Two Stages: Light-dependent absorption and light-independent reduction
• Stomatal Mechanism: Guard cells regulate gas exchange and water loss

Nutrition Part II: Heterotrophic Nutrition and the Human Digestive System

• Heterotrophs: Holozoic, Saprophytic, and Parasitic modes
• Amoeba: Nutrition via pseudopodia and food vacuoles
• Mouth: Salivary Amylase breaks starch into sugar
• Stomach: HCl, Pepsin, and Mucus for protein digestion
• Small Intestine: Bile for emulsification and Villi for absorption

Part 4 of 12 · ~3 min

Respiration Part I: Breakdown of Glucose, Aerobic vs. Anaerobic Pathways

• Glucose (6-carbon) breaks down into Pyruvate (3-carbon) in the cytoplasm
• Aerobic Respiration: Uses oxygen; occurs in mitochondria; produces CO2, H2O, and high energy
• Anaerobic Respiration in Yeast: No oxygen; produces Ethanol, CO2, and low energy
• Anaerobic Respiration in Muscles: Low oxygen; produces Lactic Acid and causes cramps
• Energy yield is significantly higher in aerobic pathways than anaerobic pathways

A 3D flowchart showing a glucose molecule splitting into pyruvate, then branching into three glowing pathways: one leading to mitochondria, one to yeast cells, and one to a human muscle fiber.

Respiration Part II: Human Respiratory System and Gas Exchange Mechanics

• Nasal passage: Filters air using hair and mucus
• Trachea: Supported by cartilage rings to prevent collapse
• Alveoli: Tiny air sacs providing massive surface area for exchange
• Diffusion: Gases move from high to low concentration
• Hemoglobin: The respiratory pigment that carries oxygen in blood

A 3D cross-section animation showing air traveling from the trachea into branching bronchioles, ending in a close-up of an alveolus surrounded by red blood cells performing gas exchange.

Part 6 of 12 · ~4 min

Transportation Part I: The Human Heart, Blood Vessels, and Double Circulation

• Heart structure: Four chambers (two atria and two ventricles)
• Double Circulation: Pulmonary and Systemic circuits
• Arteries: Thick-walled vessels carrying blood away from the heart
• Veins: Thin-walled vessels with valves to prevent backflow
• Capillaries: Microscopic vessels for nutrient and gas exchange

A 3D anatomical model of a beating human heart showing the flow of red and blue blood through the four chambers and major vessels.

Part 7 of 12 · ~5 min

Transportation Part II: Lymphatic System and Transport in Plants (Xylem & Phloem)

• Lymphatic system collects interstitial fluid to maintain fluid balance.
• Xylem transports water and minerals unidirectionally from roots to leaves.
• Transpiration pull is the main force driving upward water movement.
• Phloem performs translocation of food (sucrose) using A-T-P energy.
• Translocation in phloem is bidirectional and driven by osmotic pressure.

A split-screen 3D animation: left side shows lymph draining from cells into vessels; right side shows a cross-section of a stem with blue arrows rising in xylem and orange arrows moving in phloem.

Part 8 of 12 · ~5 min

Excretion Part I: Human Excretory System and the Structure of the Nephron

• Excretion: Removal of nitrogenous metabolic wastes like urea.
• Human Excretory System: Kidneys, Ureters, Bladder, and Urethra.
• The Nephron: The microscopic functional unit of the kidney.
• Glomerulus and Bowman's Capsule: The site of initial filtration.
• Selective Reabsorption: Recovery of glucose, salts, and water in the tubule.

A 3D medical animation starting with a wide view of the human abdomen, zooming into a single kidney, then penetrating the tissue to show a single nephron with flowing blood and filtered fluid.

Part 9 of 12 · ~3 min

Excretion Part II: Mechanism of Urine Formation and Excretion in Plants

• Three steps of urine formation: Ultrafiltration, Reabsorption, and Secretion
• Glomerular filtrate contains glucose, salts, and urea, but not proteins
• Selective reabsorption depends on the body's immediate needs
• Plants store waste in vacuoles or shed it through falling leaves
• Resins and gums are common waste products stored in old xylem
