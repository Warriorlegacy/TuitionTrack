• The French Revolution as a catalyst for global social change.
• Shift from absolute monarchy to ideas of liberty and equality.
• The transition of people from 'subjects' to 'citizens'.
• Challenge to the traditional social hierarchy of Europe.
• The foundation for 19th-century political ideologies.

Liberals, Radicals, and Conservatives: Defining Political Ideologies

• Liberals: Wanted a representative government but limited voting rights to property owners.
• Liberals: Opposed universal adult franchise and did not support women's voting rights.
• Radicals: Supported government based on the majority of the population.
• Radicals: Opposed privileges of wealthy landowners and supported women's suffrage.
• Conservatives: Initially resisted change; later accepted slow, gradual evolution.

The Coming of Socialism to Europe: Key Thinkers and Concepts

• Socialists opposed private property as the root of social problems
• Property owners prioritize personal profit over worker welfare
• Robert Owen: Advocated for cooperative communities like New Harmony
• Karl Marx: Proposed a Communist society with social control of property
• Rise of organized labor: SPD in Germany and Labour Party in Britain

The Making of a Revolution: The Russian Empire and Economy

• Tsar Nicholas II ruled as an absolute autocrat with total power.
• 85 percent of the Russian population depended on agriculture.
• Industry was concentrated in specific pockets like St. Petersburg and Moscow.
• Workers faced long hours, low wages, and harsh factory conditions.
• Peasants practiced communal land ownership through the 'mir'.

The 1905 Revolution: Bloody Sunday and the Duma

• 1904: Economic crisis, rising prices, and falling real wages
• Bloody Sunday: Peaceful procession led by Father Gapon attacked
• Massacre at the Winter Palace triggered nationwide strikes
• The Duma: An elected consultative parliament promised by the Tsar
• Tsar Nicholas II manipulated Duma elections to retain absolute power

The First World War and the Crisis of Tsarist Autocracy

• War Alliances: Central Powers vs. Allied Powers
• Massive Military Losses: Over 7 million Russian casualties by 1917
• Economic Collapse: Industry controlled by war ministry and railway breakdown
• Food Crisis: Severe shortages of bread and flour in urban centers
• Political Crisis: Tsar's personal command and the influence of Rasputin

The February Revolution: The Fall of the Monarchy

• February 1917: Severe food shortages and strikes in Petrograd
• International Women's Day: Women workers lead the initial strikes
• The Tsar's mistake: Suspending the Duma on February 25th
• The turning point: Soldiers refuse to fire and join the protesters
• The outcome: Tsar Nicholas II abdicates; Provisional Government forms

What Changed After October? The Civil War and Making a Socialist Society

• Nationalization of banks and industries in November 1917
• Land declared social property; peasants seized noble lands
• The Civil War: Reds (Bolsheviks) vs. Whites and Greens
• Foreign intervention supported the pro-Tsarist 'Whites'
• Introduction of Five-Year Plans and centralized economic planning

Stalinism and Collectivization: The Transformation of the USSR

• Grain shortages led to a debate between controlling kulaks or reforming small farms.
• Stalin introduced Collectivization: merging small farms into large 'Kolkhoz'.
• Peasants resisted by destroying livestock, causing a massive drop in cattle numbers.
• The state used repression, deportations, and executions to enforce the new policy.
• The 1930-1933 famine caused millions of deaths, especially in Ukraine and Volga.
