Natural Numbers vs. Whole Numbers: Definitions and the Role of Zero

• Natural Numbers: Counting numbers starting from 1, 2, 3...
• Whole Numbers: Counting numbers including zero: 0, 1, 2, 3...
• The symbol 'N' represents the set of Natural Numbers.
• The symbol 'W' represents the set of Whole Numbers.
• Every Natural Number is a Whole Number, but zero is not a Natural Number.

The Number Line: Visualizing Whole Numbers and Successors/Predecessors

• The Number Line starts at zero and extends infinitely to the right.
• Successor: The number that comes immediately after (n + 1).
• Predecessor: The number that comes immediately before (n - 1).
• Moving right on the number line increases the value.
• Zero is the smallest whole number and has no predecessor.

Properties of Whole Numbers I: Closure and Commutative Properties

• Closure Property: The result of an operation stays within the set of whole numbers.
• Whole numbers are closed under addition and multiplication.
• Whole numbers are NOT closed under subtraction or division.
• Commutative Property: The order of numbers does not change the result.
• Addition and multiplication are commutative; subtraction and division are not.

Part 6 of 12 · ~4 min

Properties of Whole Numbers II: Associative and Distributive Properties

• Associative Property: Grouping numbers differently doesn't change the sum or product.
• Addition Rule: (a + b) + c = a + (b + c)
• Multiplication Rule: (a * b) * c = a * (b * c)
• Distributive Property: a * (b + c) = (a * b) + (a * c)
• Use Distributive Property to break large multiplications into simpler parts.

Identity Elements: The Role of Zero in Addition and One in Multiplication

• Identity Element: A number that leaves other numbers unchanged during an operation.
• Additive Identity: Zero is the additive identity because a + 0 = a.
• Multiplicative Identity: One is the multiplicative identity because a x 1 = a.
• Adding zero to any whole number results in the original number.
• Multiplying any whole number by one results in the original number.

Operations on Whole Numbers: Addition and Subtraction Logic

• Addition is commutative: order does not change the sum.
• Subtraction is NOT commutative: order matters significantly.
• Subtraction is the inverse operation of addition.
• Adding zero preserves the identity of the whole number.
• Use grouping (Associative Property) to simplify addition.

Operations on Whole Numbers: Multiplication and Division Rules

• Multiplication follows Closure, Commutative, and Associative properties
• Any whole number multiplied by zero is always zero
• Division by zero is undefined in mathematics
• Dividend equals (Divisor multiplied by Quotient) plus Remainder
• The Remainder must always be smaller than the Divisor
