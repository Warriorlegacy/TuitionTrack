Surface Area of Cuboids and Cubes: Definitions, Formulas, Derivations, and Basic Calculations

• Cuboid: A 3D solid with 6 rectangular faces defined by length, breadth, and height.
• Total Surface Area of Cuboid = 2(lw + lh + wh)
• Cube: A special cuboid where all edges are equal (side 'a').
• Total Surface Area of Cube = 6a squared
• Lateral Surface Area: Area of the four side faces only.

Surface Area of Right Circular Cylinders: Concept, Lateral vs. Total Surface Area, and Work

• Right Circular Cylinder: Two circular bases and one curved surface
• Curved Surface Area (CSA) = 2 * pi * r * h
• Total Surface Area (TSA) = 2 * pi * r * (r + h)
• The height 'h' is the perpendicular distance between bases
• Always use square units for area measurements

Surface Area of Right Circular Cones: Geometry, Slant Height Derivation, and Formula Application

• Right Circular Cone: A circular base with an apex directly above the center.
• Key Dimensions: Radius (r), Vertical Height (h), and Slant Height (l).
• Slant Height Derivation: l squared equals r squared plus h squared.
• Curved Surface Area (CSA) = pi times r times l.
• Total Surface Area (TSA) = pi r (r + l).

Surface Area of Spheres and Hemispheres: Understanding Curvature, Formulas, and Diagrammat

• Sphere: A 3D object where all surface points are equidistant from the center.
• Surface Area of a Sphere = 4 * pi * r^2
• Hemisphere: A sphere divided into two equal halves by a central plane.
• Curved Surface Area (CSA) of Hemisphere = 2 * pi * r^2
• Total Surface Area (TSA) of Hemisphere = 3 * pi * r^2

• Volume of a Cuboid = length × breadth × height
• Volume of a Cube = side × side × side (side cubed)
• Volume represents the total 3D space occupied by an object
• Practical Method: Counting unit cubes to find capacity

Volume of Right Circular Cylinders: Concept, Formula Derivation, and Step-by-Step Examples

• Volume is the capacity or space enclosed inside a 3D object.
• Formula Derivation: Area of circular base times perpendicular height.
• Volume of Cylinder = pi times radius squared times height (V = πr²h).
• Always use perpendicular height (h), not slant height.
• Volume units are always expressed in cubic units (e.g., cm³, m³).

• Relationship: Volume of Cone = 1/3 × Volume of Cylinder (with same r and h)
• Formula: Volume (V) = 1/3 × π × r² × h
• Step 1: Identify radius (r) and height (h); convert diameter to radius if needed
• Step 2: Ensure all measurements are in the same units before calculating

Volume of Spheres and Hemispheres: Mathematical Derivation, Formulas, and Worked Examples

• Volume of a Sphere: V = 4/3 * pi * r^3
• Volume of a Hemisphere: V = 2/3 * pi * r^3
• Volume is measured in cubic units (cm³, m³)
• Always divide diameter by 2 to find the radius
• Hemisphere volume is exactly half of a sphere's volume

Complex Word Problems: Combination of Solids, Conversion of Shapes, and Multi-step Reasoning

• Combined Surface Area: Sum only the exposed curved surfaces
• Conversion of Shapes: Volume remains constant during melting/recasting
• Formula for Conversion: Volume of old shape = Total volume of new shapes
• Hollow Objects: Subtract the inner volume from the outer volume
• Critical Step: Always unify units before starting calculations
