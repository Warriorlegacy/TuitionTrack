Magnetic Field Lines: Properties, Visualizing Fields, and Field Patterns

• Magnetic field lines form continuous, closed loops.
• Lines emerge from North and enter South outside the magnet.
• Field lines never intersect each other.
• Closer lines indicate a stronger magnetic field.
• Solenoid field lines are parallel and uniform inside the coil.

Magnetic Field due to a Current-Carrying Straight Conductor (Right-Hand Thumb Rule)

• Current-carrying wires generate magnetic fields.
• Field lines form concentric circles around the conductor.
• Field strength decreases as distance from the wire increases.
• Right-Hand Thumb Rule: Thumb = Current, Fingers = Field direction.
• Direction of field depends on the direction of current flow.

Magnetic Field of Circular Loops and Solenoids: Patterns and Comparisons

• Circular loop: Field lines bend and become straight at the center.
• Solenoid: A long coil of wire creating a uniform internal magnetic field.
• Inside a solenoid, field lines are parallel and densely packed.
• Solenoid polarity: Ends act as North and South poles based on current.
• Electromagnets: Using an iron core inside a solenoid to boost field strength.

Part 7 of 12 · ~5 min

Electromagnetic Induction (EMI): Concept, Faraday’s Law, and Lenz’s Law

• Electromagnetic Induction: Generating electricity via changing magnetic fields.
• Faraday's Law: Induced E-M-F is proportional to the rate of change of magnetic flux.
• Magnetic Flux: The measure of total magnetic field passing through a loop.
• Lenz's Law: Induced current always opposes the change that created it.
• Conservation of Energy: Work done against magnetic opposition becomes electrical energy.
