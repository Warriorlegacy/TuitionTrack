Topic 1 · Discovery of Magnets

Discovery of Magnets

Magnets were discovered by a shepherd named Magnes when his iron-tipped stick stuck to a rock. It is like a hidden superpower that certain rocks possess to pull metal objects.

• Magnetite is a natural magnet.
• Magnets attract specific metals.
• Magnetic materials include iron, nickel, and cobalt.

A magnet will pick up a steel nail but will not pick up an eraser.

Topic 2 · Magnetic and Non-magnetic Materials

Magnetic and Non-magnetic Materials

Materials are divided into two groups based on their reaction to a magnet. Think of it like a VIP club where only certain metals are allowed to enter the magnetic field.

• Magnetic: Iron, Nickel, Cobalt.
• Non-magnetic: Wood, Plastic, Glass, Aluminum.
• Testing is done by bringing a magnet close to the object.

Testing a copper coin: It is non-magnetic and will not move.

Topic 3 · Poles of a Magnet

Poles of a Magnet

Every magnet has two ends called poles where the magnetic strength is strongest. It is like the two ends of a battery where the energy is most concentrated.

• Two poles: North (N) and South (S).
• Poles always exist in pairs; you cannot have a single pole.
• Magnetic strength is maximum at the poles.

If you break a magnet in half, you get two smaller magnets, each with N and S poles.

Topic 4 · Finding Directions using a Magnet

Finding Directions using a Magnet

A freely suspended magnet always comes to rest in the North-South direction. It acts like a natural compass that helps travelers find their way.

• A suspended magnet aligns with Earth's magnetic field.
• The North pole of the magnet points toward the Earth's North.
• A magnetic compass uses a tiny magnetized needle for this.

A sailor uses a compass to find North when lost at sea.

Topic 5 · Attraction and Repulsion

Attraction and Repulsion

Magnets interact based on their poles. Like poles push away, while different poles pull together. It is like two people either hugging or running away from each other.

• Like poles (N-N or S-S) repel.
• Unlike poles (N-S) attract.
• Repulsion is the surest test for magnetism.

Bringing the North pole of Magnet A to the North pole of Magnet B causes them to push apart.
