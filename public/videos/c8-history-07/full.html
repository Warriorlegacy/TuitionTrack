• Raja Rammohan Roy: Known as the 'Father of Modern India'
• Core Argument: Social evils lacked true basis in ancient scriptures
• Major Achievement: The banning of Sati in 1829
• Brahmo Samaj (1828): Advocated monotheism and social equality
• Goal: Blending traditional values with modern scientific reasoning

• The Varna system: A rigid, hereditary social hierarchy
• Four main Varnas: Brahmins, Kshatriyas, Vaishyas, and Shudras
• The 'Untouchables': Those excluded from the four-fold Varna system
• Purity and Pollution: The religious basis for social exclusion

Dr. B.R. Ambedkar and the Fight Against Untouchability

• Dr. B.R. Ambedkar: Architect of the Constitution and champion of Dalit rights.
• Mahad Satyagraha (1927): A movement to claim the right to use public water tanks.
• Political Empowerment: The belief that political power is essential for social change.
• The Poona Pact (1932): A compromise ensuring reserved seats for depressed classes.
• Constitutional Victory: The legal abolition of untouchability under Article 17.

The Intersection of Gender and Caste: Analyzing Overlapping Oppressions

• Intersectionality: How gender and caste oppression overlap and reinforce each other.
• Upper-caste women faced patriarchal control within their own social privilege.
• Marginalized women faced the double burden of caste hierarchy and gender violence.
• Caste 'purity' was often maintained through the strict control of women's bodies.
• True social reform requires dismantling both patriarchal and caste structures.

Critical Analysis: Common Mistakes in Interpreting Reform Movements and Key Definitions

• Avoid the 'Monolithic' trap: Reformers had diverse methods and goals.
• Recognize 'Bottom-Up' pressure: Grassroots struggles drove elite reforms.
• Distinguish Social Reform (customs) from Political Reform (power/rights).
• Key Terms: Orthodoxy (tradition), Emancipation (liberation), Hierarchy (rank).
• Reformist (gradual change) vs. Radical (fundamental overhaul).
