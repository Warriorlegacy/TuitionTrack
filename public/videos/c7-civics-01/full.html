Introduction: Why Equality Matters and the Concept of Universal Adult Franchise

• Equality is the fundamental pillar of a democratic society.
• Universal Adult Franchise: The right of all adults to vote.
• Universal means 'for everyone'; Franchise means 'the right to vote'.
• Political Equality: Every citizen's vote has equal value.
• Voting rights are independent of wealth, caste, or religion.

The Core Principle: Understanding Equality in the Indian Constitution

• The Constitution is the foundation of equality in Indian democracy.
• Equality before the law: The law applies to every citizen equally.
• Prohibition of discrimination based on religion, caste, sex, or place of birth.
• Equality of opportunity in education and public employment.
• Affirmative action: Special provisions to correct historical injustices.

Dimensions of Inequality: Caste-Based Discrimination and Social Hierarchies

• Inequality in India is not just economic; it is deeply social.
• The Caste System creates a rigid hierarchy based on birth.
• Social hierarchies dictate occupation, residence, and social interaction.
• Untouchability is a dehumanizing practice abolished by Article 17.
• Caste discrimination limits access to education and equal opportunities.

• Religious discrimination violates the constitutional right to equality.
• Religious disparity can lead to social exclusion and fragmented communities.
• Economic inequality creates a massive gap in access to basic resources.
• The cycle of poverty: Malnutrition and lack of education limit future growth.
• Intersectionality: How religion and economic status combine to increase hardship.

The Midday Meal Scheme: A Tool for Social Equality and Inclusion

• Midday Meal Scheme: Combating hunger and malnutrition in schools.
• Promotes social equality by breaking caste-based dining barriers.
• Increases school enrollment and reduces dropout rates significantly.
• Encourages gender equality through uniform nutrition for all.
• Fosters social inclusion by bringing diverse communities together.

Legal Framework: Article 15 and the Protection of Fundamental Rights

• Article 15 prohibits discrimination based on religion, race, caste, sex, or place of birth.
• Equality is a constitutional mandate, not just a social ideal.
• Protective Discrimination: Special provisions for women, children, and backward classes.
• Fundamental Rights are 'justiciable', meaning they can be enforced by courts.
• The Judiciary acts as the guardian of equality and constitutional rights.
