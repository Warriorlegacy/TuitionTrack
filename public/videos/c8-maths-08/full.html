Introduction: Real-world applications and the power of variables

• Variables: Letters like x or y that act as placeholders for changing numbers.

Classifying Expressions: Binomials, Trinomials, and Polynomials

• Monomial: An expression with exactly one term.
• Binomial: An expression with exactly two terms.
• Trinomial: An expression with exactly three terms.
• Polynomial: A general term for expressions with one or more terms.
• Rule: Variable exponents must be whole numbers to be a polynomial.

Part 4 of 12 · ~5 min

Like and Unlike Terms: The rules of addition and subtraction

• Like Terms: Same variables with the exact same exponents.
• Unlike Terms: Different variables or different exponents.
• Addition/Subtraction: Only perform operations on coefficients of like terms.
• Rule: The variable and its power remain unchanged during addition/subtraction.
• Example: 5x + 3x = 8x, but 5x + 3y cannot be simplified.

• Rule: x^m * x^n = x^(m+n).
• Monomial x Polynomial: Use the Distributive Law.

Multiplication Part 2: Binomial by Binomial (The Distributive Law)

• Every term in the first binomial must multiply every term in the second.
• Step 1: Distribute the first term of the first binomial.

• Always combine 'like terms' to simplify your final answer.

The Identity Framework: Understanding why identities differ from equations

• Equation: A statement true only for specific values of the variable.
• Identity: A statement true for all possible values of the variable.
• Equations are conditional; Identities are universal truths.
• Identities act as tools to simplify complex algebraic structures.
• Mastering identities allows for instant transformation of expressions.

Standard Identity 1: The Square of a Sum (a + b)² and its derivation

• Squaring a binomial means multiplying the expression by itself: (a + b) * (a + b)
• Using the distributive law: a(a + b) + b(a + b)
• Combining like terms: ab + ba = 2ab
• Standard Identity 1: (a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b²
• Identity application: (100 + 2)² = 100² + 2(100)(2) + 2²

Part 9 of 12 · ~5 min

Standard Identity 2: The Square of a Difference (a - b)² and its derivation

• Definition: (a - b)² means (a - b) multiplied by (a - b)
• Derivation: Use the distributive law to multiply every term
• Combining terms: -ab and -ab results in -2ab
• Standard Identity 2: (a - b)² = a² - 2ab + b²
• Key Tip: The middle term is negative, but b² is always positive

Part 10 of 12 · ~5 min

Standard Identity 3: The Product of Sum and Difference (a + b)(a - b)

• Identity: (a + b)(a - b) = a² - b²
• The middle terms 'ab' and '-ab' cancel each other out
• The product results in the 'difference of two squares'
• Use (x + k)(x - k) to simplify algebraic products quickly
• Apply to numbers: (50 + 2)(50 - 2) = 50² - 2²
