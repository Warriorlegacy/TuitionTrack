Introduction: Importance of Amines in Life, Medicine, and Industry

• Amines are derivatives of ammonia where hydrogen atoms are replaced by alkyl or aryl groups.
• Primary, secondary, and tertiary amines are classified by the number of substituted hydrogens.
• Biologically, amines like dopamine and serotonin act as vital neurotransmitters.
• The amine functional group is a core component in many essential pharmaceutical drugs.
• Industrially, amines are used to manufacture polymers like nylon, dyes, and pesticides.

Classification, Nomenclature, and Structure of Amines

• Classification: Primary (1°), Secondary (2°), and Tertiary (3°) amines
• Aliphatic amines contain alkyl groups; Aromatic amines contain aryl groups
• IUPAC Naming: Use suffix 'amine' or prefix 'N-' for substituted nitrogens
• Structure: sp3 hybridized nitrogen with a pyramidal geometry
• Lone pair delocalization in aromatic amines reduces basicity

Part 3 of 12 · ~4 min

Preparation of Amines: Reduction of Nitro Compounds and Amides

• Nitro compounds to primary amines via Fe/HCl or Sn/HCl
• Iron/HCl is preferred as iron acts as a reducing agent
• Amides to amines using Lithium Aluminium Hydride (LiAlH4)
• LiAlH4 reduces the carbonyl group (C=O) to a methylene group (CH2)
• Water is added after LiAlH4 reaction to release the final amine

Part 4 of 12 · ~4 min

Preparation of Amines: Hoffmann Bromamide Degradation and Gabriel Phthalimide Synthesis

• Hoffmann Bromamide: Converts amides to primary amines with one less carbon atom.
• Reaction reagents: Amide plus Bromine and Sodium Hydroxide.
• Hoffmann mechanism: Formation of N-bromoamide, isocyanate, and finally amine.
• Gabriel Phthalimide: Ideal for preparing pure primary aliphatic amines.
• Limitation: Gabriel synthesis cannot be used to prepare aromatic amines like aniline.

Physical Properties: Boiling Points, Solubility, and Hydrogen Bonding

• Amines have lower boiling points than alcohols due to weaker N-H hydrogen bonds.
• Boiling point trend: Primary > Secondary > Tertiary amines.
• Tertiary amines lack N-H bonds and cannot form intermolecular hydrogen bonds.
• Amines are soluble in water by forming hydrogen bonds with water molecules.
• Solubility decreases as the size of the hydrophobic alkyl group increases.

Part 6 of 12 · ~4 min

Basicity of Amines: Gas Phase vs. Aqueous Phase Trends

• Gas Phase: Basicity depends only on the Inductive Effect.
• Gas Phase Trend: Tertiary > Secondary > Primary > Ammonia.
• Aqueous Phase: Competition between Inductive, Solvation, and Steric effects.
• Solvation Effect: More H-atoms on the cation increase stability via H-bonding.
• Methyl Trend: Secondary > Primary > Tertiary | Ethyl Trend: Secondary > Tertiary > Primary.

Part 7 of 12 · ~4 min

Basicity of Amines: Effect of Substituents and Electronic Effects

• Basicity depends on the availability of the nitrogen lone pair
• Alkyl groups exert a +I effect, increasing basicity
• Electron-withdrawing groups exert a -I effect, decreasing basicity
• Resonance in aromatic amines delocalizes the lone pair, reducing basicity
• EDGs increase aromatic amine basicity; EWGs decrease it

Part 8 of 12 · ~3 min

Chemical Reactions I: Acylation and Carbylamine Reaction

• Amines act as nucleophiles due to the lone pair on Nitrogen.
• Acylation: Amines + Acid Anhydride/Chloride -> Amides.
• Acylation is used to protect the amino group in synthesis.
• Carbylamine Reaction: Primary Amines + Chloroform + KOH -> Isocyanides.
• The Carbylamine reaction is a qualitative test for primary amines.

Part 9 of 12 · ~4 min

Chemical Reactions II: Reaction with Nitrous Acid and Hinsberg's Reagent

• Primary aliphatic amines + nitrous acid -> Alcohol + Nitrogen gas (effervescence)
• Primary aromatic amines + nitrous acid -> Stable benzene diazonium salts
• Secondary amines + nitrous acid -> Yellow oily N-nitroso compounds
• Hinsberg's Reagent (Benzenesulfonyl chloride) distinguishes amine classes
• Primary amines form base-soluble sulfonamides; secondary amines do not

Part 10 of 12 · ~5 min

Chemical Reactions III: Electrophilic Substitution (Bromination, Nitration, Sulphonation)

• Amino group is a strong activating group due to resonance.
• Direct bromination of aniline yields 2,4,6-tribromoaniline.
• Acetylation of aniline reduces reactivity for controlled substitution.
• Nitration of aniline in strong acid yields meta-nitroaniline due to protonation.
• Protecting the amino group allows for ortho and para substitution.

Part 11 of 12 · ~4 min

Chemical Reactions IV: Diazonium Salts and their Synthetic Applications

• Diazotization: Aniline + NaNO2 + HCl at 0-5 degrees Celsius
• Sandmeyer Reaction: Replacement of diazonium group with Cl or Br using CuCl/CuBr
• Azo Coupling: Reaction with phenols or amines to produce colorful azo dyes
• Synthetic Utility: Conversion to Nitriles, Phenols, and Halobenzenes
• The diazonium group acts as an excellent leaving group in substitution reactions
