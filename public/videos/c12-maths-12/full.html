Graphical Method Part 1: Plotting Inequalities and Identifying the Feasible Region

• Convert inequalities into equations to find boundary lines
• Use x and y intercepts to plot the boundary lines accurately
• Solid lines for inclusive inequalities; dashed for strict inequalities
• Use the Test Point Method (usually origin) to determine shading
• The Feasible Region is the intersection of all shaded half-planes

Graphical Method Part 2: Corner Point Method and Finding Optimal Solutions

• The Feasible Region contains all possible valid solutions.
• Optimal solutions always occur at the corner points (vertices).
• Step 1: Identify all corner points by solving intersection equations.
• Step 2: Substitute each corner point into the Objective Function Z.
• Step 3: Compare Z-values to find the Maximum or Minimum.

Special Cases in LPP: Unbounded Regions, Infeasible Solutions, and Multiple Optimal Solutions

• Unbounded Region: Feasible area extends infinitely in one or more directions.
• Infeasible Solution: No common shaded region satisfies all constraints.
• Multiple Optimal Solutions: Objective function is parallel to a constraint line.
• Unbounded Maxima: Check if Z can increase infinitely before concluding.
• Multiple Solutions: All points on the line segment between two corners are optimal.

Linear Inequalities in Two Variables: Review of Shading Regions and Boundary Lines

• Boundary lines: Treat inequalities as equations to find the limit.
• Solid lines: Used for non-strict inequalities (<= or >=).
• Dashed lines: Used for strict inequalities (< or >).
• Test Point Method: Use (0,0) to determine the correct half-plane.
• The Feasible Region: The intersection of all shaded inequality regions.

Part 7 of 12 · ~4 min

The Geometry of Constraints: Intersection of Half-Planes and Convex Sets

• A linear inequality divides the plane into two distinct half-planes.
• The feasible region is the intersection of all given half-planes.
• A set is convex if the line segment between any two points stays within it.
• Linear constraints always produce a convex feasible region.
• Convexity ensures the optimal solution occurs at the corner points.

Common Pitfalls: Mistakes in Inequality Signs, Shading, and Corner Point Calculation

• Watch for keywords: 'at least' (>=) vs 'at most' (<=)
• Use dashed lines for strict inequalities (< or >)
• Always use the Test Point method (usually 0,0) to shade
• Find corner points using Algebra, not just visual estimation
• Verify if intersection points actually lie within the feasible region
