The Origin of Magnetism: Bar Magnets, Magnetic Moments, and Field Lines

• Magnetic poles always exist in pairs; monopoles do not exist.
• Magnetic Moment M = pole strength m times magnetic length 2L.
• SI unit of Magnetic Moment is Ampere-meter squared (A m^2).
• Field lines form continuous closed loops from North to South.
• Field line density represents field strength; lines never intersect.

Part 3 of 12 · ~5 min

Magnetization and Magnetic Intensity: H, M, B, and Susceptibility

• Magnetic Intensity (H): The external driving magnetic field (H = nI)
• Magnetization (M): Magnetic dipole moment per unit volume (M = m/V)
• Magnetic Induction (B): Total field inside material (B = mu naught H + M)
• Magnetic Susceptibility (chi): Ratio of response to cause (chi = M/H)
• Relative Permeability (mu_r): Relationship B = mu naught times mu_r times H

Part 4 of 12 · ~5 min

Magnetic Properties of Materials: Diamagnetic, Paramagnetic, and Ferromagnetic

• Diamagnetic: Negative susceptibility, weakly repelled by magnetic fields.
• Paramagnetic: Positive, small susceptibility, weakly attracted to fields.
• Ferromagnetic: Positive, very large susceptibility, strongly attracted.
• Curie's Law: Paramagnetic susceptibility is inversely proportional to temperature.
• Curie Temperature: The point where ferromagnets become paramagnetic.

Part 5 of 12 · ~5 min

Domain Theory and the Hysteresis Loop: Retentivity and Coercivity

• Magnetic Domains: Microscopic regions of aligned atomic magnetic moments.
• Saturation: The state where all domains are aligned with the external field.
• Retentivity: The residual magnetism remaining when the external field is zero.
• Coercivity: The reverse field required to reduce magnetization to zero.
• Hysteresis Loss: Energy dissipated as heat, equal to the area of the B-H loop.

Earth's Magnetism: Magnetic Elements and Field Geometry

• Magnetic Declination: Angle between geographic and magnetic meridians
• Angle of Dip (theta): Angle made by magnetic field lines with the horizontal
• At Magnetic Equator: Angle of dip is 0 degrees
• At Magnetic Poles: Angle of dip is 90 degrees
• Horizontal Component: B-H equals B cos theta; Vertical Component: Z equals B sin theta

Magnetic Declination, Dip, and Horizontal Component: Derivations and Math

• Magnetic Declination: Angle between Geographic and Magnetic North
• Magnetic Dip (Theta): Angle made by the total field with the horizontal
• Horizontal Component: B-H = B cos Theta
• Vertical Component: B-V = B sin Theta
• Relationship: tan Theta = B-V / B-H

🎬 A 3D Earth model showing a magnetic field vector B tilting at angle Theta, resolving into B-H and B-V components.

The Interaction of Magnetic Dipoles: Torque and Potential Energy

• Torque on a dipole: Tau equals M cross B
• Magnitude of Torque: Tau equals M B sin theta
• Maximum torque at theta equals 90 degrees
• Magnetic Potential Energy: U equals negative M B cos theta
• Stable equilibrium at theta equals 0 degrees

Magnetic Field of a Dipole: Axial and Equatorial Line Derivations

• Axial Field: B = 2M / r^3 (for r >> L)
• Equatorial Field: B = M / r^3 (for r >> L)
• Axial field is twice as strong as equatorial field at distance r
• Magnetic field of a dipole follows an inverse cube law
• Vector components cancel on the equatorial line but add on the axis

Comparative Analysis: Worked Examples and Common Calculation Errors

• Axial vs Equatorial: Axial field is twice the magnitude of Equatorial field at distance r.
• Torque and Energy: Always verify if theta is the angle between m and B.
• Earth's Magnetism: B-h equals B-total times cosine of dip; avoid the sine error.
• Unit Consistency: Always convert micro and milli prefixes to standard SI units.
• Directional Accuracy: Use the right-hand rule to prevent sign errors in vector results.
