Introduction: Real-world applications and the concept of Linear Equations in Two Variables

• Variables represent unknown quantities like cost, age, or distance.
• Standard form: a1x + b1y = c1 and a2x + b2y = c2.
• The term 'Linear' means the highest power of variables is one.
• A 'Pair' of equations allows us to find a unique common solution.
• Translating real-world scenarios into equations is the first step.

Part 2 of 12 · ~5 min

Graphical Method: Plotting lines and understanding the geometry of solutions

• A linear equation in two variables represents a straight line on a graph.
• The solution to a pair of equations is the point of intersection.
• Intersecting lines: Exactly one unique solution.
• Parallel lines: No intersection, therefore no solution.
• Coincident lines: Lines overlap, resulting in infinite solutions.

Algebraic Methods: Substitution Method - Concept, Steps, and Worked Examples

• Substitution means replacing a variable with an equivalent expression.
• Step 1: Express one variable in terms of the other from one equation.
• Step 2: Substitute this expression into the second equation.
• Step 3: Solve the resulting single-variable equation.
• Step 4: Back-substitute the value to find the remaining variable.

Algebraic Methods: Elimination Method - Concept, Derivations, and Worked Examples

• Goal: Remove one variable to create a single-variable equation
• Step 1: Multiply equations to make coefficients of one variable equal
• Step 2: Add or subtract equations to eliminate that variable
• Step 3: Solve for the remaining variable
• Step 4: Substitute the value back to find the second variable

Part 5 of 12 · ~5 min

Consistency and Inconsistency: Conditions for Unique, Infinite, and No Solutions

• Consistent System: Has at least one solution (Unique or Infinite)
• Inconsistent System: Has no solution (Parallel lines)
• Intersecting Lines: One unique solution (Consistent)
• Coincident Lines: Infinitely many solutions (Consistent)
• Parallel Lines: No solution (Inconsistent)

Part 6 of 12 · ~5 min

The Ratio Test: Comparing coefficients (a1/a2, b1/b2, c1/c2) for rapid analysis

• Standard Form: a1x + b1y = c1 and a2x + b2y = c2
• Unique Solution: a1/a2 ≠ b1/b2 (Intersecting Lines)
• No Solution: a1/a2 = b1/b2 ≠ c1/c2 (Parallel Lines)
• Infinite Solutions: a1/a2 = b1/b2 = c1/c2 (Coincident Lines)
• Rapid Analysis: Use ratios to check consistency without solving

Word Problems Part 1: Problems based on Age, Numbers, and Digits

• Rule 1: Age differences remain constant over time.
• Rule 2: 'Years ago' means subtraction; 'Years hence' means addition.
• Rule 3: For two-digit numbers, use the value formula: 10x + y.
• Rule 4: Always define variables clearly before forming equations.
• Rule 5: Translate English keywords into mathematical operators (+, -, =).

Word Problems Part 2: Geometry, Fractions, and Fixed/Variable Charges

• Geometry: Use perimeter and area formulas to build equations.
• Fractions: Define numerator as x and denominator as y.
• Fixed Charges: x represents the constant, y represents the rate.
• Variable Charges: Total cost = Fixed charge + (Distance × Rate).

Word Problems Part 3: Speed, Distance, Time, and Upstream/Downstream

• Fundamental Formula: Speed = Distance / Time
• Downstream Speed = Speed of Boat + Speed of Stream
• Upstream Speed = Speed of Boat - Speed of Stream
• Use substitution (1/u and 1/v) to simplify fractional equations
• Always ensure units for distance and time are consistent

Common Pitfalls: Identifying calculation errors, sign mistakes, and misinterpreting word problems

• Watch out: Always multiply the constant term on the right side of the equation.
• Sign Rule: Moving terms across the '=' sign flips the plus to minus and vice versa.
• Ratio Test: Maintain consistent order for a1/a2, b1/b2, and c1/c2.
• Word Problems: Carefully distinguish between 'years ago' and 'years hence'.
• Verification: Always plug your final values back into both original equations.
