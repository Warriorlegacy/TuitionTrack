Poetic Devices: Rhythm, Meter, and Rhyme Scheme Analysis

• Rhyme Scheme: The pattern of end-rhymes (e.g., A-B-A-B) that creates musicality.
• Rhythm: The 'heartbeat' or tempo of the poem that dictates its mood.
• Meter: The formal structure of stressed and unstressed syllables.
• Anapestic Meter: A pattern of two unstressed syllables followed by one stressed (da-da-DUM).
• Effect: Bouncy rhythm mimics the movement of the Kangaroo and the Duck.

Common Mistakes: Misinterpreting Tone and Poetic Structure

• Avoid treating 'Nonsense Verse' as a serious or tragic drama.
• Explain the effect of rhyme rather than just identifying the scheme.
• Recognize personification as human-like social interaction.
• Distinguish between rhyme (sound) and rhythm (beat/meter).
• Avoid literal interpretations; focus on the poetic meaning.
