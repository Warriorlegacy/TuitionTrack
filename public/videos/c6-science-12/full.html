The Solar System: Our Celestial Neighborhood and Sun's Role

• The term 'Solar' comes from 'Sol', the Latin word for Sun.
• The Sun is a massive ball of hot gases at the center of our system.
• The Sun's gravity keeps all planets and objects in their orbits.
• The Solar System includes 8 planets, dwarf planets, asteroids, and comets.
• Planets move in predictable, oval-shaped paths called orbits.

The Planets: Characteristics, Order, and Distinctions

• Order from Sun: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune
• Terrestrial Planets: Small, rocky, dense, and solid surfaces (Inner Planets)
• Jovian Planets: Massive, gaseous, low density, and no solid surface (Outer Planets)
• The Asteroid Belt: The dividing line between rocky and gaseous planets
• Key Distinction: Inner planets are rocky; Outer planets are gas and ice giants

Earth: Our Unique Home - Rotation, Revolution, and Seasons

• Rotation: Earth spinning on its axis causes Day and Night (24 hours).
• Revolution: Earth orbiting the Sun in an elliptical path (365.25 days).
• Leap Year: Every 4 years, the extra quarter days create February 29th.
• The Tilt: Earth's axis is tilted at 23.5 degrees, causing seasons.
• Solstices and Equinoxes: Variations in sunlight and day length throughout the year.

The Moon: Earth's Natural Satellite and Lunar Phases

• The Moon is Earth's only natural satellite.
• The Moon reflects sunlight; it does not produce its own light.
• Lunar phases are caused by the Moon's position relative to Earth and Sun.
• Waxing means the visible light is increasing; Waning means it is decreasing.
• A complete lunar cycle takes approximately twenty-nine and a half days.

Celestial Bodies: Stars, Constellations, and the Night Sky

• Stars: Massive, self-luminous spheres of hot gases (Hydrogen and Helium)
• Twinkling: Caused by atmospheric refraction of starlight
• Constellations: Recognizable patterns of stars in the night sky
• Ursa Major: The Great Bear, containing the Saptarishi pattern
• Polaris: The North Star used for navigation and finding North

The Sun: Our Star - Structure, Light, and Heat Energy

• The Sun is a massive sphere of hot gases, a star at the center of our system.
• The Core: The center where nuclear fusion creates immense energy.
• Layers: Radiative Zone, Convection Zone, Photosphere, Chromosphere, and Corona.
• Photosphere: The visible surface of the Sun that we observe.
• Energy: Light and Heat travel to Earth via electromagnetic radiation.

Gravity and Orbits: The Forces Holding the Universe Together

• Gravity is an invisible force of attraction between all objects with mass.
• The Sun's massive gravity keeps all planets in our solar system in check.
• An orbit is a curved path created by the balance of gravity and forward motion.
• Planets move in elliptical orbits, which are slightly flattened circles.
• Gravity strength increases with mass and decreases with distance.

Problem Solving: Calculating Distances, Scales, and Common Mistakes

• Astronomical Unit (A-U): Average distance from Earth to Sun.
• Scale Factor: A ratio used to create accurate space models.
• Formula: Model Distance = Real Distance divided by Scale Factor.
• Common Error: Confusing Radius with Diameter.
• Unit Check: Always convert kilometers to A-U or light-years correctly.
