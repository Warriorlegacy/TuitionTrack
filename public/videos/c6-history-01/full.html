What People Ate, Where They Lived, and How They Made Things

• Early humans were hunter-gatherers, moving to find food.
• Diet consisted of wild fruits, roots, nuts, and hunted meat.
• Shelter was found in natural caves or temporary leaf structures.
• Settlements began near rivers for water and fishing.
• Tools were crafted from stone, bone, wood, and clay.

• Rivers provided essential drinking water and a reliable lifeline.
• River flooding deposits fertile silt, making agriculture possible.
• Rivers served as ancient highways for trade and communication.
• Early settlements flourished in the Indus and Ganga river valleys.
• Geography, including mountains and forests, shaped human survival.

Finding Out About the Past: Manuscripts and Inscriptions

• Manuscripts: Hand-written documents on soft surfaces like palm leaves.
• Manuscripts were often written on birch bark in the Himalayan region.
• Inscriptions: Permanent writings carved onto hard surfaces like stone or metal.
• Inscriptions are more durable and survive much longer than manuscripts.
• Both sources act as vital clues for historians to reconstruct the past.

Archaeology: Tools, Artifacts, and the Science of Excavation

• Artifacts: Objects made or used by humans, like tools and pottery.
• Excavation: The scientific, layer-by-layer digging of the earth.
• Stratigraphy: The principle that deeper layers represent older time periods.
• Archaeological Tools: Using trowels, brushes, and sieves for precision.
• Context and Lab Work: Recording locations and using carbon dating for age.

Part 7 of 12 · ~5 min

The Concept of Time: BC, AD, BCE, and CE Explained

• Timeline: A tool to organize historical events in order.
• BC (Before Christ) is the same as BCE (Before Common Era).
• AD (Anno Domini) is the same as CE (Common Era).
• In BC/BCE, larger numbers represent older time periods.
• BCE and CE are used globally to be culturally neutral.

Common Mistakes: Misinterpreting Sources and Chronology

• Avoid Bias: Don't assume a single source tells the whole truth.
• Context Matters: One artifact does not represent an entire society.
• Chronology Check: Remember that BCE years count backwards toward zero.
• Avoid Anachronisms: Do not place objects in the wrong time periods.
• Cross-Reference: Always compare manuscripts with archaeological finds.
