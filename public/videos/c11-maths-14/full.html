Fundamental Concepts: Sample Space, Events, and Types of Events

• Sample Space (S): The set of all possible outcomes of a random experiment.
• Event (E): A subset of the Sample Space containing specific outcomes.
• Simple Event: An event consisting of exactly one outcome.
• Compound Event: An event consisting of more than one outcome.
• Mutually Exclusive Events: Events that cannot occur simultaneously (Intersection is empty).

Set Theory in Probability: Union, Intersection, Complement, and Venn Diagram Representation

• An event is mathematically defined as a subset of the Sample Space S
• Union (A cup B): The occurrence of event A, event B, or both
• Intersection (A cap B): The simultaneous occurrence of both A and B
• Complement (A bar): All outcomes in S that are not in event A
• Venn Diagrams: Visualizing sets using circles within a rectangular sample space

Axiomatic Approach: Probability of an Event, Range of Probability, and Basic Axioms

• Probability P(A) is a real number assigned to an event A.
• Range of Probability: 0 ≤ P(A) ≤ 1.
• Axiom 1: Non-negativity — P(A) ≥ 0 for any event A.
• Axiom 2: Certainty — P(S) = 1 for the entire sample space S.
• Axiom 3: Additivity — For mutually exclusive events, P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B).

Algebra of Events: Addition Theorem for Two and Three Events

• Addition Theorem for two events: P(A U B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A ∩ B)
• If events are mutually exclusive, P(A ∩ B) = 0
• For three events, we use the Principle of Inclusion-Exclusion
• Formula: P(A U B U C) = P(A)+P(B)+P(C) - [P(A∩B)+P(B∩C)+P(C∩A)] + P(A∩B∩C)
• Visualizing overlaps prevents double-counting or missing regions

Part 6 of 12 · ~5 min

Conditional Probability: Definition, Formula, and the Concept of Reduced Sample Space

• Definition: Probability of event A occurring given that event B has already occurred.
• Notation: P(A|B) represents the conditional probability of A given B.
• The Formula: P(A|B) = P(A ∩ B) / P(B), where P(B) > 0.
• Reduced Sample Space: Event B becomes the new universal set for the calculation.
• Logic: The intersection A ∩ B is the only part of A that survives the condition B.

Multiplication Theorem: Independent Events vs. Dependent Events and Derivations

• Dependent Events: The outcome of the first event affects the second event.
• Independent Events: The occurrence of one event does not affect the other.
• Multiplication Theorem (General): P(A and B) = P(A) * P(B|A)
• Multiplication Theorem (Independent): P(A and B) = P(A) * P(B)
• Key distinction: Sampling with replacement vs. Sampling without replacement.

Probability of Independent Events: Mathematical Proofs and Practical Applications

• Definition: Event A does not affect the probability of Event B
• Mathematical Condition: P(B|A) = P(B) and P(A|B) = P(A)
• The Multiplication Rule: P(A intersection B) = P(A) * P(B)
• Independence vs Mutually Exclusive: One is about influence, the other about overlap
• Application: Calculating reliability in multi-component systems

Bayes' Theorem: Concept of Prior and Posterior Probability with Detailed Derivations

• Prior Probability: Initial belief before new evidence is observed.
• Posterior Probability: Updated belief after incorporating new evidence.
• Bayes' Theorem: Relates conditional probabilities of inverse events.
• Derivation: Uses the Law of Total Probability and Multiplication Theorem.
• Formula: P(Ei|A) equals P(Ei) times P(A|Ei) divided by the sum of P(Ej) times P(A|Ej).

Common Pitfalls: Misinterpreting 'At Least/At Most', Conditional Errors, and Calculation Mistakes

• At Least: Includes the value and everything greater.
• At Most: Includes the value and everything smaller.
• Complement Rule: P(at least one) = 1 - P(none).
• Conditional Probability: Always use the condition as the new sample space.
• Replacement Matters: 'Without replacement' changes the denominator.
