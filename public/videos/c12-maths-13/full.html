Introduction: Real-world Applications, Intuition, and the Concept of Random Experiments

• Probability: The mathematical language used to quantify uncertainty.
• Deterministic vs. Random: Certainty versus unpredictability.
• Random Experiment: An action with uncertain but known possible outcomes.
• Probability Scale: Values range from zero (impossible) to one (certain).
• Core Requirement: Outcomes must be known in advance even if uncertain.

Foundations: Sample Spaces, Events, and Types of Events

• Sample Space (S): The set of all possible outcomes of a random experiment.
• Event: A subset of the Sample Space representing specific outcomes.
• Simple Event: An event consisting of exactly one outcome.
• Compound Event: An event consisting of more than one outcome.
• Mutually Exclusive: Events that cannot occur simultaneously (Intersection is empty).

Part 3 of 12 · ~5 min

Conditional Probability: Definitions, Formulas, and Logical Derivations

• Definition: Probability of A occurring given that B has already occurred
• Notation: P(A|B) = P(A intersection B) / P(B), where P(B) > 0
• Concept: The occurrence of B restricts the original sample space to B
• Logic: The new sample space is B; the favorable outcomes are A intersection B
• Caution: P(A|B) is generally not equal to P(B|A)

Part 4 of 12 · ~4 min

Multiplication Theorem of Probability and Independent Events

• Multiplication Theorem: P(A ∩ B) = P(A) × P(B|A)
• General formula for three events: P(A ∩ B ∩ C) = P(A) × P(B|A) × P(C|A ∩ B)
• Independent Events: P(B|A) = P(B)
• Special Product Rule for Independence: P(A ∩ B) = P(A) × P(B)
• Crucial Distinction: Independent events are NOT Mutually Exclusive

Bayes' Theorem: Concept, Derivation, and Systematic Problem-Solving

• Bayes' Theorem: Calculating inverse probability (cause given effect)
• Formula: P(Ei|A) = [P(Ei) * P(A|Ei)] / Sum of [P(Ek) * P(A|Ek)]
• Prior Probability: The initial probability of a cause occurring
• Likelihood: The probability of the evidence given a specific cause
• Systematic Steps: Identify causes, identify evidence, calculate, and ratio

Total Probability Theorem and its Relationship with Bayes' Theorem

• Events must be mutually exclusive and exhaustive to form a partition.
• Total Probability is the sum of probabilities of an event across all partitions.
• Formula: P(A) = Sum of [P(Ei) times P(A|Ei)] for all i.
• Bayes' Theorem uses the Total Probability as its denominator.
• Total Probability moves 'Cause to Effect'; Bayes' moves 'Effect to Cause'.

Probability Distributions: Random Variables and Probability Mass Functions (PMF)

• Random Variable (X): A function mapping outcomes to real numbers
• Range: The set of all possible numerical values of X
• Probability Mass Function (PMF): P(X = x) for discrete variables
• Condition 1: Every individual probability must be non-negative
• Condition 2: The sum of all probabilities in the distribution must equal 1

Bernoulli Trials and Binomial Distribution: Concepts and Formulas

• Bernoulli Trial: An experiment with exactly two outcomes: Success (p) or Failure (q).
• Conditions: Fixed trials (n), constant probability (p), and independent outcomes.
• Binomial Distribution: Models the number of successes in 'n' independent trials.
• Formula: P(X = x) = nCx * p^x * q^(n-x).
• Note: q is always equal to (1 - p).

Common Pitfalls: Identifying Mistakes in Conditional vs. Independent Logic

• Confusion Alert: Mutually Exclusive is NOT the same as Independent.
• Mutually Exclusive: P(A intersection B) = 0 (Events cannot coexist).
• Independent Events: P(A intersection B) = P(A) times P(B).
• Conditional Probability: Always remember the 'Shrinking Sample Space'.
• Check for Replacement: 'Without replacement' implies dependency.
