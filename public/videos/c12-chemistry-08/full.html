Introduction: The Significance of Carbonyl Compounds & Real-world Applications

• The Carbonyl Group: A carbon atom double-bonded to an oxygen atom (C=O)
• Aldehydes vs. Ketones: Based on the substituents attached to the carbonyl carbon
• Natural Aromas: Cinnamaldehyde in cinnamon and Benzaldehyde in almonds
• Industrial Importance: Acetone as a vital solvent and Vanillin in flavoring
• Biological Role: Glucose as a key polyhydroxy aldehyde in metabolism

Nomenclature and Structure of Aldehydes and Ketones

• Carbonyl group: Carbon double-bonded to Oxygen (C=O)
• Aldehydes: Terminal group, suffix '-al', carbon is C-one
• Ketones: Internal group, suffix '-one', position number required
• Geometry: sp-two hybridization, trigonal planar, 120-degree angles
• Polarity: Oxygen is delta-minus, Carbon is delta-plus (electrophilic)

Methods of Preparation: Oxidation and Reduction Pathways

• Primary alcohols + PCC -> Aldehydes (Selective Oxidation)
• Secondary alcohols + CrO3/H+ -> Ketones
• Catalytic Hydrogenation: Carbonyl + H2/Ni -> Alcohol
• Na-B-H-4: Mild reduction (Aldehydes/Ketones only)
• Li-Al-H-4: Strong reduction (Acids, Esters, and Carbonyls)

Physical Properties: Boiling Points, Solubility, and Dipole Moments

• Carbonyl group is polar due to oxygen's high electronegativity
• Aldehydes and ketones have higher boiling points than alkanes
• Carboxylic acids form dimers via strong intermolecular hydrogen bonding
• Polarity allows aldehydes/ketones to form hydrogen bonds with water
• Solubility decreases as the length of the alkyl chain increases

Part 5 of 12 · ~4 min

Nucleophilic Addition Reactions: Mechanism and Kinetics

• Carbonyl group is polar: Carbon is delta positive, Oxygen is delta negative
• Nucleophilic attack converts sp2 planar carbon to sp3 tetrahedral intermediate
• Reactivity Order: Aldehydes > Ketones due to steric and electronic factors
• Steric Effect: Larger alkyl groups in ketones hinder nucleophile approach
• Electronic Effect: Alkyl groups reduce electrophilicity via inductive effect

Addition-Elimination Reactions: Reactions with Derivatives

• Mechanism: Nucleophilic attack followed by loss of a leaving group
• Formation of Tetrahedral Intermediate: The key transition state
• Reaction with Primary Amines: Produces Imines (Schiff bases)
• Reaction with Hydroxylamine: Produces Oximes
• Reaction with Hydrazine and Semicarbazide: Produces Hydrazones and Semicarbazones

Part 7 of 12 · ~3 min

Alpha-Hydrogen Reactions: Aldol Condensation and Cannizzaro Reaction

• Alpha-hydrogens are acidic due to resonance stabilization by the carbonyl group.
• Aldol Condensation: Requires alpha-hydrogens; forms beta-hydroxy aldehydes.
• Dehydration of aldols leads to alpha, beta-unsaturated carbonyl compounds.
• Cannizzaro Reaction: Occurs in aldehydes lacking alpha-hydrogens.
• Cannizzaro is a disproportionation reaction producing alcohol and acid salt.

Part 8 of 12 · ~4 min

Chemical Tests to Distinguish Aldehydes and Ketones

• Aldehydes are easily oxidized; ketones are resistant to mild oxidation.
• Tollen's Test: Aldehydes form a silver mirror with ammoniacal silver nitrate.
• Fehling's Test: Aliphatic aldehydes produce a reddish-brown cuprous oxide precipitate.
• Iodoform Test: Identifies methyl ketones and acetaldehyde via yellow triiodomethane.
• Aromatic aldehydes like benzaldehyde do not react with Fehling's solution.

Carboxylic Acids: Structure, Acidity, and Preparation

• Carboxyl group structure: Carbonyl group plus a Hydroxyl group (C-O-O-H)
• Hybridization: The carboxyl carbon is sp-hybridized with linear geometry
• Acidity: Enhanced by resonance stabilization of the carboxylate ion
• Inductive Effect: Electron-withdrawing groups increase acidity
• Preparation: Oxidation of primary alcohols, aldehydes, and nitriles

Part 10 of 12 · ~5 min

Reactions of Carboxylic Acids and their Derivatives

• Acid-Base Neutralization: Carboxylic acids react with bases to form carboxylate salts.
• Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky Reaction: Alpha-halogenation using red phosphorus and bromine.
• Reactivity Order: Acyl Chlorides > Anhydrides > Esters > Amides.
• Saponification: Irreversible base-catalyzed hydrolysis of esters to salts and alcohols.
• Amide Stability: High resonance stabilization leads to low electrophilicity.

Part 11 of 12 · ~3 min

Common Mistakes, Reaction Mechanisms, and Troubleshooting

• Aldehydes are more reactive than ketones due to less steric hindrance
• Aldol condensation requires alpha-hydrogens; Cannizzaro does not
• Electron-withdrawing groups increase the acidity of carboxylic acids
• Use PCC to stop oxidation of primary alcohols at the aldehyde stage
• Always draw arrows from nucleophile to carbon and electrons to oxygen
