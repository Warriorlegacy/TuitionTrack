Part 3 of 12 · ~3 min

Defining Heights and Distances: Concept of Line of Sight and Horizontal Level

• Line of Sight: The straight line from the observer's eye to the object.
• Horizontal Level: An imaginary flat line passing through the observer's eye.
• The observer's eye acts as the vertex for all trigonometric measurements.
• The relationship between these two lines defines our angles.
• Accurate diagrams depend on correctly placing the horizontal level.

Part 4 of 12 · ~4 min

The Angle of Elevation: Geometric construction, diagrams, and core concept

• Angle of Elevation: Angle between horizontal level and line of sight
• Observer (Point O) to Object (Point T) creates the line of sight
• Geometric Construction: Forms a right-angled triangle O-H-T
• The angle is always measured upwards from the horizontal line
• Key sides: Perpendicular (Height), Base (Distance), and Hypotenuse

Part 5 of 12 · ~4 min

The Angle of Depression: Understanding the perpendicular drop and ray of sight

• Horizontal Level: The imaginary line parallel to the ground from the observer's eye.
• Angle of Depression: Angle between the horizontal line and the line of sight.
• The Ray of Sight: The straight line connecting the observer's eye to the object.
• Parallel Line Property: Angle of Depression = Angle of Elevation (Alternate Interior Angles).
• Pro-Tip: Move the angle of depression to the ground level to form a standard triangle.

Part 7 of 12 · ~4 min

The Power of Ratios: Strategic selection of Sin, Cos, or Tan for different scenarios

• Identify the three sides: Hypotenuse, Opposite, and Adjacent relative to theta.
• Tangent (Opposite/Adjacent): Best for height and ground distance problems.
• Sine (Opposite/Hypotenuse): Use when the slant distance or string length is involved.
• Cosine (Adjacent/Hypotenuse): Use when finding ground distance from a slant length.
• Strategy: Always ask 'What do I HAVE?' and 'What do I WANT?' before choosing.

Part 9 of 12 · ~4 min

Double Triangle Problems (Case 1): Moving towards/away from an object

• Movement creates two distinct right-angled triangles sharing one common height.
• Moving towards an object increases the angle of elevation.
• Moving away from an object decreases the angle of elevation.
• Use the Tangent ratio (Tan theta = Opposite / Adjacent) for both triangles.
• Solve using simultaneous equations to find height or distance moved.

Common Pitfalls: Calculation errors, diagram mistakes, and unit mismatches

• Diagram: Always draw the horizontal line for the angle of depression.
• Diagram: Ensure the angle of elevation/depression is correctly placed.
• Calculation: Do not round off intermediate values; keep extra decimals.
• Calculation: Be careful with rationalizing denominators and square roots.
• Units: Convert all measurements to a single unit before calculating.
