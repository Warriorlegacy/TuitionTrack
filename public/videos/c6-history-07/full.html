The Mauryan Empire: Origins, Chandragupta Maurya, and Chanakya's Role

• The Mauryan Empire rose from the fragmented Mahajanapadas of Northern India.
• Chandragupta Maurya founded the dynasty by overthrowing the Nanda kings.
• Chanakya, a scholar from Taxila, served as the chief strategist and mentor.
• The empire was built using a powerful army of infantry, cavalry, and elephants.
• Pataliputra served as the strategic and magnificent capital city.

Empire Administration: How a Vast Territory was Governed and Controlled

• Empire divided into provinces ruled by royal governors or Kumaras
• Pataliputra served as the central capital with provincial hubs like Taxila
• A vast bureaucracy managed specialized tasks like revenue and trade
• A secret network of spies monitored officials and public sentiment
• A massive standing army of infantry, cavalry, and elephants ensured stability

The Kalinga War: The Turning Point and the Concept of Dhamma

• Kalinga: A strategic and prosperous coastal kingdom near modern Odisha.
• The Kalinga War: A massive conflict resulting in immense human suffering.
• The Turning Point: Ashoka's profound remorse after witnessing the war's devastation.
• From Vijaya to Dhammavijaya: Shifting from military conquest to moral conquest.
• Concept of Dhamma: A code of social ethics rather than a new religion.

Part 6 of 12 · ~5 min

Ashoka's Dhamma: Core Principles, Definitions, and Social Ethics

• Dhamma was a code of social ethics, not a new religion or ritual system.
• Core values: Respect for elders, teachers, and social subordinates.
• Emphasis on Ahimsa: Non-violence in action, speech, and thought.
• Promotion of religious tolerance and respect for all different sects.
• Role of Dhamma Mahamattas: Special officers to spread moral teachings.

A 3D cinematic pan across a diverse Mauryan marketplace showing people of different backgrounds interacting peacefully, transitioning to a close-up of a Dhamma Mahamatta teaching a group of villagers.

Spreading the Message: Edicts, Inscriptions, and Rock/Pillar Formats

• Inscriptions: Messages carved on hard surfaces like stone or metal.
• Rock Edicts: Large messages carved on boulders in prominent locations.
• Pillar Edicts: Tall, polished stone columns placed in cities and sacred sites.
• Language: Use of Prakrit and local scripts to reach the common people.
• Purpose: To communicate Dhamma directly to all subjects across the empire.

Visualizing History: Analyzing Diagrams of Ashokan Pillars and Symbols

• Monolithic construction: Pillars carved from a single piece of stone.
• The Shaft: The long, polished vertical body of the pillar.
• The Capital: The ornate topmost section, often featuring animals.
• The Abacus: The platform below the capital supporting the animals.
• Symbolism: The Dharmachakra (Wheel of Law) and sacred animals.

Archaeological Evidence: Deciphering Inscriptions and Material Remains

• Primary Sources: Direct evidence from the time period, like Ashokan edicts.
• Decipherment: James Prinsep's breakthrough in reading the Brahmi script.
• Material Remains: Using pottery (NBPW), coins, and tools to map trade.
• Numismatics: Studying ancient coins to understand the Mauryan economy.
• Triangulation: Combining inscriptions and physical finds to verify history.
