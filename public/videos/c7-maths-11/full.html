Understanding Base and Exponent: Definitions and Notations

• Base: The number that is multiplied by itself repeatedly.
• Exponent: The number indicating how many times the base is used as a factor.
• Notation: Written as Base raised to the power of Exponent.
• Common Error: Exponent is NOT a multiplier for the base (e.g., 5^3 is not 5 times 3).
• Example: In 2 raised to the power 5, 2 is the Base and 5 is the Exponent.

Expanded Form and Exponential Form: Converting Between Representations

• Exponential Form: A compact way to write repeated multiplication.
• Expanded Form: Writing out the base as many times as the exponent indicates.
• Converting to Expanded: Write the base repeatedly with multiplication signs.
• Converting to Exponential: Count the number of times the base repeats.
• Crucial Rule: All bases in the expanded form must be identical to use one exponent.

Laws of Exponents Part 1: Product and Quotient Rules

• Product Rule: When multiplying same bases, add the exponents.
• Formula: a^m * a^n = a^(m+n)
• Quotient Rule: When dividing same bases, subtract the exponents.
• Formula: a^m / a^n = a^(m-n)
• Crucial Requirement: The bases must be exactly the same to use these rules.

Part 5 of 12 · ~5 min

Laws of Exponents Part 2: Power of a Power and Power of a Product

• Power of a Power Rule: (a^m)^n = a raised to the power of (m times n)
• Power of a Product Rule: (xy)^p = x raised to the p times y raised to the p
• To solve (a^m)^n, simply multiply the two exponents together
• The exponent outside parentheses applies to every factor inside
• Caution: These rules do NOT apply to addition or subtraction inside parentheses

The Zero Exponent Rule and Negative Exponents (Conceptual Introduction)

• Observe the pattern: decreasing exponents means dividing by the base
• The Zero Exponent Rule: Any non-zero number to the power of zero is 1
• Negative exponents do not result in negative numbers
• A negative exponent represents the reciprocal of the base
• Formula: a raised to the power of negative n equals 1 divided by a raised to the power of n

Comparing Large Numbers: Standard Form and Scientific Notation

• Standard Form: A number between 1 and 10 multiplied by a power of 10
• Step 1: Place decimal after the first non-zero digit
• Step 2: Use the number of decimal shifts as the exponent for 10
• Comparing numbers: Align the powers of ten first
• Rule: The coefficient must be 1 <= x < 10

Expressing Very Small Numbers: Decimals to Standard Form

• Standard form: a number between 1 and 10 multiplied by a power of 10.
• Moving decimal to the right results in a negative exponent.
• The number of decimal places moved equals the value of the exponent.
• Example: 0.0008 becomes 8 times 10 to the power of negative 4.
• Used to represent microscopic sizes like cells or atoms easily.

Common Pitfalls: Avoiding Calculation Errors and Sign Mistakes

• Don't confuse 'base times exponent' with 'base raised to exponent'
• Watch the parentheses: (-3) squared is 9, but -3 squared is -9
• Laws of Exponents do NOT apply to addition or subtraction
• Remember: Any non-zero number raised to the power of zero is 1
• In Standard Form, double-check your decimal point jumps
