Foundations of Coordinate Geometry: The Cartesian Plane, Axes, and Origin

• The Cartesian Plane: A two-dimensional flat surface.
• X-axis: The horizontal number line.
• Y-axis: The vertical number line.
• The Origin: The intersection point (0, 0).
• Quadrants: Four regions numbered I, II, III, and IV counter-clockwise.

Plotting Points: Understanding Coordinates (x, y) and Quadrant Logic

• An ordered pair (x, y) acts as a unique address for every point.
• The x-coordinate represents horizontal movement; the y-coordinate is vertical.
• Order matters: (x, y) is not the same as (y, x).
• Quadrant I: (+, +), Quadrant II: (-, +), Quadrant III: (-, -), Quadrant IV: (+, -).
• Points with a zero coordinate lie directly on the x-axis or y-axis.

Interpreting Linear Graphs: Reading Slopes and Constant Rates of Change

• Linear graphs represent a constant rate of change.
• Slope measures the steepness of a line.
• Slope formula: m = (y2 - y1) / (x2 - x1)
• Positive slope goes up; Negative slope goes down.
• Horizontal lines have zero slope; Vertical lines are undefined.

Mapping Relationships: Independent vs. Dependent Variables in Graphs

• Independent Variable: The 'cause' or driver, plotted on the x-axis.
• Dependent Variable: The 'effect' or result, plotted on the y-axis.
• The x-axis represents the variable that changes independently (e.g., Time).
• The y-axis represents the variable that is measured (e.g., Height or Cost).
• Mathematical relationship: y is a function of x (y depends on x).

Real-World Word Problems: Distance-Time Graphs and Speed Analysis

• X-axis represents Time; Y-axis represents Distance covered.
• A horizontal line indicates the object is stationary (Speed = 0).
• The slope of a distance-time graph represents the Speed.
• Formula: Speed = Total Distance divided by Total Time.
• Steeper slope means higher speed; curved lines indicate changing speed.

Real-World Word Problems: Time-Temperature and Growth Graphs

• Time is the independent variable (x-axis); Temperature is dependent (y-axis).
• A horizontal line in a temperature graph means the temperature is constant.
• A straight diagonal line indicates a constant rate of change.
• Growth graphs often show curves rather than straight lines.
• Steeper curves represent a faster rate of growth over time.

Common Pitfalls: Scaling Errors, Axis Labeling, and Misreading Coordinates

• Scaling: Choose intervals that fit your data range and fill the grid.
• Consistency: Use equal intervals like 5, 10, 15 or 2, 4, 6.
• Labeling: Always include the variable name and the unit (e.g., Time in sec).
• Coordinates: Always follow the (x, y) order; x is horizontal, y is vertical.
• Precision: Start counting from the origin (zero) to avoid offset errors.

Exam Techniques: Precision in Plotting and Interpreting Complex Graphs

• Choose a scale that fills at least 75% of the provided grid.
• Always state your scale clearly in the top right corner.
• Use a sharp pencil and precise marks (dots or small crosses).
• Use a ruler for all straight lines and construction lines.
• Always include units for both x and y axes to avoid errors.
