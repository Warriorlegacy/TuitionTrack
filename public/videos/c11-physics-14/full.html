Introduction: The Physics of Sound, Light, and Motion (The Hook)

• Waves carry energy from one point to another without transporting matter.
• A wave is a disturbance that propagates through a medium or vacuum.
• Mechanical waves require a medium; electromagnetic waves do not.
• Wave motion involves key parameters: amplitude, frequency, and wavelength.
• Understanding waves is essential for studying sound, light, and vibration.

Mechanical Waves: Transverse vs. Longitudinal and Wave Propagation

• Mechanical waves require a material medium to propagate.
• Transverse waves: Particle motion is perpendicular to wave direction.
• Longitudinal waves: Particle motion is parallel to wave direction.
• Transverse features: Crests and Troughs.
• Longitudinal features: Compressions and Rarefactions.

Part 3 of 12 · ~5 min

Wave Motion: Mathematical Representation, Amplitude, Frequency, and Wavelength

• Displacement Equation: y(x,t) = A sin(kx - ωt)
• Amplitude (A): Maximum displacement from equilibrium
• Wavelength (λ): Distance between two consecutive crests or troughs
• Frequency (f): Number of oscillations per second (Hertz)
• Wave Number (k) = 2π/λ and Angular Frequency (ω) = 2πf

Part 4 of 12 · ~5 min

Speed of Waves: Velocity, Period, and the Wave Equation Derivation

• Wave Velocity: V = wavelength times frequency (V = lambda * f)
• Period and Frequency Relationship: T = 1/f
• Wave Number (k): k = 2 * pi / lambda
• Angular Frequency (omega): omega = 2 * pi * f
• General Wave Equation: y = A sin(kx - omega*t)

Part 5 of 12 · ~5 min

Superposition Principle: Interference, Standing Waves, and Nodes/Antinodes

• Superposition Principle: Resultant displacement is the sum of individual displacements.
• Constructive Interference: Crest meets crest; phase difference is zero or 2-pi-n.
• Destructive Interference: Crest meets trough; phase difference is pi or (2n-1)pi.
• Standing Waves: Formed by two waves of same frequency traveling in opposite directions.
• Nodes: Zero amplitude points; Antinodes: Maximum amplitude points.

Transverse Waves in Strings: Speed, Harmonics, and Overtones

• Wave speed v = square root of (Tension divided by linear mass density)
• Boundary conditions: Fixed ends create nodes at x = 0 and x = L
• Fundamental frequency f1 = v divided by 2L
• Harmonics are integer multiples of the fundamental frequency
• First overtone corresponds to the second harmonic

Longitudinal Waves in Air: Sound Waves and Pressure Variations

• Sound waves in air are longitudinal waves.
• Compression: High pressure and high density regions.
• Rarefaction: Low pressure and low density regions.
• Pressure variation is proportional to particle displacement.
• Pressure waves and displacement waves are ninety degrees out of phase.

Part 8 of 12 · ~5 min

Speed of Sound: Newton’s Formula and Laplace’s Correction

• Newton's Assumption: Sound propagation is an isothermal process.
• Newton's Formula: Speed V = square root of (Pressure / Density).
• The Error: Newton's predicted speed was ~280 m/s, which is too low.
• Laplace's Correction: Sound propagation is actually an adiabatic process.
• Laplace's Formula: Speed V = square root of (gamma * Pressure / Density).

Beats: Formation, Frequency Difference, and Mathematical Analysis

• Beats: Periodic variation in loudness due to superposition
• Occurs when two waves of slightly different frequencies interfere
• Resultant amplitude varies as a slow-moving envelope
• Beat Frequency (n-beat) = |n-two - n-one|
• Used in acoustics to tune musical instruments precisely

Part 10 of 12 · ~5 min

Doppler Effect: Frequency Shifts in Moving Sources and Observers

• Doppler Effect: Change in perceived frequency due to relative motion
• Moving Source: Compresses wavelength (higher f) or stretches it (lower f)
• Moving Observer: Changes the rate of wave crest encounters
• Master Formula: f-prime = f * (v ± v-observer) / (v ∓ v-source)
• Sign Convention: Use '+' in numerator for approaching observer

Complex Wave Phenomena: Reflection, Refraction, and Diffraction Basics

• Reflection: Waves bounce back; angle of incidence equals angle of reflection.
• Fixed vs. Free ends: Phase changes of 180 degrees vs. no phase change.
• Refraction: Bending of waves due to change in speed between media.
• Snell's Law: Sine theta one divided by sine theta two equals a constant.
• Diffraction: The spreading of waves around obstacles or through slits.
