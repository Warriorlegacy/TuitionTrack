Literary Devices: Identifying Similes, Metaphors, and Personification

• Simile: Comparing two things using 'like' or 'as'.
• Metaphor: A direct comparison saying one thing IS another.
• Personification: Giving human qualities to non-human things.
• Simile Formula: Thing A + like/as + Thing B.
• Use literary devices to create vivid mental imagery.

Grammar Focus: Tense Usage and Sentence Structure in Narrative

• Simple Past Tense: Subject + Verb (ed) for completed actions.
• Past Continuous: Subject + was/were + Verb-ing for background settings.
• Past Perfect: Subject + had + Past Participle for earlier events.
• Compound Sentences: Joining ideas using FANBOYS conjunctions.
• Complex Sentences: Using because, although, or while for depth.

Part 10 of 12 · ~5 min

Common Pitfalls: Avoiding Interpretation Errors and Spelling Mistakes

• Avoid literal traps: Look for synonyms, not just exact words.
• Don't over-infer: Stick to the facts provided in the text.
• Master tricky spellings: Use the Look-Say-Cover-Write-Check method.
• Watch out for homophones: Distinguish between their, there, and they're.
• Use correct punctuation: Avoid run-on sentences to improve clarity.

Writing Skills: How to Draft Character Sketches and Summaries

• Character Sketch Formula: Traits + Evidence + Impact
• Use Present Tense when describing character personalities
• Summary Formula: Setting + Plot (Somebody-Wanted-But-So-Then) + Conclusion
• Always write summaries in your own words, not copied from text
• Use the 'Movie Trailer' approach: capture essence, skip tiny details
