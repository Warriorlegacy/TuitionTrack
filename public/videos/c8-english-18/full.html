Literary Devices I: Exploring Imagery, Personification, and Metaphor

• Imagery: Creating mental pictures through sensory details (sight, sound, etc.)
• Personification: Giving human traits to animals, objects, or nature
• Metaphor: A direct comparison between two different things (e.g., Nature = Poetry)
• Auditory Imagery: Using words to evoke the sense of hearing

Literary Devices II: Rhyme Scheme, Rhythm, and Sound Patterns

• Rhyme Scheme: The pattern of end-rhymes (A-B-A-B pattern).
• Alternate Rhyme: When every second line rhymes with the one after it.
• Rhythm: The musical beat created by stressed and unstressed syllables.
• Alliteration: Repetition of initial consonant sounds in nearby words.
• Sound Patterns: How word choice creates a sensory, musical experience.

Common Mistakes: Avoiding Interpretational and Grammatical Errors

• Avoid treating the Grasshopper and Cricket as unrelated; focus on continuity.
• Do not view 'frost' as a character; treat it as a seasonal change.
• Maintain Tense Consistency: Use the Simple Present Tense throughout.
• Ensure Subject-Verb Agreement (e.g., 'The insects provide' not 'provides').
• Use precise vocabulary like 'vibrant' or 'melodious' instead of 'good'.
