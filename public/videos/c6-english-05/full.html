Literary Devices: Identifying Rhyme Scheme, Alliteration, and Personification

• Rhyme Scheme: The pattern of sounds at the end of poetic lines.
• Alliteration: Repetition of initial consonant sounds in nearby words.
• Personification: Giving human traits to non-human objects or ideas.
• Rhyme Scheme Example: A-A-B-B pattern creates a musical rhythm.
• Alliteration Example: 'Soft, silent shadows' uses the 'S' sound.

Poetic Structure: Understanding Stanzas, Lines, and Rhythm

• Stanza: A group of lines forming a single unit of thought, similar to a paragraph.
• Line: A single row of words; the poet controls where a line ends for effect.
• Enjambment: When a line carries over to the next without punctuation.
• Rhythm: The musical pattern created by stressed and unstressed syllables.
• Structure: The combination of lines, stanzas, and rhythm creates the poem's flow.

Common Pitfalls: Avoiding Misinterpretations and Grammatical Errors

• Distinguish between self-centered wishes and selfless prayers for a friend.
• Use the Literary Present Tense: 'The poet says' instead of 'The poet said'.
• Ensure Subject-Verb Agreement: 'The collection is' not 'The collection are'.
• Use Adverbs to describe actions: 'Describes beautifully' instead of 'Describes beautiful'.
• Replace vague words like 'good' with precise terms like 'profound' or 'evocative'.
