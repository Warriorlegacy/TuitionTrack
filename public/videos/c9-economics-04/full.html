Introduction: Why Food Security Matters and the Concept of Food Availability vs. Accessibility

• Food Security: Access to sufficient, safe, and nutritious food at all times.
• Food Availability: Total food production and stocks within the country.
• Food Accessibility: Physical reach and ability to obtain food.
• Food Affordability: Having the purchasing power to buy necessary food.
• Security requires all three: Availability, Accessibility, and Affordability.

Dimensions of Food Security: Availability, Accessibility, and Affordability

• Availability: Total physical presence of food in the country.
• Accessibility: Food must be within reach of every individual.
• Affordability: Food must be priced reasonably for the poor.
• A crisis can impact all three dimensions simultaneously.
• Government uses PDS to ensure accessibility and affordability.

Vulnerability Analysis: Identifying Who is Food Insecure

• Vulnerability: The risk of being unable to access food during shortages.
• Social Groups: Higher risk among SC, ST, OBC, and women in many households.
• Geographical Regions: States like Bihar and Odisha face higher vulnerability.
• Economic Status: Low-income groups and landless laborers are most at risk.
• Targeted Policy: Identifying these groups helps in effective government aid.

The Role of Famine: Historical Case Studies and the Impact of Food Scarcity

• Famine: Extreme widespread hunger and high mortality rates.
• Distinction: Food shortage vs. systemic famine collapse.
• Case Study: The 1943 Bengal Famine and administrative failures.
• Economic Impact: Price shocks and the cycle of malnutrition.
• Long-term effects: Loss of assets and persistent poverty.

The Green Revolution: Technological Breakthroughs, High Yielding Varieties, and Agriculture

• Introduction of High Yielding Variety (HYV) seeds for wheat and rice
• Requirement of modern inputs: Chemical fertilizers and pesticides
• Increased dependence on advanced irrigation systems and tube wells

The Public Distribution System (PDS): Mechanism, Ration Shops, and Buffer Stocks

• PDS: A bridge between food production and food consumption.
• MSP: Minimum Support Price paid to farmers during procurement.
• Fair Price Shops: Local ration shops providing essential goods at low rates.
• Issue Price: The subsidized price at which food is sold to the public.
• Buffer Stock: Strategic reserves used to manage scarcity and inflation.

Part 7 of 12 · ~5 min

Food Security in India: Role of Government, FCI, and the Buffer Stock Strategy

• Government role: Ensuring food availability and accessibility.
• F-C-I: Procures surplus food grains from farmers.
• Minimum Support Price (M-S-P): A price guarantee for farmers.
• Buffer Stock: A reserve of grains to handle emergencies.
• The Cycle: Procurement -> Buffer Stock -> Public Distribution.

A 3D flow diagram showing grain moving from a farm to an F-C-I warehouse, then into a large buffer stock silo, and finally being distributed to a village ration shop.

Part 8 of 12 · ~5 min

Critical Evaluation of PDS: Challenges, Leakages, and the Impact of Targeted PDS (TPDS)

• Major PDS Challenges: Leakages, diversion to open market, and poor grain quality.
• Inclusion Errors: Non-poor individuals receiving subsidized food benefits.
• Exclusion Errors: Needy families being left out due to lack of documentation.
• Targeted PDS (TPDS): Introduced in 1997 to focus on BPL and APL categories.
• Goal of TPDS: Providing highly subsidized grains to the most vulnerable groups.

Current Challenges: Climate Change, Wastage, and the Need for Sustainable Food Systems

• Climate Change: Unpredictable monsoons, heatwaves, and extreme weather events.
• Post-Harvest Loss: Damage due to poor storage and inadequate transport infrastructure.
• Consumer Wastage: Significant food loss in households, hotels, and social events.
• Soil and Water Crisis: Impact of excessive chemical use and groundwater depletion.
• Sustainable Solutions: Climate-smart agriculture, organic farming, and better cold chains.
