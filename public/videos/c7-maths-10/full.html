Foundations: Understanding Constants, Variables, and Algebraic Terms

• Constants: Fixed values that do not change (e.g., 5, -10, 100)
• Variables: Symbols like x, y, or z representing unknown or changing values
• Algebraic Term: A single unit formed by constants, variables, and operations
• Coefficient: The numerical factor multiplied by a variable (e.g., 5 in 5x)
• Note: A variable with no visible number has an implied coefficient of 1

Anatomy of an Expression: Coefficients, Factors, and Like vs Unlike Terms

• Coefficient: The numerical factor multiplied by a variable.
• Factors: The individual components multiplied to form a term.
• Like Terms: Terms with identical variables and identical powers.
• Unlike Terms: Terms with different variables or different powers.
• Rule: Only Like Terms can be added or subtracted.

Part 4 of 12 · ~5 min

Classification Mastery: Monomials, Binomials, Trinomials, and Polynomials

• Monomial: An expression with only one term (e.g., 5xy)
• Binomial: An expression with exactly two unlike terms (e.g., x + y)
• Trinomial: An expression with exactly three unlike terms (e.g., x + y + z)
• Polynomial: The general term for expressions with one or more terms
• Pro-tip: Always simplify by combining like terms before classifying

The Art of Addition: Combining Like Terms and Summing Expressions

• Golden Rule: Only like terms can be added together.
• Step 1: Write the expressions together in one line.
• Step 2: Group terms with identical variables and powers.
• Step 3: Add the numerical coefficients of the grouped terms.
• Warning: Do not change the variables or their exponents during addition.

Part 6 of 12 · ~5 min

The Art of Subtraction: Handling Signs and Subtracting Expressions

• Subtraction is the same as adding the opposite of an expression.
• A minus sign outside a bracket flips the sign of every term inside.
• Step 1: Rewrite the expression by removing brackets and changing signs.
• Step 2: Group the like terms together.
• Step 3: Combine the coefficients to find the final result.

Algebraic Identities: Deriving and Understanding Basic Patterns

• Identity: An equality true for all values of the variable.
• Identity 1: (a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b²
• Identity 2: (a - b)² = a² - 2ab + b²
• Identity 3: (a + b)(a - b) = a² - b²
• Identities act as mathematical shortcuts for complex multiplication.

Multiplication Part 1: Distributive Law and Monomial by Polynomial

• Distributive Law: a(b + c) = ab + ac
• Multiply the monomial by EVERY term inside the bracket
• When multiplying variables, add their exponents
• Watch your signs: negative times negative equals positive
• Coefficient times coefficient; Variable times variable

Part 9 of 12 · ~5 min

Multiplication Part 2: Binomial by Binomial and Advanced Expansion

• Binomial by Binomial multiplication uses the FOIL method.
• FOIL: First, Outer, Inner, and Last terms.
• Distribute every term of the first expression to the second.
• Always combine like terms to simplify the final expression.
• Watch your signs when distributing negative coefficients.

Common Pitfalls: Avoiding Sign Errors and Calculation Mistakes

• Subtracting an expression means adding its additive inverse.
• Always distribute the negative sign to EVERY term inside brackets.
• Never combine unlike terms like x and y into xy.
• Remember that a lone variable like x has a coefficient of one.
• Perform a sanity check to ensure your final answer makes sense.
