Newton's First Law: Concept of Inertia, Mass vs. Weight, and Equilibrium

• Newton's First Law: Objects resist changes in their state of motion.
• Inertia: The inherent property of matter to resist acceleration.
• Mass vs. Weight: Mass is constant matter; Weight is a variable force (W = mg).
• Types of Inertia: Rest, Motion, and Direction.
• Equilibrium: Occurs when the vector sum of all external forces is zero.

Newton's Second Law: Momentum, F=ma Derivation, and Impulse-Momentum Theorem

• Linear Momentum (p) = mass (m) × velocity (v)
• Newton's Second Law: F is proportional to the rate of change of momentum
• Mathematical Derivation: F = m × a
• Impulse (J) = Force (F) × time (t) = Change in momentum (Δp)
• Increasing contact time reduces the impact force

Part 4 of 12 · ~5 min

Newton's Third Law: Action-Reaction Pairs and Conservation of Momentum

• Newton's Third Law: Action and reaction forces act on different bodies.
• Action-Reaction pairs are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction.
• Conservation of Momentum: Total momentum remains constant in an isolated system.
• Mathematical derivation: Change in momentum of A plus B equals zero.
• Applications: Rocket propulsion, collisions, and recoil of guns.

Part 5 of 12 · ~5 min

Common Pitfalls: Misinterpreting Third Law and Force Pair Identification

• Action-reaction pairs always act on two different objects.
• Action and reaction forces can NEVER cancel each other out.
• Normal force and Gravity are NOT an action-reaction pair.
• To find a pair, swap the objects: A on B implies B on A.
• Only draw forces acting ON the object in a Free Body Diagram.

Free Body Diagrams (FBD): Systematic Approach to Vector Decomposition

• Isolate the object from its surroundings
• Represent the object as a single point mass
• Identify all external forces: Gravity, Normal, Tension, Friction
• Decompose angled forces using F cos theta and F sin theta
• Apply Newton's Second Law independently for X and Y axes

Applications of Laws: Connected Bodies, Pulley Systems, and Tension

• Tension is a pulling force transmitted through strings or ropes.
• Assume strings are massless and inextensible for standard problems.
• Connected bodies in a system share the same acceleration.
• Use individual Free Body Diagrams to find internal forces like tension.
• Atwood Machine: Solve using simultaneous equations for mass and tension.

Friction I: Static vs. Kinetic Friction, Laws of Friction, and Limiting Friction

• Friction: A contact force opposing relative motion or tendency of motion.
• Static Friction: Self-adjusting force acting when there is no relative motion.
• Limiting Friction: The maximum value of static friction before motion begins.
• Kinetic Friction: Constant force acting during actual relative motion.
• Laws: Friction depends on Normal Force and material, not surface area.

Friction II: Rolling Friction, Angle of Repose, and Friction as a Necessary Evil

• Rolling friction is significantly lower than sliding friction due to reduced contact area.
• Rolling Friction Formula: F_r = mu_r * N
• Angle of Repose: The maximum angle of an incline before an object begins to slide.
• Mathematical Relation: tan(theta) = mu_s (Coefficient of Static Friction)
• Friction is a 'Necessary Evil': It causes energy loss but enables controlled motion.

Circular Motion: Centripetal Force, Banking of Roads, and Friction in Turns

• Centripetal Force: F equals m v squared over r, directed toward the center.
• Flat Road Turn: Static friction provides the necessary centripetal force.
• Maximum Speed on Flat Road: v equals square root of mu times r times g.
• Banking of Roads: Tilting the road to use the normal force component for turning.
• Ideal Banking Angle: tan theta equals v squared over r.

Solving Complex Word Problems: Multi-Body Systems and Inclined Planes

• Connected bodies share the same acceleration if the string is taut.
• Always draw a separate Free Body Diagram for every mass in the system.
• Use simultaneous equations to solve for unknown tension and acceleration.
• Resolve gravity on an incline into mass times g sine theta and g cosine theta.
• Systematic Approach: Isolate, Decompose, and Apply F equals ma.
