Introduction: Why Hydrocarbons Matter, Classification, and Structural Isomerism

• Hydrocarbons: Organic compounds made of Carbon and Hydrogen only
• Aliphatic: Straight, branched, or non-aromatic cyclic chains
• Saturated (Alkanes) vs. Unsaturated (Alkenes and Alkynes)
• Aromatic Hydrocarbons: Stable cyclic structures like Benzene
• Structural Isomerism: Same molecular formula, different connectivity

Alkanes I: Nomenclature, Preparation Methods, and Reaction Mechanisms

• Alkanes are saturated hydrocarbons also known as paraffins
• IUPAC naming: Longest chain, lowest locants, alphabetical order
• Wurtz Reaction: Alkyl halides plus Sodium in dry ether
• Decarboxylation: Carboxylic acid salts plus Soda Lime
• Free Radical Substitution: Initiation, Propagation, and Termination

Part 3 of 12 · ~4 min

Alkanes II: Physical Properties, Chemical Reactions, and Conformations of Ethane

• Boiling points increase with molecular mass and decrease with branching.
• Alkanes are non-polar and rely on weak London dispersion forces.
• Halogenation occurs via a free radical substitution mechanism.
• Complete combustion produces carbon dioxide, water, and heat.
• Staggered conformation is more stable than eclipsed due to lower torsional strain.

Part 4 of 12 · ~4 min

Alkenes I: Nomenclature, Structure, and Methods of Preparation

• Alkenes are unsaturated hydrocarbons with C=C double bonds
• IUPAC suffix is 'ene'; double bond gets lowest locant number
• Double bond consists of one sigma bond and one pi bond
• Geometry around double-bonded carbons is trigonal planar
• Preparation: Dehydration of alcohols, Dehydrohalogenation, and Dehalogenation

A 3D molecular model of ethene rotating to show the sideways overlap of p-orbitals forming the pi bond.

Part 5 of 12 · ~4 min

Alkenes II: Stereochemistry, Physical Properties, and Electrophilic Addition Reactions

• Restricted rotation due to pi-bond prevents free movement.
• Cis-isomers: identical groups on the same side; Trans: opposite sides.
• Boiling points increase with mass but decrease with branching.
• Electrophilic addition: Pi electrons attack electron-deficient species.
• Markovnikov's Rule: Hydrogen attaches to the carbon with more hydrogens.

Part 6 of 12 · ~4 min

Alkenes III: Oxidation, Ozonolysis, and Polymerization Reactions

• Baeyer's Reagent: Cold, dilute alkaline KMnO4 forms diols (glycols).
• Oxidative Cleavage: Hot, concentrated KMnO4 breaks the C=C bond completely.
• Ozonolysis: Uses Ozone followed by Zn/H2O to locate double bond positions.
• Ozonolysis Products: Cleavage yields aldehydes or ketones based on substituents.
• Addition Polymerization: Monomers link via pi-bond opening to form long chains.

Part 7 of 12 · ~4 min

Alkynes I: Nomenclature, Structure, and Methods of Preparation

• Alkynes contain at least one carbon-carbon triple bond.
• IUPAC suffix for alkynes is 'yne'.
• Triple bond consists of one sigma bond and two pi bonds.
• Hybridization is sp, resulting in a linear geometry (180 degrees).
• Preparation via dehydrohalogenation of vicinal or geminal dihalides.

A 3D molecular model of ethyne rotating to show the 180-degree linear bond angle and the overlap of sp and p orbitals.

Part 8 of 12 · ~4 min

Alkynes II: Acidic Character, Physical Properties, and Addition Reactions

• sp hybridization increases acidity due to high s-character
• Acidity order: Ethyne > Propyne > But-2-yne
• Lindlar's catalyst produces cis-alkenes; Na/NH3 produces trans-alkenes
• Halogenation leads to tetrahaloalkanes with excess reagent
• Hydration of alkynes via tautomerism produces aldehydes or ketones

Part 9 of 12 · ~4 min

Aromatic Hydrocarbons I: Benzene Structure, Resonance, and Nomenclature

• Benzene molecular formula: C-H-six
• All C-C bond lengths are equal at 139 pm
• Resonance: Delocalization of pi-electrons in a ring
• Hückel's Rule: Cyclic, planar, and 4n plus 2 pi-electrons
• Nomenclature: Using ortho, meta, and para for substituents

A 3D rotating benzene ring transitioning from two Kekule structures into a smooth, continuous electron cloud ring.

Part 10 of 12 · ~3 min

Aromatic Hydrocarbons II: Electrophilic Substitution Reactions (Nitration, Sulphonation, Halogenation)

• Benzene undergoes substitution rather than addition to maintain aromaticity.
• The reaction proceeds via a resonance-stabilized sigma complex intermediate.
• Nitration: Uses nitrating mixture to generate the nitronium ion, N-O-plus.
• Sulphonation: Uses fuming sulphuric acid; the electrophile is S-O-three.
• Halogenation: Requires a Lewis acid catalyst like AlCl-three to generate Cl-plus.

A 3D molecular animation showing a benzene ring being attacked by a nitronium ion, transitioning into a non-aromatic sigma complex before losing a proton to return to a stable benzene structure.

Part 11 of 12 · ~4 min

Aromatic Hydrocarbons III: Friedel-Crafts Reactions and Polysubstitution Patterns

• Friedel-Crafts Alkylation: Benzene + Alkyl Halide + AlCl3 yields Alkylbenzene
• Risk of Carbocation Rearrangement in Alkylation reactions
• Friedel-Crafts Acylation: Introduction of Acyl groups to form Aromatic Ketones
• Ortho-Para Directors: Activating groups like Alkyl and Hydroxyl
• Meta Directors: Deactivating groups like Nitro and Carbonyl
