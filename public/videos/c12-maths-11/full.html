Coordinate Axes and Planes in 3D: Octants, Coordinates, and Distance Formula Derivations

• Three mutually perpendicular axes: X, Y, and Z intersect at the Origin.
• 3D space is divided into 8 regions called Octants.
• A point is represented by a triplet of coordinates: (x, y, z).
• Distance Formula: Square root of [(x2-x1)^2 + (y2-y1)^2 + (z2-z1)^2].
• The 3D distance formula is derived using the Pythagorean Theorem twice.

Direction Cosines and Direction Ratios: Definitions, Properties, and Conversion Logic

• Direction Cosines (l, m, n) are cosines of angles made with x, y, and z axes
• Fundamental Property: l squared + m squared + n squared = 1
• Direction Ratios (a, b, c) are proportional to Direction Cosines
• Direction Ratios are not unique; Direction Cosines are unique
• Conversion: l = a / sqrt(a^2 + b^2 + c^2)

The Vector Approach to 3D: Representing Points and Lines using Position Vectors

• Position vector r = x i + y j + z k represents a point (x, y, z)
• Vector AB = Position Vector of B minus Position Vector of A
• Displacement vector components: (x2 - x1) i + (y2 - y1) j + (z2 - z1) k
• A line is defined by a fixed point and a direction vector
• Vector equation of a line: r = a + lambda v

Equation of a Line in 3D: Vector and Cartesian Forms (Derivations and Geometry)

• Line definition: A fixed point and a direction vector are required.
• Vector Form: r = a + lambda b (where a is position, b is direction).
• Cartesian Form: (x - x1)/a = (y - y1)/b = (z - z1)/c.
• Direction Ratios (a, b, c) act as the denominators in Cartesian form.
• A line in 3D can be viewed as the intersection of two planes.

Parametric Form of a Line: Solving for Points on a Line and Intersection Problems

• Parametric Form: r = a + lambda * b
• Parametric coordinates: x = x_n + lambda*a, y = y_n + lambda*b, z = z_n + lambda*c
• Use lambda to find points satisfying plane equations
• Intersection of two lines: Set parametric x, y, and z components equal

Line-Line Relationships: Intersecting, Parallel, Perpendicular, and Skew Lines

• Parallel Lines: Direction ratios are proportional (a1/a2 = b1/b2 = c1/c2)
• Perpendicular Lines: Dot product of direction vectors is zero (a1a2 + b1b2 + c1c2 = 0)
• Intersecting Lines: Lines meet at a single common point
• Skew Lines: Lines are neither parallel nor intersecting; they lie in different planes
• Intersection Test: Solve for parameters lambda and mu using the three coordinate equations

Equation of a Plane: The Normal Form and Vector Representation

• A plane is uniquely defined by a point and a normal vector (perpendicular to the surface).
• Vector Equation: n dot (r - a) = 0, where 'n' is the normal and 'a' is a point on the plane.
• Simplified Vector Form: n dot r = d, where 'd' is a constant scalar.
• Normal Form: h-hat dot r = p, where 'h-hat' is a unit normal vector.
• The constant 'p' represents the perpendicular distance from the origin to the plane.

Equation of a Plane: The Cartesian Form and Derivation from Point-Normal Data

• Required Data: A fixed point (x1, y1, z1) and a normal vector with direction ratios (a, b, c)
• Geometric Condition: The dot product of the normal vector and any vector in the plane is zero
• Vector AP = (x - x1)i + (y - y1)j + (z - z1)k
• Cartesian Equation: a(x - x1) + b(y - y1) + c(z - z1) = 0
• General Form: ax + by + cz = d, where (a, b, c) are the direction ratios of the normal

Plane-Line Interactions: Finding Intersection Points and Angles between Lines and Planes

• Parametric form of a line: x=x0+at, y=y0+bt, z=z0+ct
• Substitute parametric x, y, z into the plane equation Ax+By+Cz+D=0
• Solve for parameter 't' to find the specific point of intersection
• If 't' has no solution, the line is parallel to the plane
• Angle formula: sin(theta) = |Aa + Bb + Cc| / (sqrt(A²+B²+C²) * sqrt(a²+b²+c²))

Angle between Two Planes and Angle between a Line and a Plane: Formulas and Workouts

• Angle between two planes = Angle between their normal vectors
• Formula: cos(theta) = |A1A2 + B1B2 + C1C2| / (n1 * n2)
• Angle between a line and a plane is the complement of the angle with the normal
• Formula: sin(alpha) = |Aa + Bb + Cc| / (magnitude of line * magnitude of normal)
• Always use absolute values to find the acute angle
