Introduction: Why Power Sharing Matters - The Stability of Democracies

• Power sharing is essential for the stability of a diverse democracy.
• Concentration of power leads to social tension and conflict.
• Exclusion of minority groups undermines democratic legitimacy.
• Power sharing prevents the 'tyranny of the majority'.
• Distribution of power ensures all social groups have a stake in governance.

Case Study 1: Belgium - Ethnic Composition and the Model of Accommodation

• Ethnic Composition: 53% Dutch, 40% French, 1% German
• Regional Divide: Dutch majority in Flanders, French in Wallonia
• Brussels Paradox: 75% French-speaking, 25% Dutch-speaking
• Constitutional Amendment: Equal ministers in Central Government
• Community Government: Handles culture, education, and language

Case Study 2: Sri Lanka - Majoritarianism and the Ethnic Conflict

• Ethnic Composition: 74% Sinhala (Buddhist) and 18% Tamil
• Majoritarianism: The belief that the majority should rule without regard for minorities
• 1956 Act: Sinhala recognized as the sole official language
• Preferential Policies: Sinhala dominance in university and government jobs
• Consequences: Rise of Tamil nationalism and a devastating Civil War

Comparative Analysis: Why Belgium Succeeded and Sri Lanka Failed

• Belgium: Policy of Accommodation vs. Sri Lanka: Policy of Majoritarianism
• Belgium: Shared power to prevent conflict; Sri Lanka: Concentrated power to dominate
• The impact of the 1956 Act: Alienation of Sri Lankan Tamils

• Key Lesson: Inclusion leads to unity, while exclusion leads to division

The Concept of Power Sharing: Why is it Desirable? (Prudential vs Moral Reasons)

• Two types of reasons: Prudential and Moral
• Prudential: Based on careful calculation of gains and losses
• Prudential: Reduces social conflict and ensures political stability
• Moral: Based on the intrinsic value of democracy
• Moral: Upholds the principle of citizen participation

A 3D split-screen animation: Left side shows a scale balancing 'Conflict' vs 'Stability' (Prudential); Right side shows a glowing heart icon labeled 'Democratic Spirit' (Moral).

Part 6 of 12 · ~4 min

Horizontal Distribution of Power: Legislature, Executive, and Judiciary

• Horizontal distribution: Power shared among organs at the same level
• The Legislature: The organ responsible for making laws
• The Executive: The organ responsible for implementing laws
• The Judiciary: The organ responsible for interpreting laws and justice
• System of Checks and Balances: Each organ limits the power of others

A 3D diagram showing three interconnected gears labeled Legislature, Executive, and Judiciary, rotating together to maintain a balanced scale.

Part 7 of 12 · ~3 min

Vertical Distribution of Power: Federalism and Levels of Government

• Vertical power sharing involves different levels or tiers of government.
• Power flows from the Central Government down to State and Local levels.
• The Central Government handles national issues like defense and currency.
• State and Local governments manage regional and grassroots-level affairs.
• Jurisdiction of each level is clearly defined by the Constitution.

A 3D pyramid animation where the top tier glows (Central), the middle tier expands (State), and the base layer pulses (Local), with arrows showing the flow of authority.

Power Sharing Among Social Groups: Reserved Constituencies and Community Government

• Power sharing among social groups prevents social conflict.
• Reserved Constituencies: Seats for weaker sections like SC and ST.
• Ensures representation for marginalized groups in legislatures.
• Community Government: Elected by specific linguistic or social groups.
• Community Government handles cultural, educational, and language issues.

Power Sharing Among Political Parties, Pressure Groups, and Movements

• Political parties compete to ensure power doesn't stay in one hand
• Coalition governments represent power sharing through compromise
• Pressure groups influence government decisions to protect specific interests
• Methods include rallies, strikes, and media campaigns to voice demands
• Social movements act as a vital check on government authority
