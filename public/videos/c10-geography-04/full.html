Types of Farming I: Primitive Subsistence vs. Intensive Subsistence

• Primitive Subsistence: Small patches, traditional tools, and family consumption.
• Slash and Burn: Clearing forest land, burning vegetation, and moving when soil fails.
• Local Names: Jhumming (NE India), Bewar (MP), and Dipa (Andaman & Nicobar).
• Intensive Subsistence: High population pressure and high use of biochemical inputs.
• Intensive Subsistence: Heavy reliance on irrigation and fertilizers for maximum yield.

Types of Farming II: Commercial Farming & Plantation Agriculture

• Commercial Farming: Aimed at profit through market sale
• Key Inputs: HYV seeds, chemical fertilizers, and machinery
• Plantation Agriculture: A single crop grown on a large scale
• Plantations act as an interface between agriculture and industry
• Requires high capital, organized labor, and transport networks

Cropping Patterns: Rabi, Kharif, and Zaid Seasons with Comparative Analysis

• Kharif Season: Sown with monsoon (June-July), requires high heat and rainfall.
• Rabi Season: Sown in winter (Oct-Dec), requires cool growth and warm ripening.
• Zaid Season: Short summer season (March-June) between Rabi and Kharif.
• Kharif Examples: Rice, Maize, Cotton, Groundnut, and Jowar.
• Rabi Examples: Wheat, Barley, Peas, Gram, and Mustard.

Major Crops I: Grains (Rice, Wheat, Millets, Maize) - Conditions & Distribution

• Rice: Kharif crop, high temp (>25°C), high humidity, >150cm rainfall.
• Wheat: Rabi crop, cool growing season, 50-75cm moderate rainfall.
• Millets: Nutritious coarse grains; Jowar, Bajra, and Ragi.
• Maize: Kharif crop, grows in old alluvial soil, used for food and fodder.
• Distribution: Heavily dependent on soil type, temperature, and rainfall.

Major Crops II: Food Legumes and Oilseeds - Growth Requirements

• Pulses: Primary protein source and nitrogen-fixing legumes
• Pulse Seasons: Gram (Rabi) and Tur/Arhar (Kharif)
• Oilseeds: Used for cooking, soaps, and industrial fuel
• Groundnut: A major Kharif oilseed requiring 25 degrees Celsius
• Rabi Oilseeds: Mustard, Linseed, and Castor seed

Major Crops III: Cash Crops (Sugarcane, Tea, Coffee) - Economic Impact

• Sugarcane: Tropical/Subtropical, 21 to 27 degrees Celsius, major revenue source.
• Tea: Plantation crop, requires frequent showers and well-drained soil.
• Coffee: Arabica variety, majorly grown in Karnataka, Kerala, and Tamil Nadu.
• Economic Impact: High foreign exchange earnings through global exports.
• Industrial Linkage: Provides raw materials for sugar and beverage industries.

Non-Food Crops: Rubber, Cotton, and Jute - Industrial Significance

• Rubber: Equatorial crop, requires >25°C and >200 cm rainfall.
• Rubber Major States: Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, and Assam.
• Cotton: Grows best in Black Soil; requires 210 frost-free days.
• Cotton Major States: Maharashtra, Gujarat, and Madhya Pradesh.
• Jute: The 'Golden Fibre'; thrives in fertile flood plains like West Bengal.

Technological and Institutional Reforms in Indian Agriculture

• Institutional Reforms: Land reforms, abolition of Zamindari, and consolidation of holdings.
• Credit Reforms: Cooperative societies, Regional Rural Banks, and easy bank loans.
• Technological Reforms: Green Revolution, HYV seeds, and modern machinery.
• Farmer Support: Kissan Credit Card (KCC) and Personal Accident Insurance Scheme (PAIS).
• Price Security: Minimum Support Price (MSP) and weather/market forecasting services.

Contribution of Agriculture to Economy & Food Security Challenges

• Agriculture provides livelihood to over 50% of the Indian population.
• Key contributor to G.D.P. and raw materials for industries.
• Three pillars of Food Security: Availability, Accessibility, and Affordability.
• Role of Public Distribution System (P.D.S.) in food access.
• Major challenges: Population growth, soil degradation, and climate change.
