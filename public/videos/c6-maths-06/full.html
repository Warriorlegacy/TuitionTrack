The Number Line: Visualizing Positive Numbers, Zero, and Negative Numbers

• Zero is the origin: neither positive nor negative.
• Positive integers are located to the right of zero.
• Negative integers are located to the left of zero.
• The distance between consecutive integers is always one unit.
• Moving right increases value; moving left decreases value.

Understanding Absolute Value and the Concept of Opposites

• Opposites are numbers that are the same distance from zero but in different directions.
• Adding an integer and its opposite always results in zero.
• Absolute value is the distance of a number from zero on the number line.
• Absolute value is always non-negative (zero or positive).
• Notation: The absolute value of x is written as |x|.

A 3D character walks five steps right to +5 and five steps left to -5, then a magnifying glass zooms in on the distance from zero to show |5| and |-5| both equal 5.

Comparing and Ordering Integers: Using Inequality Symbols

• Greater than symbol (>) always opens toward the larger value.
• Less than symbol (<) points toward the smaller value.
• On a number line, numbers to the right are always greater.
• For negative numbers, the one closer to zero is greater.
• Ascending order: Smallest to Largest; Descending: Largest to Smallest.

Part 5 of 12 · ~5 min

Addition of Integers: Rules for Same Signs and Different Signs

• Same Signs: Add the values and keep the common sign.
• Different Signs: Subtract the smaller value from the larger value.
• Sign Rule: Use the sign of the number with the larger absolute value.
• Example: (-3) + (-4) = -7 (Same signs, add and keep negative).
• Example: (-10) + 16 = +6 (Different signs, subtract and keep positive).

Subtraction of Integers: The Additive Inverse Method and Visualizing on Number Line

• Subtraction is the addition of the additive inverse
• Additive inverse of 'a' is '-a'
• Rule: Change subtraction to addition and flip the sign of the second number
• Subtracting a negative is the same as adding a positive
• On a number line: Subtracting positive moves left; subtracting negative moves right

Common Pitfalls: Sign Errors, Zero Properties, and Misconceptions

• Double Negatives: Subtracting a negative is the same as adding.
• Negative Comparison: Further left on the number line means smaller value.
• Zero Property: Zero is the additive identity; it doesn't change the value.
• Absolute Value: Always represents a non-negative distance from zero.
• Context Matters: Words like 'depth' or 'loss' indicate negative signs.
