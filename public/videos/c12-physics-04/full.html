Introduction: The Invisible Force - Why Moving Charges Matter & Oersted's Discovery

• Electricity and magnetism were once thought to be separate forces.
• Hans Christian Oersted discovered electromagnetism in 1820.
• A moving electric current deflects a magnetic compass needle.
• Stationary charges produce electric fields; moving charges produce magnetic fields.
• Magnetism is a direct consequence of the motion of electric charges.

Magnetic Force on a Moving Charge: Lorentz Force, Derivations, and Cyclotron Motion

• Magnetic Force: F = qvB sin(theta)
• Lorentz Force: F = qE + q(v cross B)
• Radius of circular path: r = mv / qB
• Cyclotron Frequency: f = qB / 2 pi m
• Helical motion occurs when velocity is not perpendicular to B

Part 3 of 12 · ~4 min

Motion in a Magnetic Field: Radius, Frequency, and Helical Trajectories

• Perpendicular motion: Magnetic force acts as centripetal force
• Radius formula: r = (m * v) / (q * B)
• Cyclotron frequency: f = (q * B) / (2 * pi * m)
• Frequency is independent of velocity and radius
• Helical motion: Result of combined circular and linear motion

Force on a Current-Carrying Conductor in a Uniform Magnetic Field & Ampere's Force Law

• Force on a conductor: F = I L B sin theta
• Direction determined by Fleming's Left-Hand Rule
• Force is maximum when wire is perpendicular to B
• Ampere's Force Law: F = integral of (I dl cross B)
• Net force depends on the displacement vector in a uniform field

Magnetic Field due to a Straight Wire and Circular Loop: Biot-Savart Law Applications

• Biot-Savart Law: dB = (mu0 / 4pi) * I * dl * sin(theta) / r^2
• Field of an infinite straight wire: B = mu0 * I / (2 * pi * R)
• Field at the center of a circular loop: B = mu0 * I / (2 * a)
• Field on the axis of a loop: B = (mu0 * I * a^2) / [2 * (a^2 + x^2)^(3/2)]
• Direction determined by the Right-Hand Thumb Rule

Part 6 of 12 · ~4 min

Ampere's Circuital Law: Derivations, Solenoids, and Toroids

• Ampere's Circuital Law: Integral of B dot d-l = mu-naught times I
• Solenoid Field Derivation: B = mu-naught times n times I
• n = number of turns per unit length (N/L)
• Toroid Field Derivation: B = (mu-naught times N times I) / (2-pi-r)
• Magnetic field is confined inside the solenoid and toroid cores

A 3D animation showing a rectangular Amperian loop sliding through a solenoid, transitioning into a glowing circular toroid with magnetic field lines following the ring.

Part 7 of 12 · ~4 min

Force Between Two Parallel Current-Carrying Conductors & Definition of the Ampere

• Magnetic field from wire 1: B = (mu naught * I1) / (2 * pi * d)
• Force per unit length: F/L = (mu naught * I1 * I2) / (2 * pi * d)
• Same direction currents: Attraction | Opposite direction: Repulsion
• Definition of Ampere: Force of 2 x 10^-7 N/m at 1 meter distance
• Force is inversely proportional to the distance 'd'

Torque on a Current Loop in a Uniform Magnetic Field & Magnetic Dipole Moment

• Forces on loop sides create a turning effect called Torque
• Torque formula: Tau equals M A B sine theta
• Magnetic Dipole Moment M equals current I times area A
• Direction of M is given by the Right-Hand Rule
• Vector form of torque: Tau equals M cross B

Moving Coil Galvanometer: Construction, Working, Current Sensitivity, and Conversion

• Radial magnetic field ensures maximum torque at all positions
• Equilibrium condition: Magnetic Torque = Restoring Torque (NIAB = kθ)
• Current Sensitivity = NAB / k
• Voltage Sensitivity = NAB / kR
• Ammeter: Low resistance (shunt) in parallel; Voltmeter: High resistance in series

Magnetic Field of a Moving Charge: Relativistic Intuition and Field Line Symmetry

• Magnetism is a relativistic consequence of moving electric charges.
• Length contraction distorts the spherical electric field of a moving charge.
• The magnetic field arises from the 'warping' of electric field lines.
• Field lines bunch up in the plane perpendicular to the direction of motion.
• Electricity and magnetism are unified as the electromagnetic force.
