Arithmetic Progression (AP) I: Definition, common difference, and the nth term formula derivation

• Arithmetic Progression (AP): A sequence with a constant difference between terms.
• Common Difference (d): The fixed value added to each term to get the next.
• Notation: 'a' represents the first term; 'd' represents the common difference.
• nth Term Formula: a_n = a + (n - 1)d
• The number of differences added is always one less than the term position.

Arithmetic Progression (AP) II: Sum of n terms, properties, and complex word problems

• Sum formula 1: S_n = n/2 [a + l]
• Sum formula 2: S_n = n/2 [2a + (n-1)d]
• The sum of an AP is always a quadratic in n: An^2 + Bn
• Property: a_n = S_n - S_{n-1}
• Symmetry: Sum of equidistant terms is constant

Geometric Progression (GP) I: Definition, common ratio, and the nth term formula derivation

• Geometric Progression: A sequence where terms have a constant ratio.
• Common Ratio (r): The fixed multiplier between consecutive terms.
• General Form: a, ar, ar^2, ar^3, ..., ar^(n-1)
• nth Term Formula: a_n = a * r^(n-1)
• Example: If a=2 and r=3, the 6th term is 2 * 3^5 = 486.

Part 6 of 12 · ~4 min

Geometric Progression (GP) II: Sum of n terms, sum of infinite GP, and practical applications

• Sum of n terms formula: S_n = a(1 - r^n) / (1 - r) for r ≠ 1
• If r = 1, the sum of n terms is simply n times a
• Sum of infinite GP: S_infinity = a / (1 - r) only if |r| < 1
• Divergent series: Sum grows to infinity if |r| ≥ 1
• Real-world use: Modeling bouncing balls and compound growth

Geometric Mean (GM) and Arithmetic Mean (AM): Definitions, properties, and relationship between them

• Arithmetic Mean (AM) of a and b is (a + b) / 2
• Geometric Mean (GM) of a and b is square root of (a * b)
• For n numbers, AM is Sum/n and GM is n-th root of Product
• Fundamental Inequality: AM >= GM for positive real numbers
• Equality (AM = GM) holds only when all numbers are equal

Common Pitfalls: Identifying errors in series notation, formula misuse, and algebraic mistakes

• Check Sigma notation limits: Always verify the lower and upper bounds.
• Infinite GP Condition: Ensure the common ratio 'r' is between -1 and 1.
• Counting Terms: Remember to add one when calculating 'n' in an AP.
• Algebraic Expansion: Correctly expand terms like (k+1) squared before summing.
• Term vs. Sum: Distinguish clearly between a_n and S_n in word problems.
