Coordinate Planes and Axes: The Octants and Sign Conventions

• Three mutually perpendicular axes: x, y, and z intersect at the origin.
• The three axes divide 3D space into eight distinct regions called octants.
• A point is represented by an ordered triple: (x, y, z).
• The sign of x, y, and z determines the specific octant of the point.
• Points on the axes or planes have at least one coordinate equal to zero.

Coordinates of a Point in 3D Space: Mapping and Representation

• A point in 3D is represented by an ordered triple: (x, y, z)
• x is the abscissa, y is the ordinate, and z is the z-coordinate
• Coordinates are found by projecting the point onto the three coordinate planes
• The order of coordinates is critical; (x, y, z) is not equal to (z, y, x)
• The signs of (x, y, z) determine which of the eight octants the point occupies

Distance Formula in 3D: Derivation and Geometric Interpretations

• Goal: Find the length of line segment PQ between two points in 3D space
• Construct a rectangular box using the differences in x, y, and z coordinates
• Step 1: Use 2D Pythagorean Theorem to find the diagonal of the base
• Step 2: Use the base diagonal and height to find the space diagonal
• Final Formula: Distance = square root of [(x2-x1)² + (y2-y1)² + (z2-z1)²]

Section Formula: Internal and External Division of a Line Segment

• Internal Division: Point R lies between P and Q
• External Division: Point R lies on the extension of PQ
• Internal Formula: (m1*x2 + m2*x1) / (m1 + m2)
• External Formula: (m1*x2 - m2*x1) / (m1 - m2)
• Apply the same logic separately for x, y, and z coordinates

Midpoint and Centroid: Special Cases and Geometric Properties

• Midpoint Formula: Average of the coordinates of two endpoints
• Midpoint M = ((x1+x2)/2, (y1+y2)/2, (z1+z2)/2)
• Centroid G: The geometric center where medians intersect
• Centroid G = ((x1+x2+x3)/3, (y1+y2+y3)/3, (z1+z2+z3)/3)
• Property: Centroid divides each median in a 2:1 ratio

Common Pitfalls: Sign Errors, Octant Confusion, and Calculation Traps

• Use parentheses when substituting negative coordinates to avoid sign errors
• Remember: squaring a negative coordinate always results in a positive value
• Check all three signs (x, y, z) to correctly identify the octant
• In external division, use the subtraction formula and watch the denominator
• Always subtract coordinates before squaring them in the distance formula
