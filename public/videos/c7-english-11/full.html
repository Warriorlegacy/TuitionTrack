Literary Devices II: Rhyme Scheme, Rhythm, and Alliteration

• Rhyme Scheme: The pattern of rhyming sounds at the end of lines.
• How to label: Use letters like A, B, C to map the pattern.
• Rhythm: The 'heartbeat' created by stressed and unstressed syllables.
• Alliteration: Repetition of consonant sounds at the start of nearby words.
• Purpose: These devices create mood, music, and mimic movement.

Common Mistakes: Misinterpreting Tone and Literary Devices

• Tone: Avoid 'scientific'; aim for 'playful' and 'whimsical'.
• Personification: Look for human intentions like 'teasing'.
• Metaphor vs. Simile: No 'like' or 'as' means it is a metaphor.
• Alliteration: Repetition of initial consonant sounds for rhythm.
• Avoid Literalism: Distinguish between facts and poetic imagery.
