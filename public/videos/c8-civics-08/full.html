• Social Justice aims to create fairness and equal opportunities for all.
• Democracy requires both legal order and social equity to function.
• Laws must protect the vulnerable to achieve true social justice.
• Justice means ensuring dignity for every citizen regardless of background.

The Concept of Equity vs. Equality: Bridging the Gap in Social Justice

• Equality: Providing the exact same resources to every individual.
• Equity: Providing specific resources to ensure equal outcomes.
• The 'Stool Analogy': Why same treatment doesn't always mean fairness.
• Social Justice: Addressing different starting points in society.
• Protective Discrimination: Using equity to bridge historical gaps.

A 3D animation of three diverse characters of different heights trying to reach fruit on a high tree; first, they all get the same ladder (Equality) and fail, then they get different sized ladders (Equity) and all succeed.

• Welfare Schemes: Providing essential services like food, health, and education.
• Public Distribution System (PDS): Ensuring food security for the marginalized.
• Affirmative Action: Correcting historical wrongs through representation.

The Judiciary's Role: Enforcing Laws and Protecting Social Justice

• The Judiciary acts as the guardian of the Constitution and the Rule of Law.
• Judicial Review: The power to strike down laws that violate fundamental rights.
• Public Interest Litigation (PIL): Allowing citizens to fight for the marginalized.
• Suo Motu Cognizance: The court's ability to take action on its own initiative.
• Ensuring equality by checking the arbitrary use of power by the government.

Common Obstacles: Barriers to Accessing Justice and Legal Literacy

• Economic Barriers: High costs of legal fees and lost wages.
• Lack of Legal Literacy: Unawareness of rights and legal procedures.
• Social Discrimination: Prejudice based on caste, gender, or religion.
• Geographical Hurdles: Distance from courts and complex procedures.
• Psychological Barriers: Fear and intimidation caused by the legal system.

Common Mistakes: Misconceptions about Law, Rights, and Social Justice

• Law is a rule; Justice is the principle of fairness.
• Rights are not absolute; they come with reasonable restrictions.
• Equality treats everyone the same; Equity provides specific support.
• Social justice is a collective responsibility of the state and citizens.
• The law is a tool for empowerment, not just for punishment.
