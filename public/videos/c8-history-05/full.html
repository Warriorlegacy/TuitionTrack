Introduction: Why the 1857 Revolt Matters & The Changing Face of Company Rule

• The 1857 Revolt was a massive upheaval against British rule.
• The East India Company shifted from traders to political rulers.
• The Company began collecting taxes and making laws in India.
• Expansion led to political and social changes across the country.
• Growing discontent among kings, peasants, and soldiers.

Causes of Rebellion I: Policies of the East India Company & Impact on Peasants/Zamindars

• High revenue demands under various land systems caused widespread peasant distress.
• Failure to pay taxes led to land confiscation and cycles of heavy debt.
• Zamindars lost traditional land rights and social status due to strict British rules.
• Auctioning of lands created a new class of urban landlords and moneylenders.
• Economic policies disrupted the traditional social fabric of the Indian village.

Part 3 of 12 · ~5 min

Causes of Rebellion II: Discontent among Sepoys & The Doctrine of Lapse

• Sepoys faced religious taboos regarding overseas travel and caste loss.
• Pay disparity and lack of promotion for Indian soldiers caused resentment.
• The Enfield rifle cartridges rumor triggered religious outrage.
• The Doctrine of Lapse allowed the British to annex states without natural heirs.
• Major states annexed included Satara, Sambalpur, Nagpur, and Jhansi.

The Outbreak: From Meerut to Delhi & The Role of Bahadur Shah Zafar

• May 10, 1857: The violent outbreak begins in Meerut.
• Sepoys break into jails and march toward Delhi.
• Delhi's importance: The symbolic center of Indian sovereignty.
• Bahadur Shah Zafar: The reluctant leader and symbol of legitimacy.
• The revolt transforms from a mutiny to a national uprising.

• Delhi: The symbolic center under Bahadur Shah Zafar
• Kanpur: Led by Nana Saheb and the strategist Tantia Tope
• Lucknow: Begum Hazrat Mahal defends the interests of Awadh
• Jhansi: Rani Lakshmibai fights against the Doctrine of Lapse
• Bihar: Kunwar Singh leads the resistance in Jagdishpur

The Company Strikes Back: Suppression, Military Response, and Reclaiming Control

• Massive military mobilization with reinforcements from Britain
• Systematic reconquest of key centers like Delhi, Lucknow, and Kanpur
• Use of heavy artillery and advanced weaponry in siege warfare
• Psychological warfare through public executions and harsh retribution
• Superiority of British communication and centralized command

Part 7 of 12 · ~5 min

Aftermath I: The Shift in Governance & The Government of India Act 1858

• End of East India Company rule and transfer of power to the British Crown.
• The Government of India Act 1858 established direct imperial control.
• The Governor-General was given the new title of 'Viceroy'.
• Creation of the office of the Secretary of State for India in London.
• Establishment of the Council of India to assist the Secretary of State.

Part 8 of 12 · ~5 min

Aftermath II: Changes in Policy towards Princely States and Landlords

• End of Doctrine of Lapse: Princes allowed to adopt heirs.
• Princely States transformed from enemies to loyal allies.
• Guarantee of rights and territories to Indian rulers.
• New policies to protect the property rights of Landlords.
• Goal: Creating a loyal class of elites to stabilize British rule.
