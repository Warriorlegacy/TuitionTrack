Introduction: The Significance of Transition and Inner-Transition Elements & Electronic Configuration

• Transition elements: d-orbitals are progressively being filled
• Inner-transition elements: f-orbitals are being filled (Lanthanoids and Actinoids)
• General d-block configuration: (n-1) d one to ten, n s zero to two
• Stability rule: Half-filled and fully-filled subshells are extra stable
• Chromium exception: four s one, three d five instead of four s two, three d four

General Properties of d-Block: Atomic Radii, Ionization Enthalpy, and Oxidation States

• Atomic radii decrease gradually due to the balance of nuclear charge and d-electron shielding.
• Ionization enthalpy increases across the series but shows irregularities due to subshell stability.
• Half-filled and fully-filled d-orbitals provide extra stability and higher ionization energy.
• Variable oxidation states arise because ns and (n-1)d electrons have similar energy levels.
• Maximum oxidation states decrease towards the end of the series as d-electrons become more stable.

Physical Properties of d-Block: Metallic Character, Melting/Boiling Points, and Enthalpy of Atomization

• Transition metals are hard, lustrous, and excellent conductors.
• Metallic bonding involves both s-electrons and unpaired d-electrons.
• High melting and boiling points due to strong interatomic metallic bonds.
• Enthalpy of atomization measures the strength of metallic bonding.
• Values decrease in Group 12 because d-orbitals are completely filled.

Part 4 of 12 · ~3 min

Chemical Properties of d-Block: Formation of Colored Ions, Catalytic Properties, and Magnetic Properties

• Color arises from d-to-d electronic transitions in partially filled d-orbitals.
• Colorless ions occur when d-orbitals are empty (d zero) or completely full (d ten).
• Catalytic activity is due to variable oxidation states and surface adsorption.
• Paramagnetism is caused by the presence of unpaired electrons in d-orbitals.
• Magnetic moment formula: Mu equals square root of n times n plus two.

Complex Formation and Interstitial Compounds: Coordination Chemistry Foundations

• Interstitial compounds: Small atoms trapped in metal crystal interstices
• Properties of interstitial compounds: Increased hardness and higher melting points
• Complex formation: Transition metals act as Lewis acids using vacant d-orbitals
• Ligands: Lewis bases that donate electron pairs to the metal ion
• Coordination sphere: The central metal and its attached ligands in brackets

Oxides and Oxoanions of Transition Elements: Structures and Chemical Behavior

• Oxide nature: Acidic, basic, or amphoteric depending on metal position
• Increasing acidity of oxides as oxidation state and atomic number increase
• Chromate-Dichromate equilibrium: pH dependent color shift
• Oxoanion geometries: Tetrahedral and octahedral arrangements
• High oxidation states increase covalent character in metal-oxygen bonds

The d-Block in Industrial Processes: Practical Applications and Worked Examples

• Catalytic efficiency due to variable oxidation states and vacant d-orbitals.
• Haber Process: Finely divided Iron (Fe) used for Ammonia synthesis.
• Contact Process: Vanadium Pentoxide (V2O5) used for Sulfuric Acid production.
• Automotive Catalytic Converters: Platinum (Pt) and Palladium (Pd) for emission control.
• Hydrogenation of oils: Nickel (Ni) used to convert liquid oils to solid fats.

Introduction to f-Block: Lanthanoids - Electronic Configuration and Oxidation States

• Lanthanoids: Elements from Cerium (58) to Lutetium (71)
• General Configuration: 6s squared, 5d zero to one, 4f one to fourteen
• Most stable and common oxidation state is +3
• Variable oxidation states occur due to small energy gaps between 4f and 5d
• Ce(IV) is a strong oxidizing agent; Eu(II) is stabilized by f-seven

Part 9 of 12 · ~4 min

Lanthanoid Contraction: Causes, Consequences, and Mathematical Implications

• Steady decrease in atomic and ionic radii across the lanthanoid series.
• Cause: Poor shielding effect of the diffused 4f electrons.
• Increased effective nuclear charge pulls electron clouds closer.
• Consequence: Similar radii for 4d and 5d transition series elements.
• Consequence: Decrease in basicity of lanthanoid hydroxides from La to Lu.

A 3D model showing a nucleus with increasing protons, where 4f electron clouds appear diffuse and fail to block the inward pull on the outer valence shell.

Part 10 of 12 · ~4 min

Actinoids: Electronic Configuration, Oxidation States, and Comparison with Lanthanoids

• Actinoids involve progressive filling of the 5f orbitals.
• Smaller energy gap between 5f, 6d, and 7s orbitals.
• Actinoids exhibit a wider range of oxidation states (up to +7).
• All Actinoid elements are radioactive.
• Actinoid contraction is more pronounced than Lanthanoid contraction.

Common Mistakes, Problem-Solving Strategies, and NCERT Activity Analysis

• Error Alert: Always remove 4s electrons before 3d electrons during ionization.
• Color Logic: Unfilled d-orbitals are mandatory for d-d transitions.
• Magnetic Moment: Use the spin-only formula: mu = square root of n(n+2).
• Size Comparison: Lanthanoid contraction explains Zr and Hf similarity.
• Activity Tip: Focus on oxidation state and color changes in NCERT experiments.
