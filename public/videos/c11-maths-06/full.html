Fundamental Principle of Counting: Addition and Multiplication Rules

• Multiplication Principle: Used for sequential events (Event A AND Event B)
• If event 1 has 'm' ways and event 2 has 'n' ways, total ways = m times n
• Addition Principle: Used for mutually exclusive choices (Event A OR Event B)
• If event 1 has 'm' ways and event 2 has 'n' ways, total ways = m plus n
• Key Tip: 'AND' implies multiplication; 'OR' implies addition

Factorial Notation: Concept, Properties, and Computational Shortcuts

• Factorial notation (n!) represents the product of all positive integers from n down to 1.
• Definition: n factorial = n × (n-1) × (n-2) × ... × 1.
• Special Case: 0! is defined as 1 to maintain mathematical consistency.
• Recursive Property: n! = n × (n-1)!
• Simplification Shortcut: Cancel common factorial terms in fractions to avoid large numbers.

Permutations: Linear Arrangements and the nPr Formula

• Permutation means an arrangement where the order of objects matters.
• Linear arrangement refers to placing objects in a straight line.
• The number of ways to arrange 'n' distinct objects is 'n factorial'.
• The formula for 'n P r' is n! / (n - r)!
• Use permutations when the sequence or position is significant.

Permutations with Repetitions: Identical Objects and Circular Arrangements

• Permutations of n objects with n1, n2... identical items: n! / (n1! * n2! ...)
• Example: 'APPLE' has 5! / 2! = 60 unique arrangements.
• Circular Permutations of n distinct objects: (n - 1)!
• Fix one object to break rotational symmetry in a circle.
• Necklace/Garland arrangements: 1/2 * (n - 1)!

Combinations: Selection Logic and the nCr Formula

• Combination: A selection where the order of objects does not matter.
• Difference: Permutation is arrangement; Combination is selection.
• The Formula: nCr = n! / [r! * (n - r)!]
• Symmetry Property: nCr is equal to nC(n - r).
• Key Logic: Divide permutations by r! to remove redundant orderings.

Relationship between Permutations and Combinations: Derivations and Proofs

• Permutation is a two-step process: Selection followed by Arrangement
• The relationship formula: nPr = nCr × r!
• Deriving nCr: nCr = nPr / r!
• Final Combination Formula: nCr = n! / [r! × (n - r)!]
• Example: 4P2 = 4C2 × 2! (12 = 6 × 2)

• The Overcounting Rule: Dividing by r! for identical group sizes
• Must-Include Constraints: Reducing both n and r

Word Problems: Arranging Letters, Digits, and Forming Numbers

• Order matters in arrangements: Use Permutations (nPr).
• Handling repetitions: Divide by the factorial of repeating items.
• Zero Constraint: A multi-digit number cannot start with zero.
• Constraint Strategy: Always address the most restrictive rule first.
• Increasing/Decreasing Order: Often requires Combinations (nCr) logic.

Complex Word Problems: Seating Arrangements and Committee Selection

• Seating Together: Use the 'String Method' by treating them as one block.
• Seating Apart: Total arrangements minus the 'Together' arrangements.
• Committee Selection: Use nCr for choosing members from distinct groups.
• The Multiplication Rule: Multiply results when tasks are performed together.
• At Least/At Most: Break the problem into separate cases and add them.

Common Pitfalls: Distinguishing Between Arrangement and Selection

• The Golden Rule: Does the order of items change the outcome?
• Permutation = Arrangement (Order Matters)
• Combination = Selection (Order Does Not Matter)
• Keywords for Permutations: Arrange, Row, Rank, Sequence
• Keywords for Combinations: Select, Choose, Committee, Group

Exam Masterclass: Strategy, Time Management, and Full Chapter Recap

• The Golden Rule: Ask 'Does the order matter?' before choosing a formula.
• Time Saver: Always expand and cancel factorials before multiplying.
• Constraint First: Always arrange restricted items or blocks before the rest.
• Avoid Double Counting: Build arrangements according to rules rather than subtracting.
• At Least/At Most: Use the complement method to save time on complex cases.
