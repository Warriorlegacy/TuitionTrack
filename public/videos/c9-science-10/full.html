The Concept of Work: Scientific Definition, Conditions, and Formula

• Physics definition: Work requires both Force and Displacement
• Condition 1: A force must be applied to the object
• Condition 2: The object must move in the direction of the force
• Formula: Work (W) = Force (F) × Displacement (s)
• SI Unit: Joule (J), where 1 Joule = 1 Newton-meter

Part 3 of 12 · ~5 min

Work Done by a Constant Force: Mathematical Derivation and Sign Conventions

• Work formula: W = F s cos theta
• Only the component of force along displacement does work
• Positive Work: Force and displacement are in the same direction (theta = 0°)
• Zero Work: Force is perpendicular to displacement (theta = 90°)
• Negative Work: Force opposes displacement (theta = 180°)

Part 4 of 12 · ~5 min

Energy: The Capacity to Do Work and Different Forms of Energy

• Energy is defined as the capacity or ability to do work.
• The SI unit of energy is the Joule (J), same as work.
• Mechanical Energy consists of Kinetic and Potential Energy.
• Kinetic Energy is energy due to motion; Potential is due to position.
• Energy exists in many forms: Chemical, Thermal, Electrical, and Light.

Kinetic Energy: Derivation of Formula and Mathematical Representation

• Kinetic Energy: Energy possessed by an object due to its motion.
• Work Done (W) = Force (F) × Displacement (s).
• Using Newton's Second Law: F = m × a.
• Using Equation of Motion: v squared - u squared = 2as.
• Final Formula: Kinetic Energy (K) = 1/2 * m * v squared.

Part 6 of 12 · ~5 min

Work and Kinetic Energy Theorem: Concept and Numerical Applications

• Work-Energy Theorem: Work done equals the change in kinetic energy.
• Mathematical Formula: Work = one-half m v-final squared minus one-half m v-initial squared.
• Positive Work: Increases the kinetic energy and speed of an object.
• Negative Work: Decreases the kinetic energy and slows an object down.
• Practical Use: Simplifies complex problems by focusing on velocity changes.

Potential Energy: Gravitational Potential Energy and Derivations

• Potential Energy is energy due to position or configuration
• Gravitational Potential Energy is energy due to height
• Formula Derivation: Work = Force x Displacement
• Mathematical Formula: E-p = m x g x h
• Unit of Potential Energy is the Joule (J)

Law of Conservation of Energy: Mechanical Energy Transformation

• Law of Conservation of Energy: Energy cannot be created or destroyed.
• Total Mechanical Energy = Kinetic Energy + Potential Energy.
• In a free fall, Potential Energy decreases as Kinetic Energy increases.
• At any point, the sum of K.E. and P.E. remains constant.
• Friction converts mechanical energy into heat energy.

Power: Rate of Doing Work and Units of Measurement

• Power is the rate of doing work: P = W / t
• SI unit of Power is the Watt (W), where 1 W = 1 Joule per second
• Power can also be expressed as Force multiplied by Velocity (P = F x v)
• 1 Kilowatt (kW) = 1,000 Watts; 1 Megawatt (MW) = 1,000,000 Watts
• Power measures how quickly work is performed, not the total work done

Problem-Solving Workshop: Mastering Word Problems and Common Mistakes

• Step 1: Extract all given values and assign symbols (m, v, u, F).
• Step 2: Convert all units to SI (kg, m/s, Joules, Watts) immediately.
• Step 3: Check the angle between Force and Displacement (cos 90 = 0).
• Trap Alert: Distinguish between Mass (kg) and Weight (Newtons).
• Sign Convention: Negative work occurs when force opposes motion.
