Natural, Whole, and Integers: Definitions, Number Lines, and Properties

• Natural Numbers (N): {1, 2, 3, ...} - The counting numbers.
• Whole Numbers (W): {0, 1, 2, ...} - Natural numbers plus zero.
• Integers (Z): {..., -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, ...} - Positive, negative, and zero.
• The Number Line: Zero is the origin; positive to the right, negative to the left.
• Absolute Value: The distance of a number from zero, always non-negative.

Rational Numbers: Concept, Representation, and Finding Numbers Between Two Rationals

• Rational Number: p/q form where p and q are integers and q is not zero
• Representation: Divide the unit interval into 'q' equal parts
• Density Property: Infinitely many rational numbers exist between any two numbers
• Method: Use common denominators to find numbers in between
• Example: Between 2/4 and 3/4, use 20/40 and 30/40 to find values

Irrational Numbers: Definitions, Proofs of Irrationality, and the Real Number Line

• Irrational numbers cannot be written as p/q where p and q are integers.
• Decimal form: Non-terminating and non-repeating patterns.
• Proof by Contradiction: Assuming the opposite to find a logical error.
• Classic examples: Square root of 2, square root of 3, and Pi.
• The Real Number Line: A continuous line containing all real numbers.

Decimal Expansions: Terminating, Non-terminating Repeating, and Non-repeating Decimals

• Terminating Decimals: Division ends with a remainder of zero.
• Non-terminating Repeating: Digits repeat in a predictable pattern.
• Non-terminating Non-repeating: Digits continue forever without a pattern.
• Rational Numbers = Terminating OR Non-terminating Repeating decimals.
• Irrational Numbers = Non-terminating Non-repeating decimals.

Converting Recurring Decimals to p/q Form: Step-by-Step Derivations

• Assign the recurring decimal to a variable, such as x.
• Identify the number of repeating digits to determine the multiplier.
• Multiply both sides by 10, 100, or 1000 to shift the decimal block.
• Subtract the original equation from the new equation to eliminate decimals.
• Solve the resulting linear equation for x and simplify the fraction.

• Rational Decimals: Terminating or Non-terminating Repeating

Representing Real Numbers on the Number Line: Successive Magnification Method

• Successive Magnification is like using a microscope to zoom into a number line.
• Step 1: Identify the integer interval where the number resides.
• Step 2: Magnify the interval to locate the tenths place.
• Step 3: Repeat the magnification for hundredths, thousandths, and so on.
• This method works for both terminating and non-terminating recurring decimals.

Operations on Real Numbers: Rules for Addition, Subtraction, Multiplication, and Division

• Rational plus/minus Irrational = Irrational
• Rational multiplied/divided by Irrational = Irrational
• Irrational plus/minus Irrational = Can be Rational or Irrational
• Irrational multiplied/divided by Irrational = Can be Rational or Irrational
• Example: square root five times square root five equals five (Rational)

Rationalizing the Denominator: Techniques for Monomial and Binomial Surds

• Rationalization: Removing radicals from the denominator of a fraction.
• Monomial Surds: Multiply numerator and denominator by the same surd.
• Binomial Surds: Use the conjugate to apply the difference of squares identity.
• Conjugate: The same binomial expression with the opposite middle sign.
• Goal: Transform the denominator into a rational number for easier calculation.

Laws of Exponents for Real Numbers: Formulas, Derivations, and Complex Simplifications

• Product Law: a^m * a^n = a^(m+n)
• Quotient Law: a^m / a^n = a^(m-n)
• Power of a Power: (a^m)^n = a^(m*n)
• Power of a Product: (ab)^n = a^n * b^n
• Converting Radicals: a^(m/n) = n-th root of a^m
