Poetic Structure: Rhyme Scheme, Rhythm, and Meter

• Rhyme Scheme: The pattern of end-rhymes labeled with letters (e.g., A-B-A-B).
• Rhythm: The musical beat created by stressed and unstressed syllables.
• Meter: The formal, mathematical measurement of poetic feet.
• Iambic Foot: A pattern of one unstressed syllable followed by one stressed syllable.
• Structural Intent: How patterns and breaks reflect the poem's emotional tension.

Common Pitfalls: Avoiding Misinterpretations of Symbolism

• Pitfall 1: Literalism — Treating symbols as physical objects instead of ideas.
• Pitfall 2: Over-Simplification — Assigning only one meaning to a complex symbol.
• Pitfall 3: Misattribution — Projecting personal opinions instead of textual evidence.
• The Formula: Identify Symbol -> Check Context Clues -> Link to Emotion.
• Pro-Tip: Use analytical terms like 'paradox' and 'duality' instead of 'good' or 'bad'.
