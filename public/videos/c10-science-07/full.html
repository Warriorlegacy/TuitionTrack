Sexual vs Asexual Reproduction in Animals – Definitions, Reproductive Organs, Fertilization

• Sexual: two parents, gametes, meiosis, fertilization
• Asexual: one parent, no gametes, clones
• Male organs: testes, epididymis, vas deferens
• Female organs: ovaries, oviducts, uterus, vagina
• External fertilization: fish, amphibians

A 3D animation of a male and female reproductive tract, showing sperm traveling through the vas deferens, mixing with seminal fluid, and entering the female vagina, where it meets an egg in the fallopian tube and fertilization occurs.

Asexual Reproduction in Plants – Methods (Cutting, Layering, etc.), Advantages

• Asexual reproduction: single parent, genetically identical offspring.
• Vegetative propagation: using roots, stems, leaves.
• Methods: Cutting, Layering, Grafting, Tubers, Bulbs, Rhizomes.
• Advantages: Faster, maintains desirable traits, overcomes limitations.
• Useful for sterile or seedless plants.

Sexual Reproduction in Plants – Flower Structure, Gametophyte & Sporophyte, Pollination

• Flower: Modified shoot for reproduction
• Stamen (male): Anther (pollen) + Filament
• Pistil (female): Stigma, Style, Ovary (ovules)
• Gametophyte: Haploid, produces gametes (pollen/embryo sac)
• Pollination: Transfer of pollen to stigma (self/cross)

Pollination, Fertilization & Seed Formation – Mechanisms, Diagrams, Examples

• Fertilization: Fusion of male and female gametes.
• Double Fertilization in flowering plants.
• Ovule develops into seed, ovary into fruit.
• Seed structure: Embryo, endosperm, seed coat.
• Fruit's role in seed protection and dispersal.

• DNA replication at oriC
• Septum forms via FtsZ ring
• Two identical daughter cells
• Sporulation under stress

Bacterial Conjugation – Mechanism, Genetic Exchange, Significance

• Bacterial conjugation: Horizontal gene transfer
• Donor cell transfers plasmid via pilus
• Conjugation bridge forms for DNA transfer
• Recipient cell gains new genetic traits
• Significance: Antibiotic resistance spread
