Introduction: The Physics of Sight and Why Why Light Matters

• Light is a form of energy that enables vision.
• Light travels in straight lines (Rectilinear Propagation).
• Reflection: Light bouncing off a surface.
• Refraction: Light bending when changing media.
• Understanding light helps us master optics and vision.

Reflection of Light: Laws, Plane Mirrors, and Image Formation

• Reflection: Bouncing back of light into the same medium
• Law 1: Angle of incidence (i) is equal to angle of reflection (r)
• Law 2: Incident ray, reflected ray, and normal lie in the same plane
• Plane Mirror: Virtual, erect, same size, and laterally inverted
• Lateral Inversion: Left side of object appears as right side of image

A 3D animation showing a single light ray hitting a mirror, with a glowing normal line appearing to demonstrate the equality of angles i and r.

Part 3 of 12 · ~4 min

Spherical Mirrors: Concave and Convex Geometry and Ray Diagrams

• Concave mirrors curve inward; Convex mirrors curve outward
• Center of Curvature (C) is the center of the original sphere
• Principal Axis connects the Pole (P) and the Center of Curvature (C)
• Focal Length (f) is half of the Radius of Curvature (R): f = R divided by 2
• Three key ray rules: Parallel rays, Focus rays, and Center of Curvature rays

A 3D glass sphere splitting into two halves, one silvered internally (concave) and one externally (convex), with labels for P, F, and C appearing as light rays strike them.

Part 4 of 12 · ~5 min

Mirror Formula, Magnification, and Sign Convention Mastery

• New Cartesian Sign Convention: Origin is at the Pole
• Distances in direction of light are positive; against light are negative
• Mirror Formula: 1/v + 1/u = 1/f
• Magnification (m) = h'/h = -v/u
• m is negative for real images; m is positive for virtual images

Part 5 of 12 · ~4 min

Reflection Practice: Worked Examples, Word Problems, and Common Mistakes

• Step 1: Always apply the Cartesian Sign Convention first.
• Step 2: Use the Mirror Formula: 1/v + 1/u = 1/f.
• Step 3: Calculate Magnification: m = -v/u.
• Common Pitfall: Forgetting that u is always negative.
• Common Pitfall: Mixing up signs for concave vs convex mirrors.

Part 6 of 12 · ~5 min

Refraction of Light: Concept, Laws, and Refractive Index

• Refraction: Bending of light due to change in speed between media
• First Law: Incident ray, refracted ray, and normal lie in the same plane
• Snell's Law: sine i / sine r = constant (Refractive Index)
• Absolute Refractive Index: n = speed of light in vacuum / speed in medium
• Rarer to Denser: Bends towards the normal; Denser to Rarer: Bends away

Part 7 of 12 · ~4 min

Refraction through Glass Slabs and Lateral Displacement

• Light bends towards the normal when entering glass from air
• Light bends away from the normal when exiting glass into air
• The emergent ray is parallel to the original incident ray
• Lateral displacement is the perpendicular shift between rays
• Displacement increases with slab thickness and angle of incidence

Part 8 of 12 · ~4 min

Spherical Lenses: Converging and Diverging Lens Theory

• Lens: Transparent material bounded by two surfaces, one or both being spherical.
• Optical Center (O): The central point of the lens where light passes without deviation.
• Convex Lens: Thicker at center; converges parallel rays to a real Principal Focus.
• Concave Lens: Thinner at center; diverges parallel rays from a virtual Principal Focus.
• Focal Length (f): The distance between the optical center and the principal focus.

A 3D split-screen animation showing parallel light rays converging to a point through a convex lens on the left, and diverging away from a point through a concave lens on the right.

Part 9 of 12 · ~4 min

Lens Formula, Magnification, and Sign Convention for Lenses

• Sign Convention: Distances left of lens are negative; right are positive
• Convex Lens: Focal length (f) is positive; Concave Lens: (f) is negative
• Lens Formula: 1/v - 1/u = 1/f
• Magnification (m): Height of image / Height of object OR v / u
• Nature: Negative m = Real/Inverted; Positive m = Virtual/Erect

A 3D coordinate grid overlaying a glass lens, showing vectors for u, v, and f changing color based on sign.

Part 10 of 12 · ~4 min

Refraction through Lenses: Ray Diagrams and Image Characteristics

• Three principal rays: Parallel to axis, through optical center, and through focus.
• Convex Lens: Image changes from diminished to magnified as object approaches focus.
• The 'Special Case': Object between F and Optical Center creates a virtual, erect image.
• Concave Lens: Always produces virtual, erect, and diminished images.
• Image characteristics: Real/Inverted vs. Virtual/Erect depends on ray convergence.

Part 11 of 12 · ~4 min

Power of a Lens and Practical Applications in Vision

• Power of a lens (P) = 1 / focal length (f) in meters
• The SI unit of power is the Dioptre (D)
• Convex lens has positive power; Concave lens has negative power
• Myopia (nearsightedness) is corrected using a concave lens
• Hypermetropia (farsightedness) is corrected using a convex lens
