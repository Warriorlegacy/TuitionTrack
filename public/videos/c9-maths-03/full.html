The Cartesian System: Understanding the Plane, Axes, and Origin

• The Cartesian Plane is a two-dimensional surface defined by two perpendicular lines.
• The horizontal line is the x-axis; the vertical line is the y-axis.
• The Origin is the intersection point of the axes, represented as (0, 0).
• The axes divide the plane into four regions called Quadrants.
• Quadrants are numbered I, II, III, and IV in a counter-clockwise direction.

The Anatomy of a Point: Defining Coordinates (x, y) and Quadrants

• Ordered Pair: (x, y) where order determines the unique location
• Abscissa: The horizontal distance from the origin (x-coordinate)
• Ordinate: The vertical distance from the origin (y-coordinate)
• Quadrants: Four regions labeled I, II, III, and IV counter-clockwise
• Sign Convention: Quadrant I (+,+), II (-,+), III (-,-), IV (+,-)

Plotting Points: Step-by-Step Guide to the Cartesian Plane

• Always start every plotting task at the Origin (0, 0)
• The first number (x) dictates horizontal movement: Right if (+), Left if (-)
• The second number (y) dictates vertical movement: Up if (+), Down if (-)
• Do not mark the point until both horizontal and vertical moves are complete
• Points with a zero coordinate lie directly on the X or Y axes

Worked Examples: Finding Coordinates from Given Diagrams

• Step 1: Find the x-coordinate (abscissa) by dropping a vertical line to the x-axis.
• Step 2: Find the y-coordinate (ordinate) by moving horizontally to the y-axis.
• Points on the y-axis always have an x-coordinate of zero (0, y).
• Points on the x-axis always have a y-coordinate of zero (x, 0).
• Always write coordinates in the standard form: (x, y).

Common Pitfalls: Avoiding Sign Errors and Axis Confusion

• The Order Reversal Trap: Always move along the x-axis before the y-axis.
• Sign Check: Mentally verify the quadrant signs before plotting.
• Axis Placement: Points on the y-axis have an x-coordinate of zero.
• Scale Awareness: Always check the interval values on the axes.
• Mathematical Notation: Always write coordinates in the form (x, y).
