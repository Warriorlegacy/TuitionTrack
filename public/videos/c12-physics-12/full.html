Thomson's Model and Rutherford's Alpha-Particle Scattering Experiment

• Thomson's Model: Positive sphere with embedded electrons (Plum Pudding Model)
• Thomson's Atom: Electrically neutral; total positive charge equals negative charge
• Rutherford's Experiment: Bombarding thin gold foil with alpha particles
• Observation 1: Most alpha particles pass straight through (Empty space)
• Observation 2: Rare large-angle deflections (Dense, positive nucleus)

Rutherford's Nuclear Model: Postulates, Observations, and Limitations

• Nucleus: Small, dense, central region containing most mass and positive charge
• Planetary Model: Electrons revolve around the nucleus in circular orbits
• Empty Space: Explains why most alpha particles pass through undeflected
• Instability: Classical physics predicts electrons should spiral into the nucleus
• Spectral Failure: Cannot explain why atoms emit discrete line spectra

The Concept of Energy Levels and Quantization in Atoms

• Classical failure: Electrons should spiral into the nucleus due to energy loss.
• The Stability Paradox: Why atoms don't collapse according to classical laws.
• Quantization: Energy is restricted to discrete, specific values.
• Stationary States: Allowed orbits where electrons do not radiate energy.
• Photon Emission: Energy difference between levels is released as a photon.

Part 5 of 12 · ~5 min

Bohr's Model of the Hydrogen Atom: Fundamental Postulates

• Electrons move in stable, non-radiating 'stationary orbits'.
• Angular momentum is quantized: L = n * h / 2π.
• n is the principal quantum number (n = 1, 2, 3...).
• Energy is emitted/absorbed only during transitions between orbits.
• Photon energy equals the difference between energy levels: E = E2 - E1.

A 3D animation showing a nucleus surrounded by concentric glowing rings, with an electron jumping between rings and emitting a colored photon pulse.

Part 6 of 12 · ~5 min

Derivation of Bohr's Quantization Condition and Orbital Radius

• Angular Momentum L = mvr = n(h/2π)
• Centripetal Force = Electrostatic Force
• m(v²/r) = Ze² / (4πε₀r²)
• Radius r ∝ n²/Z
• Bohr Radius (n=1, Z=1) ≈ 0.529 Å

Part 7 of 12 · ~5 min

Derivation of Angular Momentum and Total Energy of an Electron

• Centripetal force equals electrostatic force of attraction
• Bohr's quantization: mvr = nh / 2π
• Radius r is proportional to n squared
• Total Energy E = Kinetic Energy + Potential Energy
• Energy E is proportional to -Z squared / n squared

Part 8 of 12 · ~5 min

Spectral Series of Hydrogen: Lyman, Balmer, Paschen, Brackett, and Pfund

• Photon energy equals the difference between initial and final energy levels.
• Lyman Series: Transitions to n=1; located in the Ultraviolet region.
• Balmer Series: Transitions to n=2; located in the Visible spectrum.
• Paschen Series: Transitions to n=3; located in the Infrared region.
• Brackett (n=4) and Pfund (n=5) series are also in the Infrared region.

Part 9 of 12 · ~5 min

Rydberg Formula: Mathematical Derivation and Wave-number Calculations

• Energy of photon: E = E-initial - E-final
• Wave-number (nu) is defined as 1/lambda
• Rydberg Formula: nu = Z^2 * R-H * (1/n_f^2 - 1/n_i^2)
• Rydberg Constant (R-H) approx 1.097 x 10^7 m^-1
• Higher energy transitions result in shorter wavelengths

Part 10 of 12 · ~5 min

Transitions, Photon Emission, and Energy Conservation in Atoms

• Electrons exist in discrete, quantized energy levels (stationary states).
• Transitions occur when electrons move between different energy orbits.
• Energy Conservation: Energy of photon = E-initial minus E-final.
• Photon Emission: Electron moves from high energy to low energy state.
• Photon Absorption: Electron absorbs energy to move to a higher state.

Part 11 of 12 · ~5 min

Advanced Applications: Ionization Energy and Photoelectric-related Atomic Transitions

• Ionization Energy: Energy required to move an electron from n=1 to n=infinity
• Ground State Energy of Hydrogen is negative thirteen point six electron volts
• Photoionization: Photon energy must equal or exceed the ionization threshold
• Kinetic Energy of ejected electron equals Photon Energy minus Ionization Energy
• At n=infinity, energy levels form a continuum and the electron becomes free
