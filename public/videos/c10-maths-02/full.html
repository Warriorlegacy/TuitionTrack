Defining Polynomials: Degree, Coefficients, and Classification

• Polynomials must have non-negative integer exponents.
• Coefficient: The numerical factor of a variable term.
• Degree: The highest exponent of the variable in the expression.
• Classification by terms: Monomial (1), Binomial (2), Trinomial (3).
• Classification by degree: Linear (1), Quadratic (2), Cubic (3), Bi-quadratic (4).

The Zeroes of a Polynomial: Geometric Meaning and Graphical Representation

• Zeroes are the values of x that make the polynomial equal to zero
• Geometrically, zeroes are the x-coordinates where the graph meets the x-axis
• The number of zeroes equals the number of x-intercepts on a graph
• Linear polynomials form straight lines with one x-intercept
• Quadratic polynomials form parabolas with zero, one, or two x-intercepts

Geometrical Interpretation of Zeroes: Visualizing Linear and Quadratic Graphs

• Zeroes are the x-coordinates where the graph intersects the x-axis.
• Linear Polynomials (ax + b) always form a straight line with one zero.
• Quadratic Polynomials (ax² + bx + c) form a U-shaped curve called a parabola.
• If 'a' is positive, the parabola opens upwards; if negative, it opens downwards.
• A quadratic can have two distinct zeroes, one repeated zero, or no real zeroes.

Relationship between Zeroes and Coefficients: Part 1 (Linear Polynomials)

• General form of a linear polynomial: ax + b (a ≠ 0)
• The zero is the value of x that makes p(x) = 0
• Relationship: Zero = - (Constant Term) / (Coefficient of x)
• Example: For 3x + 6, zero is -6/3 = -2
• This algebraic link is vital for quadratic polynomials

Part 6 of 12 · ~5 min

Relationship between Zeroes and Coefficients: Part 2 (Quadratic Polynomials - Derivations)

• Standard Quadratic Form: ax^2 + bx + c (a ≠ 0)
• Factored Form: a(x - α)(x - β)
• Derivation: Comparing coefficients of x and constant terms
• Sum of Zeroes (α + β) = -b / a
• Product of Zeroes (αβ) = c / a

Advanced Quadratic Problems: Forming Polynomials from Given Zeroes

• Formula: p(x) = k[x² - (sum of zeroes)x + (product of zeroes)]
• Step 1: Calculate Sum (α + β) and Product (α × β)
• Step 2: Substitute values into the quadratic template
• Handling Radicals: Use (a+b)(a-b) = a² - b² for products
• Handling Reciprocals: If zeroes are reciprocal, product is always 1

Division Algorithm for Polynomials: Concepts, Long Division, and Remainder Theorem

• Division Algorithm: p(x) = g(x) * q(x) + r(x)
• The degree of remainder r(x) < degree of divisor g(x)
• Long Division: Step-by-step reduction of polynomial degrees
• Remainder Theorem: If p(x) is divided by (x - a), remainder is p(a)
• Remainder Theorem is a shortcut for linear divisors

Factor Theorem: Testing Factors and Factoring Polynomials

• Factor Theorem: (x - a) is a factor of p(x) if and only if p(a) = 0
• Testing Factors: Use substitution instead of long division to save time
• Cubic Factorization Strategy: 1. Find one zero, 2. Divide, 3. Factorize quadratic
• Example: x^3 - 6x^2 + 11x - 6 factors into (x - 1)(x - 2)(x - 3)
• Pro Tip: Always double-check your signs during substitution

Solving Complex Polynomial Equations: Step-by-Step Worked Examples

• Step 1: Use Trial and Error with the Factor Theorem to find the first zero.
• Step 2: Use Long Division to divide the polynomial by the identified factor.
• Step 3: Solve the resulting quadratic quotient using splitting the middle term.
• Example 1: x cubed minus 6x squared plus 11x minus 6 has zeroes 1, 2, and 3.
• Example 2: For 2x cubed minus 5x squared minus 4x plus three, zeroes are -1, 1/2, and 3.
