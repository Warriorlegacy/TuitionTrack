Pressure of an Ideal Gas: Molecular Model & Derivation

• Ideal Gas Assumptions: Point masses and perfectly elastic collisions
• Momentum change per collision: 2 * m * v-x
• Force of one molecule: m * (v-x squared) / L
• Pressure formula: P = (1/3) * (N * m * v-squared) / V
• Pressure is proportional to molecular kinetic energy

Part 3 of 12 · ~5 min

Kinetic Interpretation of Temperature & Root Mean Square Speed

• Temperature is a macroscopic measure of microscopic kinetic energy.
• Average kinetic energy per molecule = 3/2 k T.
• R-M-S speed is the square root of the mean of the squares of speeds.
• Formula: v-rms = square root of (6RT / M).
• Lighter molecules move faster than heavier molecules at the same temperature.

Part 4 of 12 · ~5 min

Law of Equipartition of Energy & Degrees of Freedom

• Degrees of Freedom (f): Independent ways a molecule can store energy.
• Monatomic gas: 3 translational degrees of freedom (f = 3).
• Diatomic gas: 3 translational + 2 rotational degrees of freedom (f = 5).
• Law of Equipartition: Average energy per degree of freedom is one-half k T.
• Total Internal Energy (U) = one-half N f k T.

Part 5 of 12 · ~5 min

Specific Heat Capacities (Cv and Cp) & Mayer's Relation

• Molar specific heat at constant volume: Cv = (dU/dT)
• Molar specific heat at constant pressure: Cp = (dH/dT)
• At constant pressure, heat is used for both internal energy and work
• Mayer's Relation: Cp - Cv = R
• For monatomic gas: Cv = 3/2 R and Cp = 5/2 R

Part 6 of 12 · ~5 min

Molecular Speeds: Most Probable, Average, and RMS Speeds

• Molecules in a gas do not move at a single uniform speed.
• Most Probable Speed (v mp): The speed at the peak of the distribution.
• Average Speed (v bar): The arithmetic mean of all molecular speeds.
• RMS Speed (v rms): The square root of the mean of the squares of speeds.
• Hierarchy: v mp < v bar < v rms

Maxwell-Boltzmann Distribution of Velocities

• Molecules in a gas possess a wide range of individual velocities.
• The distribution function f(v) gives the probability of a specific velocity.
• The curve is asymmetrical with a long tail at high velocities.
• Increasing temperature shifts the peak to the right and flattens the curve.
• Higher temperature leads to a broader spread of molecular speeds.

Part 8 of 12 · ~5 min

Mean Free Path and Collision Frequency

• Mean Free Path (λ): Average distance traveled between two successive collisions.
• Formula: λ = 1 / (√2 * n * σ²), where n is number density and σ is molecular diameter.
• Collision Frequency (z): Number of collisions occurring per unit time per molecule.
• Formula: z = v_avg / λ, showing frequency is inversely proportional to path length.
• Higher density or larger molecular size directly impacts collision dynamics.

Part 9 of 12 · ~5 min

Real Gases vs. Ideal Gases: van der Waals Equation

• Ideal gas law assumes zero molecular volume and no intermolecular forces.
• Real gases deviate due to finite molecular size and attractive forces.
• Volume correction: Replace V with (V - b), where b is the co-volume.
• Pressure correction: Observed pressure is lower due to intermolecular attraction.
• Van der Waals Equation: (P + a/V^2)(V - b) = nRT

Part 10 of 12 · ~5 min

Critical Constants and the Compressibility Factor

• Critical Temperature (Tc): The point where liquid and gas phases become indistinguishable.
• Critical Pressure (Pc) and Volume (Vc): Unique state variables at the critical point.
• Van der Waals Constants: Tc = 8a/27Rb; Pc = 27a²/64R²B³; Vc = 3B.
• Compressibility Factor (Z): Defined as Z = PV / RT.
• Z < 1: Attractive forces dominate; Z > 1: Repulsive forces and molecular volume dominate.

Worked Examples: Solving Complex Numerical Problems

• RMS speed is proportional to the square root of absolute temperature
• Internal energy U equals one-half f R T, where f is degrees of freedom
• Use Mayer's Relation: C-p equals C-v plus R for diatomic gases
• Real gases require the van der Waals equation: P equals RT/(V-b) minus a/V^2
• Always convert units to SI, especially liters to cubic meters
