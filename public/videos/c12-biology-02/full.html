Male Reproductive System: Anatomy, Histology, and Spermatogenesis

• Scrotum maintains temperature 2 to 2.5 degrees Celsius below body temperature
• Seminiferous tubules are the site of sperm production
• Sertoli cells provide nourishment; Leydig cells secrete testosterone
• Spermatogenesis: Mitosis and Meiosis producing haploid spermatids
• Spermiogenesis: Transformation of spermatids into motile spermatozoa

Female Reproductive System: Anatomy, Histology, and Oogenesis

• Ovaries produce ova and hormones: estrogen and progesterone
• Fallopian tube parts: Infundibulum, Ampulla, and Isthmus
• Uterine layers: Perimetrium, Myometrium, and Endometrium
• Oogenesis begins during fetal development and arrests in meiosis
• Unequal division ensures maximum cytoplasm for the ovum

Part 4 of 12 · ~5 min

Menstrual Cycle: Hormonal Regulation and Phases

• Menstrual Phase: Shedding of the endometrial lining due to low hormone levels.
• Follicular Phase: FSH stimulates follicle growth and estrogen production.
• Ovulation: A sudden LH surge triggers the release of the secondary oocyte.
• Luteal Phase: Corpus Luteum secretes progesterone to maintain the endometrium.
• Hormonal Feedback: The cycle is regulated by FSH, LH, Estrogen, and Progesterone.

Fertilization: The Journey of Gametes and Zygote Formation

• Fertilization occurs in the ampullary region of the fallopian tube
• Capacitation: Biochemical activation of sperm motility
• Acrosome Reaction: Release of enzymes to penetrate the zona pellucida
• Prevention of Polyspermy: The cortical reaction hardens the zona pellucida
• Zygote Formation: Fusion of haploid pronuclei to create a diploid cell

A 3D microscopic animation showing a sperm cell penetrating the corona radiata and undergoing the acrosome reaction upon contact with the zona pellucida.

Implantation and Early Embryonic Development

• Cleavage: Rapid mitotic divisions of the zygote into blastomeres
• Morula: An eight to sixteen-cell stage resembling a mulberry
• Blastocyst: A hollow structure with trophoblast and inner cell mass
• Implantation: The attachment of the blastocyst to the uterine endometrium
• Gastrulation: Formation of ectoderm, mesoderm, and endoderm layers

Pregnancy: Placental Functions and Gestation

• Placenta: A temporary endocrine organ connecting mother and fetus
• Functions: Nutrient supply, oxygen delivery, and waste removal
• Hormones secreted: H-C-G, Estrogen, Progesterone, and Placental Lactogen
• Gestation: The nine-month period of embryonic and fetal development
• Exchange mechanism: Diffusion across the placental membrane

Part 8 of 12 · ~5 min

Parturition and Lactation: The Mechanics of Birth

• Parturition is triggered by the Fetal Ejection Reflex.
• Oxytocin causes uterine contractions and cervical dilation.
• The process follows a positive feedback mechanism.
• Prolactin (Anterior Pituitary) stimulates milk production.
• Oxytocin (Posterior Pituitary) triggers the milk ejection reflex.

Common Pitfalls: Conceptual Errors and Misconceptions

• Spermatogenesis vs. Spermiogenesis: The whole process vs. the final transformation.
• Menstrual Cycle: The luteal phase is constant; the follicular phase is variable.
• Fertilization Site: Strictly occurs in the ampullary region of the Fallopian tube.
• Placental Function: An endocrine organ secreting hCG, hPL, and progesterone.
• Ovulation Detail: The released cell is a secondary oocyte arrested in Metaphase II.

Numerical & Logical Application: Hormonal Graphs and Timing

• Identify the LH Surge as the primary trigger for ovulation around day 14.
• Correlate high Progesterone levels with the functional Corpus Luteum.
• Recognize that the decline in Progesterone and Estrogen triggers menstruation.
• Calculate ovulation day by subtracting 14 from the total cycle length.
• Understand positive feedback (Estrogen to LH) vs negative feedback (Progesterone).
