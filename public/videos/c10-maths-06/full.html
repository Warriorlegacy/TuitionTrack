Similarity vs Congruence: Definitions, Criteria, and Visual Distinctions

• Congruence: Same shape AND same size (Identical)
• Similarity: Same shape BUT different size (Proportional)
• All congruent triangles are similar, but not vice versa
• Similarity requires equal angles and proportional sides
• Congruence uses SSS, SAS, ASA; Similarity uses AA, SSS, SAS

Basic Proportionality Theorem (Thales Theorem): Statement, Proof, and Diagrammatic Logic

• Statement: A line parallel to one side divides the other two sides proportionally.
• Given: In triangle ABC, DE is parallel to BC, where D is on AB and E is on AC.
• To Prove: AD / DB = AE / EC.
• Construction: Join BE and CD; draw perpendiculars h1 and h2 to the sides.
• Key Logic: Triangles on the same base and between same parallels have equal areas.

Converse of BPT: Application, Logical Derivations, and Common Pitfalls

• Converse of BPT: If ratios are equal, then the line is parallel to the third side.
• Logical Derivation: Uses the method of proof by contradiction.
• Application: Use given segment lengths to prove parallelism in triangles.
• Pitfall 1: Always compare corresponding segments (e.g., AD/DB = AE/EC).
• Pitfall 2: Ensure points are collinear on the sides of the triangle.

Criteria for Similarity of Triangles: AAA, SSS, and SAS Theorems

• Similarity requires equal angles and proportional sides.
• AAA Criterion: If three angles are equal, triangles are similar.
• AA Shortcut: Two equal angles are sufficient for similarity.
• SSS Criterion: If all three pairs of sides are proportional.
• SAS Criterion: Two sides in ratio and the included angle must be equal.

Similarity in Right-Angled Triangles: Properties and Special Cases

• Right triangles are similar if they share one acute angle (AA Criterion)
• An altitude to the hypotenuse creates two smaller triangles similar to the original
• Altitude Squared Property: BD squared equals AD times DC
• Leg Squared Property: AB squared equals AD times AC
• Similarity connects the altitude to the segments of the hypotenuse

Area of Similar Triangles: Theorem, Derivation, and Ratio Applications

• Theorem: Ratio of areas = Square of the ratio of corresponding sides
• If ΔABC ~ ΔPQR, then Area(ABC)/Area(PQR) = (AB/PQ)² = (BC/QR)²
• Derivation uses the ratio of altitudes and similarity of sub-triangles
• Side ratio 3:5 implies Area ratio 9:25
• Area ratio 16:49 implies Side ratio 4:7

Pythagoras Theorem: Concept, Proof, and Relationship to Similarity

• Pythagoras Theorem: Hypotenuse squared = Sum of squares of other two sides
• Formula: c squared = a squared + b squared
• The theorem is a direct consequence of triangle similarity
• Proof Method: Using a perpendicular to create two smaller similar triangles
• Similarity Criterion: AA (Angle-Angle) is used to establish side ratios
