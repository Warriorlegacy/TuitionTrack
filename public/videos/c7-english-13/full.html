Poetic Devices I: Rhyme Scheme, Rhythm, and Stanza Structure

• Stanza: A group of lines; a four-line stanza is called a 'Quatrain'.
• Rhyme Scheme: The pattern of end-rhymes identified by letters (e.g., A-B-C-B).
• Rhyme Pattern: In 'The Shed', the second and fourth lines often rhyme.
• Rhythm: The musical beat created by stressed and unstressed syllables.
• Purpose: These devices create mood, tone, and a sense of natural speech.

Literary Techniques: Personification, Alliteration, and Metaphor

• Personification: Giving human qualities to non-human objects.
• Alliteration: Repetition of consonant sounds at the start of words.
• Metaphor: Direct comparison between two unlike things without 'like' or 'as'.

Grammar Integration: Adjectives and Adverbs in Descriptive Poetry

• Adjectives: Words that describe or modify nouns (e.g., dusty, dark shed)
• Adverbs: Words that modify verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs (e.g., swings eerily)
• Adjectives answer: What kind? Which one? How many?
• Adverbs answer: How? When? Where? To what extent?
• Formula: Use Adjectives for texture and Adverbs for motion in descriptive writing

Critical Thinking: Comparing the Poet's Fear to Reality

• Subjective Reality: How the poet feels (fear, mystery, ghosts).
• Objective Reality: The physical facts (dust, rust, spider webs).
• Projection: Attributing internal fears to external objects.
• The Gap: The difference between imagination and truth.
• Formula: Fear + Fact = The Psychological Gap.

Common Mistakes: Misinterpreting Tone and Symbolism

• Mistake 1: Treating the tone as pure horror instead of a struggle with rationality.
• Mistake 2: Viewing the shed as just a building rather than a symbol of the unknown.
• Mistake 3: Mischaracterizing the speaker as helpless instead of courageous and curious.
• Mistake 4: Treating the brother's ghost stories as literal facts rather than manipulation.
• Mistake 5: Ignoring how the steady rhyme scheme reflects the speaker's growing control.
