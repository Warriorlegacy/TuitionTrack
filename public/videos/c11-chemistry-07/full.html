Concept of Oxidation and Reduction: Classical vs. Electronic Concepts

• Classical Oxidation: Addition of oxygen to a substance
• Classical Reduction: Removal of oxygen from a substance
• Electronic Oxidation: Loss of electrons (OIL: Oxidation Is Loss)
• Electronic Reduction: Gain of electrons (RIG: Reduction Is Gain)
• Redox reactions are coupled processes: Oxidation and Reduction occur together

Oxidation Number: Rules, Calculation Methods, and Formal Charge

• Oxidation number: Hypothetical charge in an ionic model
• Elemental state oxidation number is always zero
• Sum of oxidation numbers in a neutral molecule is zero
• Oxygen is -2, except in peroxides (-1) and OF2 (+2)
• Formal Charge = Valence electrons - Lone electrons - 1/2 Bonding electrons

Identifying Redox Reactions: Balancing by Oxidation Number Method

• Redox reaction: Defined by a change in oxidation numbers.
• Step 1: Assign oxidation numbers to all atoms in the equation.
• Step 2: Identify the atoms being oxidized and reduced.
• Step 3: Equalize the total increase and total decrease in oxidation numbers.
• Step 4: Balance remaining atoms and verify total ionic charge.

Balancing Redox Reactions: Ion-Electron Method in Acidic Medium

• Split the reaction into two distinct half-reactions: Oxidation and Reduction
• Balance atoms other than O and H first
• Balance Oxygen by adding H2O molecules to the deficient side
• Balance Hydrogen by adding H+ ions to the deficient side
• Balance charge by adding electrons (e-) to the more positive side

Part 6 of 12 · ~5 min

Balancing Redox Reactions: Ion-Electron Method in Basic Medium

• Use the same steps as the acidic medium method initially
• Balance oxygen with H-two-O and hydrogen with H-plus ions
• Neutralize H-plus by adding equal O-H-minus to both sides
• Combine H-plus and O-H-minus to form water molecules
• Equalize electrons and simplify water before final assembly

Disproportionation and Comproportionation Reactions: Deep Dive

• Disproportionation: A single substance is both oxidized and reduced.
• Example: H2O2 decomposes into H2O (reduction) and O2 (oxidation).
• Comproportionation: Two species with different oxidation states form one product.
• Requirement: Disproportionation requires an intermediate oxidation state.
• Key Tip: Always calculate oxidation numbers to identify these special cases.

Electrochemical Cells: Galvanic Cells and Electrode Potentials

• Galvanic cells convert chemical energy into electrical energy via spontaneous redox.
• Anode is the site of oxidation (negative terminal); Cathode is the site of reduction (positive).
• The salt bridge maintains electrical neutrality by allowing ion migration.
• Electrode potential measures the tendency of an electrode to gain or lose electrons.
• Standard Cell Potential (E standard cell) = E standard cathode - E standard anode.

Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE) and the Electrochemical Series

• SHE serves as the universal reference electrode with a potential of zero volts.
• Standard conditions for SHE: 1 M H plus concentration and 1 bar H two pressure.
• Platinum black provides a high surface area for the hydrogen electrode reaction.
• Electrochemical Series: Arrangement of elements based on standard reduction potentials.
• Predicting spontaneity: A positive E cell value indicates a spontaneous reaction.

Redox in Biological Systems and Industrial Applications

• Metallurgy: Using reducing agents like carbon to extract pure metals from ores

Common Mistakes, Problem-Solving Strategies, and Numerical Practice

• Common Error: Forgetting that polyatomic ion oxidation numbers must sum to the ion's charge.
• Balancing Tip: Always balance Oxygen with H2O and Hydrogen with H+ ions.
• Mnemonic: Use OIL RIG (Oxidation Is Loss, Reduction Is Gain) to track electrons.
• Step-by-Step: Assign numbers, identify redox pairs, write half-reactions, and equalize electrons.
• Cell Potential: E cell = E cathode - E anode. Positive E cell means spontaneous reaction.
