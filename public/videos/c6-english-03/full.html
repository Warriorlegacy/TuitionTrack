Literary Devices: Exploring Imagery, Similes, and Personification

• Imagery: Using sensory details to create mental pictures (sight, sound, smell, touch, taste).
• Simile: Comparing two different things using the words 'like' or 'as'.
• Personification: Giving human qualities or emotions to non-human objects or nature.
• Purpose: These devices build mood, emotion, and make the setting feel alive.
• Pro-Tip: Look for 'like/as' for similes and human actions for personification.

Worked Examples: Answering Short and Long Answer Questions

• Short Answers: Use the 'Direct-plus-Detail' formula for precision.
• Long Answers: Follow the 'P-E-E-L' method (Point, Evidence, Explanation, Link).
• Avoid 'I think'; use 'The text suggests' for a professional tone.
• Maintain tense consistency: match your answer to the question's tense.
• Use transition words like 'Furthermore' to improve writing flow.

Part 11 of 12 · ~4 min

Common Pitfalls: Avoiding Errors in Interpretation and Grammar

• Literal vs. Inferential: Don't mistake similes for literal actions.
• Tone Consistency: Ensure synonyms match the story's emotional context.
• Tense Stability: Maintain a single tense throughout your entire answer.
• Subject-Verb Agreement: Match verbs to the core subject, not nearby nouns.
• Pronoun Clarity: Always specify who 'he', 'she', or 'it' refers to.
