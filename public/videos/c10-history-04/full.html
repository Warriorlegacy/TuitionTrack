Print Comes to Europe: Gutenberg and the Revolution of the Printing Press

• Paper arrived in Europe via trade routes before the printing press.
• Johann Gutenberg, a German goldsmith, invented the first printing press.
• The press combined the mechanism of olive/wine presses with movable type.
• Gutenberg used metal molds to create individual, reusable letters.
• The first major book printed was the Bible, with 180 copies in three years.

The 19th Century: Innovations in Print, Children's Literature, and Women Readers

• Technological shift: From hand-operated presses to the high-speed rotary press.
• Richard March Hoe's cylindrical press significantly increased production speed.
• Lower production costs made books accessible to the common masses.
• Emergence of specialized children's literature with simpler language and art.
• Rise of women readers through access to novels, magazines, and journals.

The Spread of Print in India: Manuscripts, Oral Traditions, and Early Print Culture

• Manuscripts: Handwritten texts on palm leaves or handmade paper.
• High Cost: Hand-copying made books expensive and rare luxury items.
• Oral Tradition: Knowledge passed through singing, reciting, and storytelling.
• Mass Reach: Oral culture allowed non-literate populations to access stories.
• The Contrast: Elite written manuscripts versus widespread oral traditions.
