Core Concepts: Drainage Basins, Water Divide, and River Systems

• Drainage Basin: The total land area drained by a single river system.
• Water Divide: An upland or mountain separating two drainage basins.
• River System: The main river plus all its tributaries and distributaries.
• Tributary: A smaller stream that joins and feeds into a larger river.
• Distributary: A branch that flows away from the main river near the mouth.

The Himalayan Rivers: Indus River System - Features and Tributaries

• Indus rises near Lake Mansarovar in Tibet
• Flows through Ladakh, creating deep mountain valleys
• Himalayan tributaries: Zanskar, Nubra, Shyok, and Hunza
• Major Punjab tributaries: Satluj, Beas, Ravi, Chenab, and Jhelum
• Final destination: Arabian Sea, east of Karachi

The Himalayan Rivers: Ganga River System - Course and Significance

• Headwaters: Bhagirathi meets Alaknanda at Devprayag to form the Ganga
• Major Tributaries: Yamuna, Ghaghara, Gandak, Kosi, and Son
• The Ganga-Brahmaputra confluence forms the massive Sunderban Delta
• The river splits into the Bhagirathi-Hooghly and the Padma in Bangladesh
• Significance: Fertile plains, high population density, and cultural importance

Part 5 of 12 · ~5 min

The Himalayan Rivers: Brahmaputra River System - Dynamics and Challenges

• Originates in Tibet as the Tsangpo, flowing through a cold and dry region.
• Takes a sharp U-turn at Namcha Barwa to enter India via Arunachal Pradesh.
• Transforms into a high-volume, sediment-heavy river in the Assam valley.
• Develops a braided channel pattern due to massive silt deposition.
• Causes frequent flooding and severe riverbank erosion in Northeast India.

The Peninsular Rivers: West Flowing vs. East Flowing Patterns

• Peninsular rivers are seasonal and depend on monsoon rainfall.
• East-flowing rivers: Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, and Kaveri.
• East-flowing rivers form large, fertile deltas at the Bay of Bengal.
• West-flowing rivers: Narmada and Tapi, flowing into the Arabian Sea.
• West-flowing rivers flow through rift valleys and form estuaries.

The Peninsular Rivers: Detailed Study of Narmada and Tapi Basins

• Narmada rises in the Amarkantak hills, Madhya Pradesh
• Both Narmada and Tapi flow through tectonic rift valleys
• Narmada features: Marble Rocks and Dhuandhar Falls
• Tapi rises in the Satpura ranges, Madhya Pradesh
• West-flowing rivers form estuaries instead of deltas

The Peninsular Rivers: Detailed Study of Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, and Kaveri

• Mahanadi: Originates in Chhattisgarh, flows through Odisha to the Bay of Bengal.
• Godavari: Known as 'Dakshin Ganga', largest peninsular river, rises in Nasik.
• Krishna: Rises near Mahabaleshwar; major tributaries include Tungabhadra and Bhima.
• Kaveri: Rises in Brahmagiri range; receives water from both monsoons.
• East-flowing rivers form large deltas before entering the Bay of Bengal.

Lakes: Types, Formation, and Ecological Importance in India

• Lakes: Large bodies of water surrounded by land
• Glacial Lakes: Formed by melting ice in mountain hollows
• Ox-bow Lakes: Formed by meandering river loops being cut off
• Lagoons: Saltwater lakes formed by coastal wave action
• Ecological Role: Flood control, climate regulation, and biodiversity

Part 10 of 12 · ~5 min

Drainage Patterns: Dendritic, Trellis, Rectangular, and Radial Models

• Drainage Pattern: The geometric arrangement of streams in a basin.
• Dendritic Pattern: Tree-like branching in uniform rock areas.
• Trellis Pattern: Tributaries join the main river at right angles.
• Rectangular Pattern: Channels follow joints and cracks in bedrock.
• Radial Pattern: Streams flow outward from a central high point.

River Pollution: Causes, Impact, and National River Conservation Plan (NRCP)

• Major Causes: Industrial effluents, domestic sewage, and agricultural runoff
• High B-O-D levels lead to oxygen depletion and loss of aquatic life
• Impacts: Water-borne diseases, loss of biodiversity, and economic damage
• N-R-C-P: National River Conservation Plan for water quality improvement
• Key Strategies: Sewage Treatment Plants and the Namami Gange Programme
