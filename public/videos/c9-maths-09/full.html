Fundamental Definitions: Center, Radius, Chord, Arc, and Segment of a Circle

• Center: The fixed point at the heart of the circle.
• Radius: The constant distance from the center to the boundary.
• Chord: A line segment connecting two points on the circumference.
• Arc: A curved portion of the circle's circumference.
• Segment: The region bounded by a chord and an arc.

The Perpendicular Bisector Theorem: Relationship between Center and Chords

• A perpendicular from the center to a chord bisects the chord.
• A line from the center bisecting a chord is perpendicular to it.
• The center, the midpoint of the chord, and an endpoint form a right triangle.
• Use the Pythagorean Theorem: Radius squared = (Half-chord)^2 + (Distance)^2.
• The distance from the center is the length of the perpendicular segment.

Equal Chords and their Distance from the Center: Properties and Proofs

• Distance from center is the length of the perpendicular segment to the chord.
• Theorem: Equal chords in a circle are equidistant from the center.
• Proof uses R-H-S congruence of triangles formed by radii and perpendiculars.
• Converse: Chords equidistant from the center are equal in length.
• Relationship can be solved using the Pythagorean theorem with the radius.

Angles Subtended by an Arc: Theorem on Central Angles and Inscribed Angles

• Central Angle: Angle formed at the center by an arc (Angle AOB)
• Inscribed Angle: Angle formed at the circumference (Angle APB)
• Theorem: Central angle is double the inscribed angle
• Formula: Angle AOB = 2 times Angle APB
• The inscribed angle remains constant for any point on the same arc segment

Angles in the Same Segment and Angles in a Semicircle: Derivations and Visuals

• Angle subtended by a diameter at the circumference is 90 degrees.
• The angle in a semicircle is always a right angle.
• Angles in the same segment are equal in measure.
• Central angle is twice the inscribed angle in the same segment.
• Points must stay on the same side of the chord for equality.

Cyclic Quadrilaterals: Properties, Theorems, and Sum of Opposite Angles

• Definition: A quadrilateral with all four vertices on a circle's circumference.
• Theorem: The sum of opposite angles in a cyclic quadrilateral is 180 degrees.
• Opposite angles are supplementary (Angle A + Angle C = 180 degrees).
• Proof involves the relationship between central angles and inscribed angles.
• If one angle is known, its opposite is found by: 180 minus the given angle.

Common Pitfalls: Identifying Conceptual Errors and Calculation Mistakes

• Diameter is the longest chord; always check if it passes through the center.
• The perpendicular from the center bisects the chord; non-perpendicular lines do not.
• Central angle is exactly double the inscribed angle on the same arc.
• Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral sum to one hundred and eighty degrees.
• In right triangles, the radius is always the hypotenuse, not the chord.
