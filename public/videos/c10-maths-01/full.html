The Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic: Concept and Prime Factorization

• Every composite number is a product of prime numbers.
• Prime factorization is unique, regardless of the order of factors.
• Prime Factorization: Breaking a number into its prime components.
• Example: 120 = 2 raised to the power 2 times 3 times 5.
• The 'DNA' concept: Prime factors uniquely identify a number.

Finding HCF and LCM using Prime Factorization: Theory and Methods

• HCF: Product of the smallest power of each common prime factor.
• LCM: Product of the greatest power of all prime factors involved.
• Example: HCF of 12 and 18 is 2^1 * 3^1 = 6.
• Example: LCM of 12 and 18 is 2^2 * 3^2 = 36.
• Always use prime factorization as the foundation for accuracy.

Relationship between HCF and LCM of Two Numbers: Proofs and Examples

• Theorem: Product of two numbers = HCF × LCM
• HCF uses the lowest powers of common prime factors
• LCM uses the highest powers of all prime factors
• Formula for finding LCM: (a × b) / HCF
• Caution: This specific rule only applies to TWO numbers

Application of HCF and LCM in Real-Life Word Problems

• HCF: Used for splitting into largest possible equal groups or parts.
• LCM: Used for finding when repeating events will coincide or meet.
• Keywords for HCF: Maximum, Greatest, Largest, Dividing equally.
• Keywords for LCM: Minimum, Smallest, Least, Next time, Together.
• Strategy: Identify if the answer should be smaller (HCF) or larger (LCM) than inputs.

Revisiting Irrational Numbers: Definitions and Properties

• Irrational numbers cannot be expressed as p/q where p and q are integers.
• Decimal expansion: Non-terminating and non-repeating patterns.
• Examples: Square root of 2, 3, 5 and the constant Pi.
• Rational + Irrational = Always Irrational.
• Irrational + Irrational = Can be Rational or Irrational.

Formal Proof of Irrationality: Proving √2, √3, and √5 are Irrational

• Method used: Proof by Contradiction
• Assumption: Assume the number is rational (p/q)
• Condition: p and q must be co-prime (no common factors)
• Step 1: Square both sides to remove the radical
• Step 2: Show both p and q share a common factor to find contradiction

Proving Irrationality of Combined Numbers: (a + √b) and (a√b) types

• Method: Proof by Contradiction (Assume the opposite is true)
• Type 1: Proving (a + √b) is irrational by isolating √b
• Type 2: Proving (a√b) is irrational by dividing by 'a'
• Key Logic: An irrational number cannot equal a rational number
• Final Step: Conclude that the initial assumption was false

Decimal Expansions: Terminating vs Non-terminating Repeating Decimals

• Terminating decimals: Division ends after a finite number of digits.
• Non-terminating repeating: Decimals that never end but follow a pattern.
• Terminating condition: Denominator q must be in the form 2^m * 5^n.
• Rational numbers: Always result in terminating or repeating decimals.
• Irrational numbers: Always result in non-terminating, non-repeating decimals.

Rationality and Irrationality in Decimal Form: Identifying Patterns

• Rational numbers: Terminating or Non-terminating repeating decimals.
• Irrational numbers: Non-terminating and non-repeating decimals.
• Repeating decimals use a bar notation to show the repeating block.
• A pattern of increasing digits (like 0.101001...) is still irrational.
• Key Test: Does a fixed block of digits repeat infinitely?

Common Pitfalls, Calculation Errors, and Conceptual Misunderstandings

• One is neither prime nor composite; do not include it in factorization.
• HCF uses smallest powers; LCM uses highest powers of prime factors.
• The HCF times LCM formula applies ONLY to two numbers, not three.
• In contradiction proofs, always isolate the irrational term clearly.
• Non-terminating repeating decimals are rational, not irrational.
