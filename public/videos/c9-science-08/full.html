Introduction: The Physics of Motion and Why Force Governs Our Universe

• Motion involves changes in state: rest, speed, or direction.
• Force is defined as a push or a pull acting on an object.
• Force is required to change an object's velocity.
• Force governs all physical interactions in the universe.
• This chapter explores Newton's three fundamental laws of motion.

Concept of Force: Balanced and Unbalanced Forces with Real-world Activities

• Forces often act in pairs or groups rather than in isolation.
• Balanced Forces: Net force is zero; no change in state of motion.
• Unbalanced Forces: Net force is non-zero; causes acceleration.
• Balanced forces can change an object's shape but not its velocity.
• Unbalanced forces are required to change speed or direction.

Newton's First Law of Motion: The Concept of Inertia and Mass Dependency

• Newton's First Law: Objects resist changes to their state of rest or motion.
• Inertia: The inherent property of matter to resist changes in motion.
• The Law of Inertia: An unbalanced force is required to change motion.
• Mass as a Measure: Higher mass equals higher inertia.
• Mass-Inertia Relationship: Inertia is directly proportional to mass.

A split-screen 3D animation showing a small marble and a heavy bowling ball being pushed by the same force, demonstrating the difference in acceleration and resistance.

Part 4 of 12 · ~4 min

Types of Inertia: Inertia of Rest, Motion, and Direction with Practical Examples

• Inertia of Rest: Tendency to remain stationary (e.g., falling backward in a starting bus).
• Inertia of Motion: Tendency to maintain velocity (e.g., jerking forward when brakes are applied).
• Inertia of Direction: Tendency to maintain a straight path (e.g., leaning outward during a turn).
• Mass is the measure of inertia: More mass equals more resistance to change.
• Practical Safety: Why seatbelts are essential to counter inertia of motion.

Momentum: Mathematical Definition, SI Units, and Vector Nature

• Momentum (p) is the 'quantity of motion' in an object.
• Mathematical Formula: p = m × v (Mass times Velocity)
• SI Unit of Momentum: kilogram meter per second (kg m/s)
• Momentum is a Vector Quantity (has both magnitude and direction)
• Direction of momentum is the same as the direction of velocity

Part 6 of 12 · ~5 min

Newton's Second Law of Motion: Derivation of F = ma and Concept of Acceleration

• Second Law: Force is proportional to the rate of change of momentum.
• Initial Momentum = m × u; Final Momentum = m × v.
• Change in Momentum = m(v - u).
• Derivation: F = m × a (Force = mass × acceleration).
• Acceleration is directly proportional to Force and inversely to Mass.

Part 7 of 12 · ~5 min

Second Law Deep Dive: Worked Examples, Force Calculations, and Unit Conversions

• Always convert mass to kilograms (kg) before calculating.
• Convert velocity from km/h to m/s by multiplying by 5/18.
• Formula: Force = mass × acceleration (F = ma).
• Rearrange for mass: m = F/a; Rearrange for acceleration: a = F/m.
• 1 kilonewton (kN) is equal to 1,000 Newtons (N).

Newton's Third Law of Motion: Action-Reaction Pairs and Common Misconceptions

• Newton's Third Law: Every action has an equal and opposite reaction.
• Action and reaction forces always act on two different objects.
• Because they act on different objects, they never cancel each other out.
• Action and reaction forces occur simultaneously with no time delay.
• Misconception: Action and reaction are not applied to the same body.

Part 9 of 12 · ~3 min

Applications of Third Law: Recoil of Guns, Walking, and Rocket Propulsion

• Action and reaction forces always act on two different bodies.
• Recoil of a gun: Bullet moves forward, gun moves backward.
• Walking: Foot pushes ground backward, ground pushes foot forward.
• Rocket Propulsion: Exhaust gases push down, rocket moves up.
• Thrust is the reaction force that enables rocket flight.

Law of Conservation of Momentum: Derivation and Mathematical Framework

• Law: Total momentum remains constant if no external unbalanced force acts.
• Initial Momentum = m1u1 + m2u2
• Final Momentum = m1v1 + m2v2
• Newton's Third Law: F12 = -F21 ensures momentum balance.
• Conservation Equation: m1u1 + m2u2 = m1v1 + m2v2
