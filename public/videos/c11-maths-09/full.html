The Cartesian Plane: Distance Formula, Section Formula, and Area of a Triangle

• Distance Formula: d = √[(x₂ - x₁)² + (y₂ - y₁)²]
• Section Formula: P = (m₁x₂ + m₂x₁)/(m₁ + m₂) , (m₁y₂ + m₂y₁)/(m₁ + m₂)
• Midpoint Formula: M = [(x₁ + x₂)/2 , (y₁ + y₂)/2]
• Area of Triangle: ½ |x₁(y₂ - y₃) + x₂(y₃ - y₁) + x₃(y₁ - y₂)|
• If Area = 0, then the three points are collinear

Slope of a Line: Angle of Inclination, Trigonometric Relationship, and Slope between Two Points

• Angle of Inclination (theta): Angle made with the positive direction of the x-axis.
• Trigonometric Relation: Slope (m) = tan(theta).
• Slope Formula: m = (y2 - y1) / (x2 - x1) for two points (x1, y1) and (x2, y2).
• Positive Slope: Line rises from left to right; Negative Slope: Line falls from left to right.
• Undefined Slope: Occurs when the line is vertical (theta = 90 degrees).

Various Forms of Equations of a Line I: Slope-Intercept Form and Point-Slope Form

• Slope-Intercept Form: y = mx + c
• m represents the slope; c represents the y-intercept
• Point-Slope Form: y - y1 = m(x - x1)
• Use Point-Slope form when one point and slope are known
• Simplify Point-Slope equations to reach the general form

Part 5 of 12 · ~3 min

Various Forms of Equations of a Line II: Two-Point Form and Intercept Form

• Two-Point Form: Used when two distinct points (x1, y1) and (x2, y2) are known.
• Formula: (y - y1) = [(y2 - y1) / (x2 - x1)] * (x - x1)
• Intercept Form: Used when x-intercept 'a' and y-intercept 'b' are known.
• Formula: x/a + y/b = 1
• Note: Intercept form is undefined if the line passes through the origin.

Various Forms of Equations of a Line III: Normal Form and General Equation Derivations

• Normal Form: x cos theta + y sin theta = p
• p is the perpendicular distance from origin (always positive)
• theta is the angle between the normal and positive x-axis
• General Equation: Ax + By + C = 0 (A and B cannot both be zero)
• Slope from General Equation: m = -A / B

Angle Between Two Lines: Acute Angle Formula and Conditions for Parallel and Perpendicular

• Angle between lines is typically defined as the acute angle.
• Formula: tan(alpha) = |(m2 - m1) / (1 + m1*m2)|
• Condition for Parallel Lines: m1 = m2
• Condition for Perpendicular Lines: m1 * m2 = -1
• Perpendicular slopes are negative reciprocals of each other.

Distance of a Point from a Line and Distance Between Two Parallel Lines

• Perpendicular distance d = |Ax1 + By1 + C| / sqrt(A^2 + B^2)
• Distance is always positive; use absolute values in the numerator
• Parallel lines must have identical A and B coefficients
• Distance between parallel lines = |C1 - C2| / sqrt(A^2 + B^2)
• Always normalize equations before calculating distance between lines

Parametric Form of a Line and Linear Inequalities in Two Variables

• Parametric Form: x = x1 + r cos(theta), y = y1 + r sin(theta)
• r represents the signed distance from the fixed point (x1, y1)
• Linear Inequality: ax + by < c, ax + by > c, ax + by <= c, or ax + by >= c
• Dashed line for strict inequalities; Solid line for inclusive inequalities
• Test point method: Use (0,0) to determine the solution region

Common Pitfalls: Identifying Mistakes in Slope, Intercepts, and Sign Conventions

• Watch for sign errors when subtracting negative coordinates in the slope formula.
• In intercept form, always include the sign of the intercept in your calculation.
• Slope from general form Ax + By + C = 0 is always negative A divided by B.
• Perpendicular slopes are negative reciprocals, not just negative values.
• Angle of inclination theta is always measured between zero and one hundred eighty degrees.
