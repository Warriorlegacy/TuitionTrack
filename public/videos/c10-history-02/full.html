Introduction: The Significance of Nationalism and the Impact of World War I

• Nationalism: A sense of shared identity and collective belonging.
• Impact of WWI: Increased defense expenditure and heavy taxation.
• Economic Hardship: Doubling of prices and introduction of income tax.
• Social Unrest: Forced recruitment in rural areas and crop failures.
• Consequence: Widespread resentment against British colonial rule.

The Idea of Satyagraha: Concept, Philosophy, and Early Experiments

• Satyagraha: The power of truth and the need to search for truth.
• Philosophy: Non-violence as a weapon against injustice, not brute force.
• Champaran (1917): Fighting the oppressive Tinkathia indigo system.
• Kheda (1917): Demanding revenue relaxation due to crop failure.
• Ahmedabad (1918): Supporting cotton mill workers' wage demands.

Part 3 of 12 · ~5 min

The Rowlatt Act and the Jallianwala Bagh Massacre: Causes, Consequences, and Reactions

• The Rowlatt Act (1919): Allowed detention of political prisoners without trial for 2 years.
• Nationwide Satyagraha: Gandhi organized hartals and rallies against the 'Black Act'.
• Jallianwala Bagh (April 13, 1919): General Dyer opened fire on an unarmed crowd in Amritsar.
• Dyer's Objective: To 'strike terror' and produce a 'moral effect' on the Indian people.
• Consequences: Widespread strikes, brutal British repression, and a surge in nationalism.

Differing Strands within the Non-Cooperation Movement: Towns, Countryside, and Plantations

• Urban Movement: Boycott of schools, courts, and foreign cloth by the middle class.
• Economic Impact: Rise of Indian textile mills and handlooms due to cloth boycott.
• Rural Struggle: Peasants in Awadh fought against high rents and the 'begar' system.
• Awadh Movement: Led by Baba Ramchandra and supported by the Oudh Kisan Sabha.
• Plantation Workers: Interpreted Swaraj as freedom of movement under the Inland Emigration Act.

Withdrawal of Non-Cooperation: Chauri Chaura and the Shift in Political Strategy

• Non-Cooperation Movement faced rising violence in early 1922
• Chauri Chaura incident: Protesters set fire to a police station
• Twenty-two policemen died in the Chauri Chaura violence
• Gandhiji withdrew the movement to uphold the principle of Ahimsa
• Shift from mass non-cooperation to political regrouping and reorganization

Towards Civil Disobedience: The Simon Commission and the Demand for Purna Swaraj

• 1927: British government appoints the Simon Commission.
• The Commission lacked any Indian members, causing nationwide outrage.
• Protest slogan: 'Simon Go Back' used by Congress and Muslim League.
• 1929 Lahore Session: Jawaharlal Nehru presides over the Congress.
• Resolution of 'Purna Swaraj'—the demand for Complete Independence.

• Salt as a universal symbol of unity against British monopoly
• Gandhi's 11 demands sent to Viceroy Irwin in January 1930
• The 240-mile march from Sabarmati Ashram to Dandi
• Difference: Non-Cooperation (refusal to cooperate) vs Civil Disobedience (breaking laws)

Limits of Civil Disobedience: Perspectives of Dalits and Muslim Organizations

• Congress's initial hesitation to include Dalit demands to avoid offending high-caste Hindus.
• Dr. B.R. Ambedkar's demand for political empowerment and separate electorates.
