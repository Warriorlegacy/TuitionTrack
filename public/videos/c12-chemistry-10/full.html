Introduction: The Chemistry of Life, Importance of Biomolecules, and Classification

• Biomolecules: Organic compounds produced by living organisms.
• Role: Essential for metabolism, energy, and genetic information.
• Classification: Small biomolecules vs. large macromolecules.
• Key Groups: Carbohydrates, Proteins, Lipids, Nucleic Acids, and Vitamins.
• Building Blocks: Amino acids, monosaccharides, and nucleotides.

Carbohydrates I: Monosaccharides, Structure of Glucose, and Stereoisomerism

• Monosaccharides: Polyhydroxy aldehydes or polyhydroxy ketones
• Classification: Trioses (3C), Tetroses (4C), Pentoses (5C), Hexoses (6C)
• Stereoisomerism: D and L configurations based on chiral center orientation
• Glucose Structure: An aldohexose with formula C6H12O6
• Anomers: Alpha and Beta forms resulting from cyclic hemiacetal formation

A 3D transition showing a linear glucose chain folding into a six-membered pyranose ring, highlighting the alpha and beta anomeric positions.

Part 3 of 12 · ~5 min

Carbohydrates II: Disaccharides, Polysaccharides, and Glycosidic Linkages

• Glycosidic linkage: Formed via dehydration between anomeric carbons
• Sucrose: Glucose + Fructose (alpha-1, beta-2 linkage); Non-reducing sugar
• Maltose & Lactose: Reducing sugars with alpha-1,4 or beta-1,4 linkages
• Starch: Mixture of linear Amylose and branched Amylopectin
• Cellulose: Linear beta-D-glucose polymer providing structural strength

A 3D molecular animation showing two glucose molecules releasing a water molecule to form a covalent glycosidic bond, transitioning into a large branched glycogen tree.

Part 4 of 12 · ~5 min

Proteins I: Amino Acids, Zwitterions, and Classification based on R-groups

• Amino acids consist of an alpha-carbon with amino, carboxyl, H, and R-groups.
• Zwitterion: A dipolar ion with both positive and negative charges.
• Non-polar/Aliphatic: Hydrophobic R-groups like Alanine and Valine.
• Polar/Uncharged: Hydrophilic R-groups like Serine and Threonine.
• Charged: Acidic (negative) like Aspartic acid and Basic (positive) like Lysine.

A 3D molecular model of an amino acid rotating, transitioning into a Zwitterion state with +/- charge indicators, followed by a split-screen comparison of different R-group structures.

Part 5 of 12 · ~5 min

Proteins II: Peptide Bond Formation, Primary to Quaternary Structures, and Denaturation

• Peptide bond: A covalent amide linkage formed via condensation reaction.
• Primary Structure: The linear sequence of amino acids in a polypeptide.
• Secondary Structure: Alpha-helices and Beta-pleated sheets via hydrogen bonding.
• Tertiary Structure: 3D folding stabilized by R-group interactions.
• Denaturation: Loss of 3D shape due to heat or pH change; primary structure remains.

A 3D animation showing a linear chain of amino acids folding into a helix, then a complex globular shape, and finally unravelling when heat is applied.

Proteins III: Essential vs Non-essential Amino Acids, Peptide Tests, and Biological Import

• Essential Amino Acids: Must be obtained through diet
• Non-essential Amino Acids: Synthesized by the body
• Biuret Test: Copper sulfate reacts with peptide bonds (Violet color)
• Ninhydrin Test: Detects free alpha-amino groups (Ruhemann's purple)
• Biological Roles: Enzymes, Hormones, Transport, and Immunity

Part 7 of 12 · ~5 min

Lipids I: Fatty Acids, Saturated vs Unsaturated, and Triglycerides

• Lipids: Insoluble in water, soluble in organic solvents
• Fatty Acids: Long hydrocarbon chains with a carboxyl group
• Saturated Fats: Single bonds only, solid at room temperature
• Unsaturated Fats: One or more double bonds, liquid at room temperature
• Triglycerides: Glycerol + 3 Fatty Acids via esterification

A 3D molecular animation showing a glycerol molecule reacting with three fatty acid chains to form a triglyceride, releasing three water molecules.

Part 8 of 12 · ~5 min

Lipids II: Phospholipids, Steroids, and Micelle Formation Mechanisms

• Phospholipids are amphipathic: hydrophilic heads and hydrophobic tails.
• Phospholipid structure: Glycerol + two fatty acids + phosphate group.
• Steroids feature a core of four fused rings (perhydrocyclopentanophenanthrene).
• Cholesterol is a key steroid regulating membrane fluidity.
• Micelles form when amphipathic molecules hide tails in a central core.

A 3D animation showing phospholipid molecules spontaneously organizing from a chaotic state into a structured lipid bilayer in an aqueous solution.

Part 9 of 12 · ~5 min

Nucleic Acids I: Structure of Nucleotides, Nucleosides, and DNA vs RNA

• Nucleotide components: Nitrogenous base, Pentose sugar, and Phosphoric acid
• Purines (Adenine, Guanine) have double rings; Pyrimidines (C, T, U) have single rings
• Nucleoside = Base + Sugar; Nucleotide = Base + Sugar + Phosphate
• DNA contains Deoxyribose and Thymine; RNA contains Ribose and Uracil
• Phosphodiester linkages connect nucleotides into a polymer chain

A 3D molecular assembly animation showing a Nitrogenous base attaching to a Pentose sugar to form a nucleoside, followed by the addition of a Phosphate group to create a nucleotide.

Part 10 of 12 · ~5 min

Nucleic Acids II: Watson-Crick Model, Base Pairing, and Genetic Information Flow

• DNA structure: Double helix with anti-parallel strands
• Backbone: Covalent phosphodiester bonds
• Base Pairing: Adenine with Thymine (2 H-bonds), Guanine with Cytosine (3 H-bonds)
• Central Dogma: DNA $\rightarrow$ RNA $\rightarrow$ Protein
• Processes: Replication, Transcription, and Translation

A 3D rotating double helix model showing the sugar-phosphate backbone and hydrogen bonds forming between A-T and G-C pairs.

Part 11 of 12 · ~5 min

Vitamins: Classification, Water-soluble vs Fat-soluble, and Deficiency Diseases

• Vitamins: Essential organic regulators for metabolic processes.
• Fat-soluble: Vitamins A, D, E, K (Stored in liver/fat; risk of toxicity).
• Water-soluble: Vitamin C and B-complex (Excreted in urine; daily intake needed).
• Deficiency Examples: Vitamin A (Night Blindness), Vitamin C (Scurvy), Vitamin D (Rickets).
• Vitamin B Complex: Includes Thiamine (B1), Riboflavin (B2), Niacin (B3), and B12.
