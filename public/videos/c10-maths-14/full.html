Fundamental Definitions: Experiment, Outcome, Sample Space, and Events

• Experiment: A process that results in one or more observable results.
• Outcome: A single possible result of a random experiment.
• Sample Space (S): The set of all possible outcomes of an experiment.
• Event (E): A subset of the sample space representing specific outcomes.
• Impossible Event: An event with no outcomes; Certain Event: All outcomes.

Theoretical vs. Experimental Probability: The Classical Approach

• Experimental Probability: Based on actual observations and recorded data.
• Theoretical Probability: Based on mathematical reasoning and logic.
• Formula: P(E) = (Number of favorable outcomes) / (Total number of outcomes).
• Law of Large Numbers: More trials lead to experimental results matching theory.
• Classical Approach assumes all outcomes in a sample space are equally likely.

The Probability Formula: Calculating P(E) and Understanding Limits

• Formula: P(E) = (Number of favorable outcomes) / (Total number of outcomes)
• The probability of any event is always between 0 and 1 (inclusive)
• P(E) = 0 means the event is impossible
• P(E) = 1 means the event is certain
• Always express probability as a simplified fraction, decimal, or percentage

Complementary Events: The Rule of P(E) + P(not E) = 1

• An event E and its opposite 'not E' are called complementary events.
• The sum of P(E) and P(not E) is always equal to 1.
• Formula: P(E) + P(not E) = 1.
• Use the complement rule to simplify complex 'at least' problems.
• The complement covers all outcomes in the sample space except E.

Probability in Tossing Coins: Single, Double, and Triple Toss Scenarios

• Single Coin: Sample Space S = {H, T}; Total outcomes = 2
• Two Coins: Sample Space S = {HH, HT, TH, TT}; Total outcomes = 4
• Three Coins: Total outcomes = 2 to the power of 3 = 8
• Key Terms: 'Exactly' means specific count; 'At least' means that number or more
• Key Terms: 'At most' means that number or fewer

Probability with Numbers: Selecting Integers, Primes, and Multiples from Sets

• Sample Space: The total set of all possible integer outcomes.
• Prime Numbers: Natural numbers greater than 1 with only two factors.
• Multiples: Numbers obtained by multiplying a given integer by another.
• Composite Numbers: Integers greater than 1 that are not prime.
• Avoid Double-Counting: Check for numbers that fit two categories.

Complex Word Problems: Combining Multiple Probability Concepts

• Step 1: Identify the total sample space carefully.
• Step 2: Break complex conditions into smaller events.
• Step 3: Watch out for overlapping outcomes (Double Counting).
• Step 4: Use the Complementary Rule for 'At Least' problems.
• Step 5: Apply P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A and B).

Common Pitfalls: Avoiding Calculation Errors and Logical Fallacies

• Range Check: Probability must always be between 0 and 1 inclusive.
• Sample Space: Ensure all outcomes are listed; order matters in coin tosses.
• Complement Rule: Use '1 minus P(not E)' for 'at least one' problems.
• Logical Operators: Distinguish between 'and' (multiplication) and 'or' (addition).
• Precision: Avoid double-counting overlaps and always simplify fractions.
