Introduction: The Pre-Modern World, Silk Routes, and Food Travels

• Globalization is a long-standing historical process, not just a modern one.
• Silk Routes: Vast networks linking Asia, Europe, and Northern Africa.
• Silk Routes facilitated the exchange of goods, religion, and culture.
• Food Travels: Many staples like potatoes and chilies originated in the Americas.
• The Irish Potato Famine illustrates the risks of global food dependency.

The 19th Century (1815-1914): The Three Flows of International Trade

• The 19th Century (1815-1914) saw intense global economic integration.
• Flow 1: Trade - Movement of goods like wheat, cotton, and meat.
• Flow 2: Labour - Large-scale migration of people for employment.
• Flow 3: Capital - Movement of money for short-term or long-term investment.
• These three flows were interconnected and driven by colonial expansion.

A 3D animated globe showing three distinct colored streams of light—gold for capital, blue for trade, and green for labour—spiraling and interconnecting across continents.

Part 3 of 12 · ~4 min

The Role of Technology: Railways, Steamships, and the Impact of Colonialism

• Steamships replaced sailing ships, making sea trade faster and more predictable.
• Railways connected resource-rich interiors directly to coastal export ports.
• The telegraph enabled real-time communication and global market synchronization.
• Technology facilitated the extraction of raw materials for European industries.
• Technological progress often reinforced colonial control and economic inequality.

A 3D split-screen animation: one side shows a slow wooden sailing ship in a storm, the other shows a powerful steamship cutting through calm waves, transitioning into a map of railway lines spreading like veins from African and Indian interiors to the coast.

Late 19th Century Colonialism: Indentured Labour and the New System of Slavery

• Shift from traditional slavery to indentured labour contracts.
• Major migration routes: India and China to Caribbean, Fiji, and Mauritius.
• Deceptive recruitment practices and harsh plantation conditions.
• Indentured labour often described as a 'new system of slavery'.
• Cultural fusion: Emergence of Chutney music and Hosay celebrations.

A split-screen 3D animation: on the left, a dark, crowded recruitment camp in an Indian village; on the right, a vast, sun-drenched but oppressive sugar plantation in the Caribbean.

Part 5 of 12 · ~4 min

The Inter-war Economy: The Impact of the First World War and Mass Production

• WWI was the first modern industrial war, mobilizing entire economies.
• Shift in power: Britain moved from debtor to debtor; US became a creditor.
• Mass Production: Henry Ford's assembly line revolutionized manufacturing.
• The Assembly Line: Increased efficiency through repetitive, specialized tasks.
• The Cycle: Higher wages led to mass consumption and economic growth.

A 3D split-screen animation: on the left, dark industrial factories producing artillery; on the right, a bright, fast-moving Ford assembly line with cars moving on a conveyor belt.

Part 6 of 12 · ~4 min

The Great Depression: Causes, Global Impact, and Economic Consequences

• Agricultural overproduction led to a drastic fall in global food prices.
• The 1929 US Stock Market crash triggered a massive banking crisis.
• Withdrawal of US loans caused a liquidity crisis in European economies.
• Colonial economies like India faced severe rural distress and debt.
• The Depression demonstrated the risks of global economic interconnectedness.

A 3D motion graphic showing a glowing web of gold lines connecting the US to Europe and India, which suddenly turns red and snaps, causing the lines to collapse into darkness.

Part 7 of 12 · ~4 min

Rebuilding the World Economy: The Post-War Era and the Bretton Woods Institutions

• Bretton Woods Conference (1944) aimed at post-war economic reconstruction.
• Creation of the IMF and the World Bank (Bretton Woods Twins).
• The IMF focused on global monetary stability and short-term loans.
• The World Bank focused on long-term reconstruction and development.
• System based on fixed exchange rates tied to the US Dollar and Gold.

A 3D cinematic fly-through of a 1940s conference hall transitioning into a digital infographic showing the link between Gold, the US Dollar, and global currencies.

Decolonization and the Challenges of New Nations in a Globalized World

• Decolonization: The emergence of independent nations in Asia and Africa.
• Challenge 1: Building political stability and national identity.
• Challenge 2: Overcoming extractive colonial economic structures.
• Challenge 3: Navigating the bipolar world of the Cold War.
• The Non-Aligned Movement: Seeking sovereignty amidst superpower rivalry.

The End of Bretton Woods and the Rise of MNCs and Global Finance

• Collapse of the Bretton Woods fixed exchange rate system
• Transition from fixed to floating exchange rates
• Rise of Multinational Corporations (MNCs) and cost-cutting
• Fragmentation of production across different nations
• Increased mobility of capital and global financial integration
