Types of Employment: Permanent vs. Casual Workers

• Permanent Employment: Fixed contracts and predictable monthly salaries.
• Social Security: Benefits like paid leave, medical aid, and pensions.
• Casual Employment: Day-to-day hiring with no long-term guarantee.
• Income Instability: Earnings depend entirely on the availability of daily work.
• Key Difference: Stability and benefits versus flexibility and high risk.

Working in the Street: Street Vendors and Their Challenges

• Street vendors provide affordable goods and services to urban residents.
• Vending is a key source of livelihood for millions in the informal sector.
• Challenges include lack of permanent space and legal recognition.
• Income is highly unpredictable and dependent on weather and location.
• Vendors lack social security benefits like medical insurance or pensions.

Working in Factories: The Life of a Casual Laborer

• Casual laborers are hired on an as-needed basis without fixed contracts.
• Work is often unpredictable, leading to daily income uncertainty.
• Many workers are hired through contractors rather than directly by factories.
• Working conditions can be physically demanding with limited safety gear.
• Lack of benefits like paid leave, medical security, or pension schemes.

The Service Sector: Shops, Offices, and Small Businesses

• The Service Sector provides services rather than physical goods.
• Retail includes everything from large supermarkets to local Kirana stores.
• Office work involves organized environments like banks and IT firms.
• Small businesses like salons and repair shops rely on specialized skills.
• Self-employment offers independence but lacks a fixed monthly salary.

The Formal Sector: Security, Benefits, and Job Stability

• Formal Sector: Characterized by regular work and legal employment contracts.
• Job Security: Protection against arbitrary dismissal through legal procedures.
• Employee Benefits: Includes Provident Fund, health insurance, and paid leaves.
• Regulated Hours: Strict laws govern daily working hours and weekly holidays.
• Legal Protection: Governed by labor laws and the right to minimum wage.

Common Pitfalls: Misunderstanding Employment Terms and Concepts

• Casual Labor vs. Self-Employment: Working for wages vs. working for profit.
• Formal vs. Informal Sector: Defined by legal protections and social security.
• Seasonal vs. Disguised Unemployment: Time-based gaps vs. excess manpower.
• Minimum Wage: A legal floor, not just a suggested starting salary.
• Key Tip: Always look for 'job security' and 'contracts' to identify sectors.

Exam Technique: How to Answer Descriptive and Case-Based Questions

• Use Point-Evidence-Explanation (PEE) for descriptive answers.
• Organize long answers with clear headings and bullet points.
• For Case-Based questions, stick strictly to the provided text.
• Read passages twice: once for context, once for evidence.
• Use technical terms like 'Formal Sector' and 'Social Security'.
