The method — 4 steps
STEP 1 · REFLECTIONApply Law of Reflection: $\angle i = \angle r$ and light stays in the same plane.
STEP 2 · REFRACTIONUse Snell's Law: $n_1 \sin i = n_2 \sin r$ to find light's path change.
STEP 3 · LENSESApply Lens Maker's Formula: $\frac{1}{f} = (n-1)(\frac{1}{R_1} - \frac{1}{R_2})$ to find focal length.
STEP 4 · OPTICSUse Magnification $m = \frac{v}{u}$ for mirrors and $m = \frac{v}{u}$ for lenses.

An object is placed 20cm from a convex lens of focal length 10cm. Find image position. Using $1/v - 1/u = 1/f$: $1/v - 1/(-20) = 1/10 \implies 1/v = 1/10 - 1/20 = 1/20 \implies v = 20cm$. Answer: Real image at 20cm.

Topic 1 · Reflection and Spherical Mirrors

Reflection and Spherical Mirrors

Light bounces off surfaces; spherical mirrors curve to focus light like a satellite dish. It follows the rule that the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection.

• Mirror Formula: $1/f = 1/v + 1/u$
• Magnification $m = -v/u$
• Sign convention: direction of incident light is positive

Find $f$ if $R=30cm$. $f = R/2 = 15cm$.

Topic 2 · Refraction at Spherical Surfaces and Lenses

Refraction at Spherical Surfaces and Lenses

Light bends when moving between media of different densities, like a straw looking broken in water. This bending allows lenses to focus images.

• Snell's Law: $n_1 \sin i = n_2 \sin r$
• Lens Maker's Formula: $\frac{1}{f} = (n-1)(\frac{1}{R_1} - \frac{1}{R_2})$
• Lens Formula: $1/v - 1/u = 1/f$

If $n=1.5$ and $R_1=R_2=\infty$ (plane), $f$ is infinite. For a lens, $n$ determines power.

Topic 3 · Refraction through a Prism

Refraction through a Prism

A prism bends light and splits it into colors because different colors bend at different angles. It is like a race where different runners (colors) take different turns.

• Angle of deviation: $\delta = (i + e) - A$
• Minimum deviation occurs when $i = e$
• Refractive index $n = \sin((A+\delta_m)/2) / \sin(A/2)$

For $A=60^\circ$ and $\delta_m=30^\circ$, $n = \sin(45)/\sin(30) = 1.414$.

Topic 4 · Optical Instruments

Optical Instruments

Devices like microscopes and telescopes use multiple lenses to magnify tiny or distant objects. They act like magnifying glasses for the whole world.

• Simple Microscope: $M = 1 + D/f$
• Compound Microscope: $M = m_o \times m_e$
• Astronomical Telescope: $M = f_o / f_e$

Microscope with $f_o=1cm, f_e=5cm$ has $M = 1/5$ (ratio of focal lengths).
