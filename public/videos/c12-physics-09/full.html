Reflection of Light: Laws, Spherical Mirrors, and Mirror Formula Derivations

• Laws of Reflection: Incident angle equals reflected angle
• Concave mirrors converge light; Convex mirrors diverge light
• Relationship: Focal length f equals Radius of Curvature R divided by 2
• Mirror Formula: 1 over v plus 1 over u equals 1 over f
• Magnification m equals negative v divided by u

A 3D ray diagram showing a concave mirror reflecting parallel rays through a single focal point, transitioning into a geometric derivation of the mirror formula using similar triangles.

Part 3 of 12 · ~5 min

Refraction of Light: Snell's Law, Refractive Index, and Lateral Shift

• Refraction: Bending of light due to change in speed between media
• Snell's Law: sine i / sine r = constant (Refractive Index)
• Absolute Refractive Index: n = c / v
• Denser to Rarer: Bends away from normal; Rarer to Denser: Bends towards normal
• Lateral Shift: Perpendicular distance between incident and emergent rays

A 3D ray diagram showing a light beam entering a glass slab, bending towards the normal, and emerging parallel to the original path with a highlighted lateral displacement vector.

Part 4 of 12 · ~5 min

Total Internal Reflection: Critical Angle, TIR Applications, and Optical Fibers

• TIR occurs when light travels from denser to rarer medium
• Critical Angle: Refracted ray travels along the boundary
• Formula: sine of theta critical equals one divided by refractive index
• Condition 1: Denser to rarer medium; Condition 2: Angle of incidence > Critical angle
• Optical Fibers use continuous TIR for high-speed data transmission

A 3D animation showing a light ray traveling through a glass block, hitting the boundary at increasing angles until it skims the surface and then reflects back entirely.

Part 5 of 12 · ~5 min

Refraction at Spherical Surfaces: Derivations and Lens Maker's Formula

• Refraction at a single spherical surface: n2/v - n1/u = (n2 - n1)/R
• Small angle approximation: sin theta is approximately equal to theta
• Lens Maker's Formula: 1/f = (n2/n1) * (1/R1 - 1/R2)
• Focal length depends on refractive index and surface curvature
• Sign convention is critical for R1 and R2 in lens design

A 3D transparent glass lens rotating in space, showing light rays bending at both surfaces with mathematical labels appearing for u, v, R1, and R2.

Part 6 of 12 · ~5 min

Thin Lenses: Lens Formula, Magnification, and Power of Lenses

• Thin Lens Approximation: Thickness is negligible compared to radii of curvature.
• Lens Formula: One over v, minus one over u, equals one over f.
• Linear Magnification: m equals h prime over h, or v over u.
• Power of Lens: P equals one over f, measured in Dioptres (D).
• Sign Convention: Convex f is positive; Concave f is negative.

Combination of Lenses, Refraction through Prisms, and Dispersion

• Effective Power: P = P1 + P2
• Effective Focal Length: 1/F = 1/f1 + 1/f2
• Prism Angle (A) and Deviation (delta) relationship
• Minimum Deviation: delta = delta sub m
• Dispersion: Splitting of light due to wavelength-dependent refractive index

Part 8 of 12 · ~5 min

Prism Mathematics: Angle of Deviation, Minimum Deviation, and Dispersion Formula

• Angle of Deviation (Delta): Angle between incident and emergent rays
• Prism Relation: A + Delta = i + e
• Minimum Deviation: Occurs when angle of incidence equals angle of emergence
• Prism Formula: n = sin((A + Delta-m)/2) / sin(A/2)
• Dispersive Power (omega): (nv - nr) / n_mean

Optical Instruments II: Microscopes (Simple and Compound) and Telescopes

• Compound Microscope: Objective + Eyepiece combination
• Microscope Magnification: M = (v_o/u_o) * (D/f_e)
• Astronomical Telescope: Large objective for light collection
• Telescope Magnification (at infinity): M = F_o / f_e

Advanced Optical Instruments: Astronomical Telescopes and Resolving Power

• Astronomical Telescopes: Designed for objects at effectively infinite distances.
• Keplerian Telescope: Uses two convex lenses; produces an inverted image.
• Magnification Formula: M equals f-sub-o divided by f-sub-e.
• Resolving Power: The ability to distinguish two closely spaced objects.
• Rayleigh's Criterion: Resolution limit equals one point two two lambda over D.

Exam Masterclass: Problem-Solving Strategies, Common Mistakes, and Full Recap

• Always start with a precise, labeled ray diagram.
• Strictly follow the Cartesian sign convention.
• Distinguish clearly between magnification and power.
• At minimum deviation, angle of incidence equals angle of emergence.
• Check sign conventions for concave versus convex elements.
