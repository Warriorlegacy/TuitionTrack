Grammar Focus: Understanding Direct Speech and Narrative Tenses

• Direct Speech: Using quotation marks for exact words spoken by characters.
• Direct Speech Formula: Speaker + Reporting Verb + Comma + 'Spoken Words'.
• Past Simple: Subject + V-two (e.g., 'The Djinn said') for completed actions.
• Past Continuous: Subject + was/were + verb-ing for ongoing background actions.
• Past Perfect: Subject + had + V-three for actions completed before another past action.

Literary Devices: Personification, Imagery, and Anthropomorphism

• Anthropomorphism: Animals given human traits, speech, and emotions.
• Personification: Giving human qualities to inanimate objects or ideas.
• Imagery: Descriptive language that appeals to the five senses.
• Difference: Anthropomorphism builds characters; Personification builds mood.
• Exam Tip: Use specific sensory details to explain imagery in your answers.

Common Pitfalls: Avoiding Misinterpretations of the Moral

• Distinguish between 'Punishment' and 'Consequence' or 'Remedy'.
• Characterize the Camel as 'Indolent' rather than 'Malicious'.
• Understand the moral as a pursuit of 'Social Equilibrium'.
• Treat the story as a 'Universal Fable', not a biological fact.
• Use sophisticated vocabulary: 'Restitution', 'Indolence', and 'Equilibrium'.
