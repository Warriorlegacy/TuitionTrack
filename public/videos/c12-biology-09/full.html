Part 3 of 12 · ~4 min

Tools of Recombinant DNA Technology: Restriction Endonucleases and DNA Ligase

• Restriction Endonucleases: Molecular scissors that cut DNA at specific sites.
• Palindromic Sequences: DNA sequences that read the same in 5' to 3' on both strands.
• Sticky Ends: Staggered cuts creating single-stranded overhangs for easy pairing.
• Blunt Ends: Straight cuts through both strands with no overhangs.
• DNA Ligase: The molecular glue that forms covalent phosphodiester bonds.

A 3D molecular animation showing the EcoRI enzyme docking onto a DNA double helix, cutting it to reveal glowing sticky ends, followed by DNA ligase sealing a new fragment into place.

Tools of rDNA Technology: Cloning Vectors, Plasmids, and Bacteriophages

• Cloning Vectors: Molecular vehicles to carry and replicate foreign DNA
• Essential Feature 1: Origin of Replication (O-R-I) for independent replication
• Essential Feature 2: Cloning Sites for precise insertion of DNA fragments
• Essential Feature 3: Selectable Markers to identify transformed host cells
• Plasmids vs. Bacteriophages: Size capacity and method of entry

Advanced Vectors: Expression Vectors, Selectable Markers, and Insertional Inactivation

• Expression Vectors: Contain promoters and ribosome binding sites for protein synthesis.
• Selectable Markers: Genes like antibiotic resistance used to identify transformants.
• The Selection Challenge: Distinguishing between transformed and non-transformed cells.
• Insertional Inactivation: Disruption of a marker gene by the insertion of foreign DNA.
• Verification: Using loss of resistance to confirm successful gene integration.

The Process of rDNA Technology: Isolation, Fragmentation, and Ligation

• Step 1: Isolation of DNA using enzymes like Lysozyme, Cellulase, and Protease
• Step 2: Purification via organic solvents and ethanol precipitation
• Step 3: Fragmentation using Restriction Endonucleases at palindromic sites
• Step 4: Creation of complementary 'sticky ends' for precise cutting
• Step 5: Ligation using DNA Ligase to form covalent phosphodiester bonds

Transformation and Cloning: Competent Cells and Selection of Transformants

Problem Solving: Calculating Transformation Efficiency and Genetic Mapping

• Transformation Efficiency = Total Colonies / Micrograms of DNA used
• High efficiency indicates high-quality competent cells and optimized protocols
• Genetic Mapping determines the relative positions of genes or markers
• Restriction enzyme mapping uses fragment sizes to deduce DNA structure
• Double digestion provides overlapping data to reconstruct a DNA map

Common Pitfalls: Conceptual Errors in Restriction Sites and Vector Selection

• Restriction enzymes require exact palindromic sequences for recognition.
• Sticky ends must be perfectly complementary for successful ligation.
• Cloning vectors replicate DNA; Expression vectors produce proteins.
• Selectable markers identify transformants; Reporter genes screen recombinants.
• The Origin of Replication (O-R-I) must be compatible with the host cell.
