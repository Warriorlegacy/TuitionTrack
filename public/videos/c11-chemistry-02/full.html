Discovery of Subatomic Particles: Cathode Ray, Anode Ray, and Electron Properties

• Discharge tube experiment: High voltage in a vacuum tube
• Cathode rays: Streams of negatively charged electrons
• Properties: Travel in straight lines and deflect to positive plates
• Electrons are universal components of all matter
• Anode rays: Positive ions that depend on the gas used

Thomson’s Model, Rutherford’s Alpha-Scattering Experiment, and Nuclear Postulates

• Thomson's Model: Positive sphere with embedded negative electrons
• Rutherford's Experiment: Alpha particle bombardment of gold foil
• Observation: Most particles pass through; some deflect at large angles
• Nuclear Postulate: Mass and positive charge concentrated in a tiny nucleus
• Planetary Model: Electrons revolve around the nucleus in orbits

Atomic Number, Mass Number, Isotopes, Isobars, and Isotonic Concepts

• Atomic Number (Z): Total number of protons in the nucleus.
• Mass Number (A): Sum of protons and neutrons (A = Z + N).
• Isotopes: Same atomic number, different mass number.
• Isobars: Different atomic number, same mass number.
• Isotones: Atoms with the same number of neutrons.

Wave Nature of Electromagnetic Radiation: Planck’s Quantum Theory and Photoelectric Effect

• Electromagnetic waves defined by wavelength (λ), frequency (ν), and velocity (c)
• Relationship: c = λ × ν (Speed of light is constant)
• Planck's Theory: Energy is quantized in packets called 'quanta' or 'photons'
• Planck's Equation: E = hν (where h is Planck's constant)
• Photoelectric Effect: Light behaves as particles to eject electrons from metal

Part 6 of 12 · ~4 min

The Bohr Model: Postulates, Energy Levels, and Derivation of Radius and Energy Formulas

• Electrons move in fixed circular orbits called stationary states.
• Angular momentum is quantized: L = n times h divided by 2 pi.
• Energy is emitted or absorbed only during electronic transitions.
• Radius of orbit is proportional to the square of n (r ∝ n squared).
• Energy of electron E = negative thirteen point six times Z squared divided by n squared.

A 3D animation of a hydrogen atom showing an electron jumping from a high orbit to a low orbit, emitting a glowing photon of light.

Part 7 of 12 · ~4 min

Limitations of Bohr’s Model and the Dual Nature of Matter (de Broglie Hypothesis)

• Bohr's model only works for hydrogen-like species (one electron systems)
• Failure to explain multi-electron atoms and fine spectral structures
• Inability to explain Zeeman and Stark effects
• De Broglie Hypothesis: Matter exhibits both particle and wave properties
• De Broglie Equation: wavelength equals h divided by mass times velocity

A 3D transition showing a Bohr electron orbit dissolving into a shimmering, oscillating wave pattern around the nucleus.

Part 8 of 12 · ~4 min

Heisenberg’s Uncertainty Principle and the Transition to Quantum Mechanics

• Heisenberg's Principle: Position and momentum cannot be measured simultaneously with precision.
• Mathematical Formula: Delta x times Delta p is greater than or equal to h divided by 4 pi.
• Inverse Relationship: Decreasing uncertainty in position increases uncertainty in velocity.
• Measurement Effect: The act of observing a particle alters its state.
• Shift in Concept: Moving from definite 'orbits' to probabilistic 'orbitals'.

A 3D animation showing a particle being hit by a light photon, causing it to jitter unpredictably, transitioning from a solid circular path to a fuzzy, translucent electron cloud.

Quantum Mechanical Model: Orbitals, Quantum Numbers, and Shapes

• Orbital: Region of maximum probability of finding an electron
• Principal Quantum Number (n): Defines shell size and energy
• Azimuthal Quantum Number (l): Defines subshell shape (s, p, d, f)
• Magnetic Quantum Number (m_l): Defines orbital orientation in space
• Spin Quantum Number (s): Defines electron spin direction (+1/2 or -1/2)

Part 10 of 12 · ~5 min

Rules for Filling Electrons: Aufbau Principle, Pauli Exclusion Principle, and Hund’s Rule

• Aufbau Principle: Electrons fill orbitals in increasing order of energy (n + l rule).
• n + l Rule: Lower n + l value means lower energy; if equal, lower n wins.
• Pauli Exclusion Principle: No two electrons can have the same four quantum numbers.
• Maximum Capacity: An orbital holds max 2 electrons with opposite spins.
• Hund's Rule: Degenerate orbitals are singly occupied before electron pairing begins.
