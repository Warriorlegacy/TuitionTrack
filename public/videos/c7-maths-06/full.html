Introduction: Why Triangles Matter in Real Life and Architecture

• Triangles are the most stable and rigid geometric shapes.
• Structural rigidity prevents shapes from deforming under pressure.
• Architects use triangles in bridges, towers, and roof trusses.
• Triangles distribute weight and load effectively in engineering.
• Digital images and computer graphics are built using triangles.

Anatomy of a Triangle: Vertices, Sides, Angles, and Basic Definitions

• Vertices: The corner points where two sides meet.
• Sides: The three line segments forming the boundary.
• Angles: The space between two sides at a vertex.
• Interior vs. Exterior: Inside the shape vs. outside the shape.
• Non-collinear Points: Vertices must not lie on a single straight line.

Classification of Triangles: Based on Sides and Based on Angles

• Classification by Sides: Equilateral, Isosceles, and Scalene
• Equilateral: All 3 sides equal and all angles are 60 degrees
• Isosceles: At least 2 sides are equal
• Classification by Angles: Acute, Right, and Obtuse
• A triangle can have two names (e.g., Isosceles Right-angled triangle)

Medians of a Triangle: Concept, Construction, and Properties

• Median: A line segment from a vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side.
• Every triangle has exactly three medians.
• The point where all three medians intersect is called the Centroid.
• The Centroid is the geometric center or center of gravity of the triangle.
• The Centroid divides each median in the ratio of 2:1.

Perpendicular Bisectors and the Circumcentre of a Triangle

• Perpendicular Bisector: A line through the midpoint at a 90-degree angle.
• Property: Any point on the bisector is equidistant from the segment's endpoints.
• Circumcentre: The point where all three perpendicular bisectors meet.
• Circumcircle: A circle passing through all three vertices of a triangle.
• Location: Inside acute, on the hypotenuse of right, and outside obtuse triangles.

The Angle Sum Property: Concept, Proof, and Numerical Applications

• The sum of all interior angles in a triangle is always 180 degrees.
• Proof: Use a line parallel to the base through the opposite vertex.
• Alternate interior angles are key to the formal geometric proof.
• Formula: Angle A + Angle B + Angle C = 180 degrees.
• Use the property to find a missing angle when two are known.

Exterior Angle Property: Theorem, Derivation, and Problem Solving

• Exterior Angle: An angle formed by extending one side of a triangle.
• Theorem: Exterior angle = Sum of the two interior opposite angles.
• Derivation: Based on the Angle Sum Property and Linear Pair Property.
• Example 1: If opposite angles are 60 and 70, exterior angle is 130 degrees.
• Example 2: Use subtraction to find an interior angle if exterior is known.

Inequality Property 1: The Relationship Between Sides and Angles

• In a triangle, the side opposite to the largest angle is the longest side.
• The angle opposite to the shortest side is the smallest angle.
• Larger Angle ➔ Longer Opposite Side.
• Longer Side ➔ Larger Opposite Angle.
• This property applies to all types of triangles.

Inequality Property 2: Sum of Two Sides vs. Third Side (Triangle Inequality)

• Rule: Sum of any two sides > Third side
• Condition 1: a + b > c
• Condition 2: a + c > b
• Condition 3: b + c > a
• Shortcut: Smallest two sides sum > Longest side

Pythagoras Theorem: Concept, Derivation, and Right-Angled Triangles

• Pythagoras Theorem applies only to right-angled triangles.
• The hypotenuse is the longest side, opposite the 90-degree angle.
• Formula: a squared plus b squared equals c squared.
• The sum of the areas of squares on the legs equals the square on the hypotenuse.
• To find a leg, subtract the square of the known leg from the hypotenuse square.
