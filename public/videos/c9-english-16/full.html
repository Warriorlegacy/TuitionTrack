Poetic Structure: Understanding the Ballad Form, Rhyme Scheme, and Rhythm

• Ballad Form: A narrative poem designed to tell a story through song-like verses.
• Quatrains: The poem is organized into four-line stanzas.
• Rhyme Scheme: Follows an ABCB pattern where lines two and four rhyme.
• Rhythm and Meter: A steady, repetitive beat that aids oral storytelling.
• Purpose of Structure: Enhances memorability and creates a musical, engaging flow.

Literary Devices: Alliteration, Simile, Repetition, and Personification

• Alliteration: Repetition of consonant sounds at the start of nearby words.
• Simile: Comparing two unlike things using the words 'like' or 'as'.
• Repetition: Repeating words or phrases to emphasize a theme or rhythm.
• Personification: Giving human qualities to non-human objects or nature.
• Tip: Identify these devices to understand the poem's musicality and impact.
