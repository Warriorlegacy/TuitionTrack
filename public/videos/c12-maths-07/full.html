Integration by Substitution: Changing Variables and Simplifying Complex Expressions

• Substitution is the reverse process of the Chain Rule in differentiation.
• Identify a part of the function, u or t, whose derivative is also present.
• Step 1: Set u equals the inner function and find du in terms of dx.
• Step 2: Replace all x terms and dx with the new variable and du.
• Step 3: Integrate the simplified form and substitute back to the original variable.

Integration by Parts: The ILATE Rule and Derivation of the Product Rule

• Integration by Parts is the reverse process of the Product Rule of differentiation.
• Formula: Integral of v du = uv - Integral of u dv.
• Use the ILATE rule to choose the function 'u' for easier integration.
• I: Inverse Trig, L: Logarithmic, A: Algebraic, T: Trigonometric, E: Exponential.
• Priority: The function higher in the ILATE list should be chosen as 'u'.

Definite Integrals: Fundamental Theorem of Calculus and Properties of Definite Integrals

• Definite Integral: Represents a specific numerical value or net area.
• Fundamental Theorem: Integral of f(x) from a to b is F(b) minus F(a).
• Limit Reversal: Integral from a to b equals negative integral from b to a.
• Interval Splitting: Integral from a to b equals integral from a to c plus c to b.
• Linearity: Integrals distribute over addition and subtraction.

Advanced Properties of Definite Integrals: Symmetry, Periodicity, and King's Property

• Even Functions: Integral from -a to a is 2 times integral from 0 to a
• Odd Functions: Integral from -a to a is always zero
• Periodic Functions: Integral over n periods equals n times the period integral
• King's Property: Integral of f(x) from a to b equals f(a + b - x) from a to b
• Strategy: Apply King's Property and add the resulting integral to the original

Applications of Integrals: Finding Areas Under Simple Curves and Between Two Curves

• Area under a curve y=f(x) is the definite integral from a to b of f(x) dx
• If the curve is below the x-axis, use the absolute value of the function
• Area between two curves is the integral of (Upper Function - Lower Function)
• Step 1: Sketch curves; Step 2: Find intersection points; Step 3: Set limits
• Integration can be performed with respect to x or with respect to y
