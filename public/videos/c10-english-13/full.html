Poetic Devices I: Rhyme Scheme, Rhythm, and Meter Analysis

• Rhyme Scheme: The pattern of end-rhymes (e.g., A-B-A-B alternate rhyme).
• Rhythm: The musical 'beat' created by the arrangement of sounds.
• Meter: The formal measurement of stressed and unstressed syllables.
• Iambic Tetrameter: A pattern of four 'da-DUM' beats per line.
• Irony: The contrast between scary content and a bouncy, nursery-rhyme rhythm.

The Asian Lion and Bengal Tiger: Characterization through Imagery

• Imagery: Using descriptive language to create mental pictures and evoke emotions.
• The Asian Lion: Characterized by 'tawny' color and a terrifying, fatal roar.
• The Bengal Tiger: Characterized by 'noble' majesty and high-contrast black stripes.
• Visual vs. Auditory Imagery: Lion (sound/color) vs. Tiger (pattern/presence).
• Humorous Contrast: Using vivid, scary details to create an absurd, funny tone.

Part 4 of 12 · ~4 min

Poetic Devices II: Alliteration, Personification, and Hyperbole

• Alliteration: Repetition of initial consonant sounds (e.g., 'Roaming round')
• Personification: Giving human traits to animals (e.g., the Tiger 'greeting' you)
• Hyperbole: Deliberate exaggeration for humor (e.g., identifying animals by being eaten)
• How these devices combine to create a humorous and absurd tone
• Exam Tip: Look for repetition, human actions, and impossible scenarios

The Hyena and Crocodile: Distinguishing Traits and Irony

• Hyena's 'merry smile' is a deceptive physical trait used for irony.
• Crocodile's 'weeping' refers to the myth of fake 'crocodile tears'.
• Irony: Using emotions like joy and sorrow to mask predatory intent.
• Contrast: Hyena uses false happiness; Crocodile uses false sadness.
• Theme: The deceptive and dangerous nature of wild animals.

Vocabulary Workshop: Archaic Words and Contextual Meanings

• Archaic Words: Old-fashioned terms used to create a whimsical, storytelling tone.
• Discern vs. See: 'Discern' implies a deeper, more careful observation.
• Contextual Meaning: The meaning of a word changes based on the surrounding text.

Critical Thinking Activity: Comparing Tone and Mood

• Tone: The author's attitude toward the subject (the poet's voice).
• Mood: The atmosphere or emotional response created for the reader.
• Formula: Tone (Author's intent) + Context = Mood (Reader's feeling).
• Analysis: Playful Tone + Dangerous Subject = Humorous/Absurd Mood.
• Exam Tip: Use 'whimsical tone' and 'lighthearted mood' in your answers.

Common Mistakes: Misinterpreting Irony and Literary Devices

• Don't just say 'funny'; identify it as 'Dark Situational Irony'.
• Use the term 'Hyperbole' instead of just 'exaggeration'.
• Distinguish between 'Imagery' (visuals) and 'Personification' (human traits).
• Differentiate between 'Tone' (author's attitude) and 'Mood' (reader's feeling).
• Recognize 'Poetic License' when scientific facts are altered for art.
