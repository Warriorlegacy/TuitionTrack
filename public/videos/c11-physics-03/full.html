Vector Algebra I: Addition, Subtraction, and Graphical Methods

• Vectors cannot be added arithmetically; direction matters.
• Triangle Law: Head-to-tail method for two vectors.
• Parallelogram Law: Resultant is the diagonal of the parallelogram.
• Magnitude Formula: R = square root of (A^2 + B^2 + 2AB cos theta).
• Vector Subtraction: Adding the negative of a vector.

Vector Algebra II: Resolution of Vectors and Unit Vectors

• Resolution: Breaking a vector into perpendicular rectangular components.
• Horizontal component: A-x = A cos theta.
• Vertical component: A-y = A sin theta.
• Unit Vectors: Vectors with magnitude of one used to indicate direction.
• Vector Notation: A = A-x i-cap + A-y j-cap.

Motion in a Plane with Constant Acceleration: Position, Velocity, and Acceleration Vectors

• Position vector r = x i + y j defines location in a 2D plane
• Instantaneous velocity is the time derivative of position: v = dr/dt
• Velocity components: v-x = dx/dt and v-y = dy/dt
• Acceleration is the time derivative of velocity: a = dv/dt
• Constant acceleration allows component-wise kinematic equations

Relative Velocity in Two Dimensions: River-Boat and Rain-Man Problems

• Relative velocity is the vector difference: V-ab = V-a - V-b
• Rain-Man: Umbrella angle depends on the relative velocity vector
• River-Boat: Resultant velocity is the sum of swimmer and river velocities
• Shortest time to cross: Aim perpendicular to the river flow
• Zero drift: Aim upstream to cancel the river's horizontal velocity

Projectile Motion I: Derivations of Trajectory, Time of Flight, and Maximum Height

• Horizontal velocity (u cos theta) remains constant throughout the flight.
• Vertical motion is under constant acceleration due to gravity (g).
• Trajectory Equation: y = x tan(theta) - [g x^2 / (2 u^2 cos^2(theta))].
• The path of a projectile is a parabola.
• Time of Flight (T) = 2 u sin(theta) / g.

Projectile Motion II: Range, Velocity at any point, and Horizontal Projection

• Horizontal Range: R = (u^2 * sin(2θ)) / 2g
• Maximum Range occurs when the projection angle is 45 degrees
• Complementary angles (θ and 90-θ) yield the same horizontal range
• Instantaneous Velocity: V = square root of (v-x^2 + v-y^2)
• Horizontal Projection: Initial vertical velocity is zero

Uniform Circular Motion I: Angular Variables and Centripetal Acceleration Derivation

• Angular displacement (theta) = arc length (s) / radius (R)
• Angular velocity (omega) = d-theta / dt
• Relationship: Linear velocity (v) = omega * R
• Centripetal acceleration (a) = v^2 / R or omega^2 * R
• Centripetal acceleration always points toward the center

Uniform Circular Motion II: Speed, Period, and Frequency in Circular Paths

• Linear speed V = R * omega
• Period T = Time for one complete revolution
• Frequency f = 1 / T (measured in Hertz)
• Angular velocity omega = 2 * pi / T = 2 * pi * f
• Relationship: V = 2 * pi * R * f

Problem-Solving Workshop: Worked Examples and Common Conceptual Mistakes

• Always resolve initial velocity into horizontal and vertical components first.
• Common Error: Adding vector magnitudes like simple scalar numbers.
• Check if the launch angle is relative to the horizontal or vertical axis.
• Remember: Horizontal velocity in projectile motion is constant.
• In circular motion, constant speed does NOT mean zero acceleration.
