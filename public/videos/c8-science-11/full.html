The Motion of the Sun: Apparent Motion and the Concept of Day and Night

• Apparent Motion: The illusion of the Sun moving across the sky.
• The Sun stays central; Earth's rotation creates the illusion of movement.
• Day: The half of Earth facing the Sun.
• Night: The half of Earth turned away from the Sun.
• The Terminator Line: The moving boundary between day and night.

Earth's Rotation and Revolution: The Mechanics of Time and Seasons

• Rotation: Earth spinning on its axis from West to East every 24 hours.
• Axial Tilt: The Earth is tilted at an angle of 23.5 degrees.
• Revolution: Earth's elliptical orbit around the Sun taking 365.25 days.
• Seasons: Caused by the tilt of the axis and the Earth's revolution.
• Solstices and Equinoxes: Key points in the Earth's yearly journey.

Understanding Shadows: Sundials, Gnomons, and Measuring Solar Time

• Shadows change length and direction as the Sun's position shifts.
• The Gnomon is the vertical rod or blade that casts the shadow.
• A Sundial uses a marked scale to translate shadows into hours.
• Solar Noon occurs when the Sun is at its highest point and shadow is shortest.
• For accuracy, the gnomon must be aligned with the Earth's rotational axis.

The Concept of Longitude and the Origin of Standard Time

• Longitude: Vertical lines called meridians meeting at the poles.
• Prime Meridian: The zero-degree line passing through Greenwich, London.
• Mathematical Rule: Earth rotates fifteen degrees every one hour.
• Time Calculation: One degree of longitude equals four minutes of time.
• Standard Time: Using a central meridian to synchronize time across a region.

Time Zones and the International Date Line: Global Synchronization

• Earth is divided into 24 time zones based on 15 degrees of longitude per hour.
• Moving 15 degrees East adds one hour; moving 15 degrees West subtracts one hour.
• The International Date Line (IDL) is located near the 180-degree meridian.
• Crossing the IDL Westbound (toward Asia) means skipping forward one day.
• Crossing the IDL Eastbound (toward America) means repeating the same day.

The Moon's Phases: Lunar Cycles and the Concept of a Month

• Lunar phases are caused by the Moon's position relative to Earth and Sun
• Waxing means the visible illuminated portion is increasing
• Waning means the visible illuminated portion is decreasing
• A full lunar cycle takes approximately 29.5 days
• The Moon reflects sunlight rather than producing its own light

Eclipses Explained: Solar and Lunar Mechanics and Geometry

• Umbra: The darkest, central part of a shadow.
• Penumbra: The outer, partial shadow region.
• Solar Eclipse: Moon between Earth and Sun (New Moon).
• Lunar Eclipse: Earth between Sun and Moon (Full Moon).
• Orbital Tilt: The 5-degree tilt prevents monthly eclipses.
