Introduction: Why Triangles Matter, Congruence Concept & Axioms

• Triangles are the most stable and rigid geometric shapes.
• Congruence means figures have the same shape and the same size.
• Order of vertices matters: Triangle ABC ≅ Triangle PQR.
• C-P-C-T: Corresponding Parts of Congruent Triangles are equal.
• Basic Axioms: S-S-S (Side-Side-Side) and S-A-S (Side-Angle-Side).

Criteria for Congruence I: SAS and ASA Congruence Rules

• Congruence shortcuts save time by using minimal measurements.
• S-A-S Rule: Two sides and the included angle must be equal.
• The 'Included Angle' is the angle located between two specific sides.
• A-S-A Rule: Two angles and the included side must be equal.
• The 'Included Side' is the segment connecting two specific angles.

Part 3 of 12 · ~5 min

Criteria for Congruence II: SSS and RHS Congruence Rules

• SSS Rule: Three sides of one triangle equal to three sides of another.
• RHS Rule: Right angle, Hypotenuse, and one Side must be equal.
• SSS works for any triangle; RHS is exclusive to right-angled triangles.
• Hypotenuse is the longest side opposite the ninety-degree angle.
• After proving congruence, use CPCT to find remaining equal parts.

Properties of Isosceles Triangles: Theorems and Proofs

• Definition: Isosceles triangle has at least two equal sides.
• Theorem 1: Angles opposite to equal sides are equal.
• Proof Method: Use SAS congruence and CPCT.
• Theorem 2: Sides opposite to equal angles are equal.
• Proof Method: Use ASA congruence and CPCT.

Common Pitfalls: Misidentifying Congruence & Logical Errors

• The SSA Trap: The angle must be included between the sides (SAS).
• AAA is for Similarity, not Congruence: Size matters!
• RHS is specific: Only use it for right-angled triangles.
• Avoid Circular Reasoning: Don't assume what you are trying to prove.
• Don't trust your eyes: Never assume properties not explicitly given.
