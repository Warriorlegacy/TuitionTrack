The Composition of Parliament: Rajya Sabha vs. Lok Sabha

• Bicameralism: Parliament consists of two houses: Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha.
• Lok Sabha: The House of the People, elected directly by citizens.
• Rajya Sabha: The Council of States, representing the interests of States.
• Lok Sabha Term: 5 years (can be dissolved); Rajya Sabha: Permanent body.
• Composition: Lok Sabha (Direct Election) vs. Rajya Sabha (Indirect Election).

Part 5 of 12 · ~4 min

How Laws are Formed: The Journey of a Bill

• A Bill is a formal proposal for a new law or an amendment to an existing one.
• First Reading: Introduction of the Bill and its objectives in the House.
• Second Reading: Detailed clause-by-clause debate and committee scrutiny.
• Third Reading: Final vote to accept or reject the Bill as a whole.
• President's Assent: The final step that transforms a Bill into an Act.

Parliamentary Oversight: Question Hour and Controlling the Executive

• Parliamentary Oversight: Ensuring the Executive remains accountable to the people.
• Question Hour: The first hour of a sitting used to question Ministers.
• Starred Questions: Require oral answers and allow for follow-up 'supplementary' questions.
• Unstarred Questions: Require written answers; used for detailed data collection.
• No-Confidence Motion: The ultimate tool to remove a government from power.

Common Mistakes in Answering Civics Questions and Terminology Pitfalls

• Don't confuse Legislature (law-makers) with Executive (law-implementers).
• A 'Bill' is a proposal; it only becomes an 'Act' after Presidential assent.
• The Rajya Sabha is a permanent body, but its members serve 6-year terms.
• Democracy is more than just voting; it includes accountability and rule of law.
• Question Hour is for oversight and scrutiny, not for passing new laws.
