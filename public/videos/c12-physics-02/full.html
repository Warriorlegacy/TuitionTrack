Electrostatic Potential and Potential Difference: Definitions and Derivations

• Electrostatic Potential (V) = Work Done (W) / Test Charge (q)
• Unit of Potential: Volt (V) = Joule per Coulomb (J/C)
• Potential Difference (V_B - V_A) = Work done to move charge from A to B
• Potential due to a point charge: V = kQ / r
• Potential is a scalar quantity; use algebraic sum for multiple charges

Potential due to a Point Charge and System of Charges

• Potential of a point charge: V = kQ / r
• Potential is a scalar quantity, not a vector
• Sign of the charge determines the sign of the potential
• Superposition Principle: V_total = V1 + V2 + V3...
• Use algebraic sum (include positive and negative signs)

Part 4 of 12 · ~5 min

Equipotential Surfaces: Properties, Diagrams, and Visualizations

• Equipotential Surface: A surface where potential (V) is constant everywhere.
• Work Done: W = q times delta V. Since delta V is zero, work done is zero.
• Field Orientation: Electric field lines are always perpendicular to the surface.
• No Intersection: Two equipotential surfaces can never cross each other.
• Field Strength: Closely spaced surfaces indicate a stronger electric field.

Part 5 of 12 · ~5 min

Electrostatic Potential Energy of a System of Charges

• Potential energy is the work done to assemble a charge configuration from infinity.
• For two charges, U equals k times q1 times q2 divided by r.
• Total energy of a system is the sum of potential energies of all unique pairs.
• Positive energy indicates repulsive forces; negative energy indicates attractive forces.
• Electrostatic potential energy is a scalar quantity, not a vector.

Relation between Electric Field and Potential: Mathematical Derivation

• Electric field is the negative gradient of electrostatic potential
• Relationship: E = -grad V or E = -dV/dx in one dimension
• The negative sign indicates E points toward decreasing potential
• Electric field lines are always perpendicular to equipotential surfaces
• Potential is a scalar, while Electric Field is a vector quantity

Dipole in an External Electric Field: Potential and Torque

• Potential of a dipole varies as 1/r squared, unlike point charges (1/r)
• Potential on the equatorial plane of a dipole is always zero
• A dipole in a uniform field experiences zero net force but non-zero torque
• Torque magnitude: Tau equals p times E times sine theta
• Stable equilibrium occurs when dipole is parallel to the electric field

Introduction to Capacitance: Conductors and Dielectrics

• Capacitance (C) is the ratio of charge (Q) to potential difference (V): C = Q/V
• The SI unit of capacitance is the Farad (F)
• Capacitance depends on conductor geometry and the surrounding medium
• Dielectrics are insulators that undergo polarization in an electric field
• Inserting a dielectric reduces the electric field and increases capacitance

Parallel Plate Capacitor: Derivation and Effect of Dielectrics

• Parallel Plate Capacitor: Two conducting plates separated by distance 'd'
• Electric Field (E) = sigma / epsilon naught
• Capacitance (C) = epsilon naught * A / d
• Dielectric effect: Reduces net electric field and potential difference
• New Capacitance (C') = K * C

Capacitors in Series and Parallel: Formulas and Network Analysis

• Series Connection: Charge Q is constant; Voltage V is additive.
• Series Formula: 1/C_eq = 1/C1 + 1/C2 + 1/C3
• Parallel Connection: Voltage V is constant; Charge Q is additive.
• Parallel Formula: C_eq = C1 + C2 + C3
• Network Analysis: Simplify circuits step-by-step using reduction.

Energy Stored in a Capacitor and Dielectric Polarization

• Work done by battery is stored as electrostatic potential energy
• Formula for energy stored: U = one-half C V squared
• Dielectric polarization: induced dipoles create an opposing field
• Net electric field E = E-zero divided by dielectric constant K
• Capacitance increases by factor K when dielectric is inserted

Exam Masterclass: Problem-Solving Techniques, Common Mistakes, and Full Recap

• Identify if Charge (Q) or Potential (V) is constant in capacitor networks.
• Always include the algebraic sign of charges when calculating total potential.
• Dielectric inserted with battery connected? V is constant. Battery removed? Q is constant.
• Electric field points from high potential to low potential (negative gradient).
• Treat dielectric slabs in a gap as capacitors in series for accurate calculation.
